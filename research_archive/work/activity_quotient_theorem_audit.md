## 1. Completeness and equality of totals

The componentwise limit/Fatou argument proves completeness of the anchored activity norm, including norm convergence of a Cauchy sequence. Each compact-field evaluation is bounded by twice the number of support cells times the activity norm. The equality kernel is therefore closed. The quotient norm does not require a norm-minimizing representative.

The invariant preimage is closed and contains the equality kernel. Hence its quotient is a closed subspace with the same quotient norm. Invariance is imposed on normalized totals, not on individual carrier functions. Carrierwise constants are removed; a physical vacuum sector is consequently not supplied by this quotient.

Directional Cauchy on a segment in an interior polydisc gives the stated cell-l1 bound without a scalar-coordinate dimension factor. The carrier maximum is bounded by the sum of cell maxima, after which the anchored row sum applies. Compact truncation then extends equality of normalized totals to all l1 fields in a common strict interior ball. This is a substantive ingredient in the classical descent, not a formal identification of germs at arbitrary noncompact fields.

## 2. Kernels and extraction

For a carrier with m scalar coordinates, the sum of ordered degree-n derivatives is bounded by n! times the number of degree-n scalar multiindices, and hence by (m+n)^n times the Cauchy coefficient bound. Connectedness bounds slot displacements by a fixed multiple of J times carrier size. At each fixed n at most four, the cardinality reserve pays both this polynomial and a positive exponential spatial weight. Constants retain their J and coordinate-count dependence.

Every total kernel component is a finite-coordinate derivative of the normalized total and therefore descends to the quotient. The Maxwell coefficient is a continuous linear functional on the invariant quotient under the already established exact infinite total Ward classification. Its b^{-2} bound, including the displayed cubic polynomial in carrier size for a second spatial moment, is correct.

The fixed-rho reference construction is sufficient for the uniform extraction norm. Coefficientwise homogeneous projection to degrees at least two changes neither the normalized total nor its class, because its total linear sector is zero. Summing the geometric bound gives 2 M_S b^2/rho^2 for b at most rho/2. This does not require individual linear coefficients to vanish and does not impose coefficientwise gauge invariance. Combining this bound with the b^{-2} Maxwell-functional bound gives a reference projection whose norm is independent of the subsequently reduced b.

## 3. Classical descent and its additional invariant-domain requirement

For compact final data, a nonzero supported background coefficient has a variable support meeting the input support. Its rooted tree then gives exponential decay of the output root. At a fixed endpoint ratio L, the output fiber has finitely many current coordinates, so the background is l1. Equality of input totals therefore implies equality of their scalar classical substitutions.

A connected representation estimate is separately needed to obtain a bounded output quotient map. The fragment correctly makes this an input rather than inferring it from amplitude contraction. Once such representations exist, their assignments need not be canonical: equality of normalized totals identifies their output classes, and near-minimal input representatives yield the quotient bound.

The final qualification concerning invariant descent is essential and is correctly preserved. A possibly noncompact connecting gauge is not automatically covered by compact-gauge invariance. Sufficient conditions are compact-gauge approximation with transformed fields converging in l1 within a common strict margin, or a joint finite invariant approximation of the particular interaction and backgrounds. The latter proves the identity for that input; it does not by itself produce an action on every invariant quotient class. A universal operator statement therefore retains the corresponding every-class hypothesis.

## 4. Conditional contraction

After full-reference extraction, the exact total Ward identities supply the low-order moment cancellations. They hold for the total kernels of any representative of the extracted class. Coefficientwise phase projections have norm at most one; the fifth-order remainder has norm at most six. These projections descend to the unrestricted quotient, although they need not preserve the invariant subspace individually.

The low-kernel spatial theorem is applied before assigning new carriers. Its kernel constants are fixed after J and before L. The high-order transport acts directly on the coefficientwise fifth-order remainder. The zero total linear part stays zero after substitution by l1 continuity, even if its carrierwise coefficients are nonzero. Adding the two output representations, then taking the representative infimum, gives the displayed classical quotient estimate. The explicit output reference projection is needed; the proof makes no claim that substitution itself preserves zero Maxwell coefficient.

The parameter order is correct as a conditional theorem: fix J and all support reserves; choose a finite allowed endpoint power L=R^q; then choose b within the high-order analytic continuation and classical margins. The extraction constants do not deteriorate when b shrinks. The separate hypotheses that the high-order bound is uniform in L and that the coherent classical maps and identities are chosen jointly remain visible. The theorem does not identify sequential quantum cutoffs with a one-shot construction.

## 5. Fragment fidelity and boundaries

The aqb fragment faithfully transcribes these statements and their proofs, including the fixed-rho realization and the new invariant-domain qualification. Its displayed contraction is an operator estimate only under the every-class invariant-domain hypothesis; the text also permits a pointwise estimate on a separately justified collection of inputs.

The final boundary is appropriately explicit. Equality of infinite vacuum-normalized totals on compact fields is not yet a theorem identifying finite-regulator exponentials, sharp restricted integrals, their normalizations, or their limits. No quantum map on this quotient is constructed, and the quotient cannot yet license an arbitrary change of representative inside the next finite quantum integration. Amplitude closure and the coupling trajectory are also not proved here.
