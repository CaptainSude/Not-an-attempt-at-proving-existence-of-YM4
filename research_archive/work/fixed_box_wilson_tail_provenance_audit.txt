INDEPENDENT AUDIT: FIXED-BOX CONDITIONAL WILSON TAIL
17 October 2026. Audited work/fixed_box_wilson_tail_fragment.tex
in full. PASS in its explicitly fixed-geometry scope. No requested
mathematical correction.

1. Forest and nonidentity boundary.
The construction is valid: extend a tree of the connected boundary
graph to a tree of the complete graph, then remove the boundary-tree
edges. Every remaining component has exactly one boundary vertex.
An added edge between already connected boundary vertices could not
belong to the extended tree, so all retained forest edges belong to
the integrated edge set. Gauge transformations fixed at every boundary
vertex uniquely remove these forest edges. This uses no condition
that the fixed boundary values equal I.

The coordinate transformation is exactly triangular Haar:
forest links <-> interior gauge values, followed by independent
left/right translations of nonforest links. Normalized compact Haar
integrates the gauge variables to1. This has neither a missing orbit
Jacobian nor an untracked gauge-volume factor.

2. Zero set and Hessian.
For SU(N), h(U)=0 iff U=I. Flatness on the full cubical two-skeleton
of the box gives a pure gauge, and identity boundary edges make its
vertex gauge constant on the connected boundary. Normalize that
constant away. The unique forest representative is then I.

The linear nullspace check is also exact: da=0 on a cubical box
implies a=dphi. Vanishing boundary edges makes phi constant on the
boundary; vanishing forest edges propagates its chosen zero value
to every vertex. Thus the restricted Hessian is positive definite.
Its smallest eigenvalue may depend on R; the statement retains this.

3. Unique global minimizer for perturbed boundary.
The IFT gives the local critical branch. Strict convexity holds
throughout one fixed coordinate ball after shrinking the boundary
neighborhood. The compact complement of a smaller ball has positive
action at V=I. Uniform continuity and S_V(y*(V))->0 preserve a
positive relative gap there. Combining the convex ball and complement
therefore proves global, not merely local, uniqueness for this fixed
box and small boundary. The nested chart/ball choices in the fragment
are compatible; no growing-box claim follows from them.

4. Normalized probability and g powers.
The lower denominator c g^n exp(-Smin/g^2) is uniform in the chosen
boundary neighborhood. The local numerator has the SAME g^n, because
it is integrated in the same n-dimensional chart and centered at the
same exact minimizer. Its finite-dimensional Gaussian polynomial
factor can be absorbed into a smaller exponential constant.
The compact remainder gives C g^-n exp(-eta/g^2); splitting off
exp(-eta/(2g^2)) controls the polynomial, and T<=r/g converts the
remaining factor to exp(-cT^2). Every comparison sign and exponent
is correct. The entire sup-coordinate window lies in one injective
chart after r is reduced by the fixed finite-dimensional norm
equivalence. Constants need not be dimension-uniform.

5. Exact bare conditional interpretation.
A positive edge along direction mu not contained in the topological
boundary has every transverse coordinate in {1,...,R-1}. Each
plaquette containing it changes one such coordinate by +/-1 and
is therefore contained in the box. Consequently no exterior
plaquette depends on an integrated edge. All differences between
the full conditional action and S_V are fixed additive constants.
This proves the stated actual conditional bare-Wilson interpretation,
provided no local periodic identification is imposed.

6. Scope.
The proof assumes a fixed finite box and boundary data in a fixed
small neighborhood after a consistently applied boundary gauge.
It proves an unconstrained forest-coordinate event. It does not
control an arbitrary exterior field, a nonlinear coarse-average
fiber, a refined/growing physical box, or a mixed effective action.
The normalization on a coarse constraint fiber is a separate
problem; conditioning cannot be inferred from the unconditional
tail. The fragment states all these limitations.
