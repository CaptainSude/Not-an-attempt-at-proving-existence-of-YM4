2. A useful rigorous theorem, stronger than a generic universality claim

Adams--Lee, "Structure of logarithmically divergent one-loop lattice
Feynman integrals", Phys.Rev.D77 (2008) 045010:
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/0709.0781
  https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.77.045010
Primary locations: §II, pp5--7, (7)--(14); §III (21)--(24);
§IV (29)--(31); Appendix A, p19, theorem (A1).

Their fixed-integrand hypotheses use
  I(p,a)= integral_BZ(a) V(k,p,a)/C(k,p,a) d^4k,
  V=a^-m F(ak,ap), C=a^-n G(ak,ap),
with smooth F,G, finite polynomial continuum limits, positive
denominator, elliptic leading continuum denominator, no additional
zero of G(k,0), infrared finiteness, and lattice divergence degree zero.
The result is
  I=f(p)log(a mu)+g(p)+h(p,mu)+o(1),
with universal f and a finite remainder. Their convergent-integral
theorem gives |I-I_cont| <= c(p)a log(1/a). A positive auxiliary mass
regularizes the Taylor subtraction in (21). The constants are for
the specified integrand; no uniform family parameter is asserted.

This theorem can certify a standard reference calculation once its
integrand and normalization are identified. It does not already
identify our constrained determinant.

5. Precise obstruction to direct application of the one-loop theorem

These are conclusions from our actual formulas, not attributed to the
papers above.

(a) The new pair is periodic at final-block scale. Its Bloch fiber has
O(K^4) fine coordinates. Sending a=K^-1 to zero changes the fiber,
projection and background vertices; it does not give a single fixed
F,G in the theorem merely by writing a Bloch determinant.

(b) H_res contains the effective S_M Hessian and constraint curvature.
The existing uniform flat q_M bounds and fixed-K source estimates do
not identify its magnetic vertices with the Wilson background-Feynman
vertices uniformly in M,K.

(c) The theorem's pointwise integral asymptotic does not license
differentation at zero external momentum without an infrared-safe
Taylor-subtraction representation or a uniform analytic neighborhood.
The actual target is already a zero-momentum second spatial moment.

(d) Pure background gauge covariance does not bound every separate
relative vertex. The independent audit of
mean_zero_gauge_vertex_obstruction.txt confirms an actual compact
pure-gauge orbit for which the scalar first relative vertex grows
at least sqrt(K), while every total heat trace is constant.
Cancellation must be kept before an absolute estimate.

6. Concrete route now available

First derive an EXACT scalar/vector/coarse Schur identity, preserving
all current and residual terms, against a fully specified massive
Wilson reference. The theorem agent is working on this algebra.
Then prove the coarse correction's combined Maxwell moment is bounded
uniformly in K and old M. A fixed number of coarse degrees of freedom
per final block is helpful, but neither finite rank nor a flat gap
alone proves that bound: their background vertices must be controlled.

For the remaining translation-invariant Wilson reference, combine
diagrams or trace terms before estimating. If the combined Taylor
integrals satisfy the fixed-integrand assumptions in §2, that theorem
supplies the logarithmic coefficient and finite remainder. A separate
normalization calculation must match the Hermitian color trace and
the actual beta functional. No number is imported here.

An alternative sufficient target can be stated entirely in terms of
the actual coupled heat coefficient, so it avoids separate raw-vertex
bounds. Write the established identity as
  beta_(M,K)= integral_0^infinity h_(M,K)(s) ds/s.
Suppose there are b_*,delta>0 and C independent of M,K such that
  integral_0^1 |h_(M,K)(s)| ds/s <= C,
  |h_(M,K)(s)-b_*/2|
      <= C[s^-delta+(s/K^2)^delta],       1<=s<=K^2,
  integral_(K^2)^infinity |h_(M,K)(s)| ds/s <= C.
Then direct integration proves
  |beta_(M,K)-b_* log K| <= 2C+2C/delta.
This is a proved conditional reduction, not an estimate already
available for the actual pair. It identifies the missing intermediate
time comparison exactly. Uniform heat bounds alone concern the flat
trace and do not establish these differentiated inequalities.

The desired error need only be integrable over logarithmic time. A
scale-derivative version with an integrable defect also suffices; a
mere limit of the derivative to b_* would allow a log-log divergence
and is weaker than the bounded finite part needed here.
