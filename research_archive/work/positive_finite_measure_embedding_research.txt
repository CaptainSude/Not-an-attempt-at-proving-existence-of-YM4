Exact positive finite-measure embedding of the retained sector
17 October 2026. Research proof; no output or master edits.

1. Purpose, fixed choices and scope

This note supplies an exact finite positive-g bridge beyond the existing
homogeneous local integral. It does not estimate the complementary
histories. In particular, it does not identify the complete Wilson
measure with the homogeneous analytic density of Notebook37.

Fix a finite compatible periodic lattice, a compact connected matrix
group G, normalized product link Haar m_E, and the primitive ordinary
mean specified in composite_macrostep_geometry_research.md. Fix ell and
R=ell^s. The global map F is the composition of the measurable primitive
completions in Section2 below, not a fictitious globally analytic map.
This choice is an exact coarse observable of the original finite Wilson
measure. It is not identified with an unspecified different large-field
completion in the deposited account.

The local chart, physical centered branch V_*(W), one-pivot incidence,
and contour variables are precisely those in
composite_macrostep_integral_research.md. They agree with the actual
ordinary composite map in its local domain. Preceding classical depth M
enters the chosen centered branch and local action, but not the elementary
measure identities below. No limit or asymptotic stationary-phase formula
is used in those identities.

New conclusions:
(a) a pointwise normalized compact-gauge Markov kernel gives an exact
positive retained/complement split even for an arbitrary finite input
measure;
(b) an explicit normalized output-orbit average turns the locally computed
retained density into a globally gauge-invariant positive submeasure of
the true pushforward;
(c) this yields positive linear good/remainder operators on invariant
finite measures, hence an exact decomposition of the full finite Wilson
pushforward into all binary histories;
(d) on each fixed finite torus, after an explicit possibly volume-dependent
shrink, the good submeasure has exactly the previously computed local
analytic germ, including all vacuum factors.
The last conclusion requires literal input-chart containment at positive
g. Holomorphic continuation alone is not a substitute for that condition.

2. A real measurable global map, not a global analytic action

For a path tuple (U_1,...,U_n) from y to x let T=U_1. Choose a sufficiently
small conjugation-invariant identity neighborhood O on which the actual
selected local mean Phi of the normalized tuple is defined. On the good
set U_j T^{-1} in O for every j, put

 P(U_1,...,U_n)=Phi(U_1 T^{-1},...,U_n T^{-1}) T.

On its complement put P=T. Use the same ordering, multiplicities and
reference as the selected local definition; no alternative group mean
is substituted. The good set is Borel and endpoint-invariant. The local
mean's conjugation covariance gives, globally,

 P(g U_1 h^{-1},...,g U_n h^{-1})=g P(U_1,...,U_n) h^{-1}.

Right covariance shows agreement with the original mean wherever its
original small-field formula is used. This constructs the internal
contour P on all real group configurations. It need not be continuous
across the selected good-set boundary and is not asserted holomorphic
there.

For each primitive ordinary coarse bond use these completed internal
contours and the original reference center string T_c. If all transported
relative strings U_x T_c^{-1} lie in the prescribed small logarithm
neighborhood, use the original formula

 exp[ell^{-d} sum_x log(U_x T_c^{-1})] T_c;

otherwise use T_c. This is again Borel, globally gauge covariant, and
agrees with the analytic ordinary map near the identity. Composing the
primitive completions defines F=M_ell^s. Complete the independently
chosen direct-R internal P_R in the same manner. Their relations are

 F(U^v)=F(U)^{r(v)},
 P_R(U^v;y,x)=v(y)P_R(U;y,x)v(x)^{-1},                 (2.1)

where r restricts a current-site gauge to final block centers. No
identity between F and the direct-R ordinary average is used.

The original Wilson measure is still the original product-Haar measure
with its globally defined Wilson plaquette density. Only its coarse
observable has now been fixed everywhere. Thus identities below concern
that specified pushforward and do not replace the fine measure.

3. Exact compact-gauge randomization and positive sectors

Let H_R be the current-site gauge group equal to I at every final block
center, with product normalized Haar. Put n_s=number of other current
sites, q(h)=1-Re tau(h),

 G_R(U)=sum_(y,x!=y) q(P_R(U;y,x)),
 c(g)=integral_G exp[-q(h)/g^2] dh,                   (3.1)

for any positive real g. This is the exact compact integral, not a
Gaussian normalization or its small-g approximation. Equation(2.1),
independence of the site Haar variables and Haar invariance give

 integral_(H_R) exp[-G_R(U^u)/g^2] du=c(g)^{n_s}      (3.2)

for EVERY U, including fields outside all analytic charts. For based u,
each contour equals P_R(U;y,x)u(x)^{-1}; this proves (3.2) directly.

Define the orbit Markov kernel

 K_g(U,dV)=c(g)^{-n_s} integral_(H_R)
                exp[-G_R(U^u)/g^2] delta_(U^u)(dV)du. (3.3)

It has total mass one and is supported on F(V)=F(U). Let chi(V,W) be any
Borel function in [0,1], including the exact indicator of a selected
retained chart/window, with every extra pivot/contour/field condition
left inside chi. Then

 p_chi(U)=integral K_g(U,dV) chi(V,F(U)) in [0,1],
 F_*mu=F_*(p_chi mu)+F_*((1-p_chi)mu)                 (3.4)

for every finite positive input measure mu. This is a pointwise
acceptance/rejection identity, not an asymptotic sector approximation.
F_* denotes measure pushforward and does not require a global density.

If mu is H_R invariant, change variables U to U^u under the Haar gauge
action to obtain

 K_g^*mu(dV)=c(g)^{-n_s} exp[-G_R(V)/g^2] mu(dV).      (3.5)

Consequently the accepted measure in (3.4) is exactly the familiar
gauged retained-sector integral. For noninvariant mu the correct input
in this formula is its H_R average, not the original density. Silently
reusing (3.5) on a noninvariant history is an error.

The sum in (3.4) always retains the complete pushforward and its total
mass. Ordinary Gaussian tail bounds alone give no bound on 1-p_chi for
the interacting Wilson input.

4. Exact local retained density with all factors

Let mu be a finite measure invariant under the FULL current-site gauge
group. Write its Haar Lebesgue decomposition

 mu=rho m_E+mu_sing.

Both parts are invariant, by uniqueness of that decomposition and Haar
invariance. Only rho m_E is used in the local coarea operator below;
the remaining positive singular part is kept in the remainder. This
permits the operator to act on arbitrary intermediate positive invariant
measures, even if an arbitrary global completion has produced singular
pushforwards.

Take a small real coarse domain D and the actual physical V=V_*(W),
W in D. Let Phi_V(z) be the exact relative constraint chart with retained
coordinates z unchanged. Choose a fixed real retained window B_z on
which the chart is injective, and, if needed, an additional measurable
factor chi_extra in [0,1]. The sector is only this specified local sheet;
other sheets are in its complement. There are no silently omitted
conditions from a different source window.

Let q_col=dim g, n_e and n_c be the numbers of positive current and coarse
bonds, and n_z=q_col(n_e-n_c). In orthonormal real Lie coordinates,

 d(exp(iX))=h_0 j(X)dX, j(0)=1.

The target group delta gives h_0^{-1} per coarse bond at its identity.
Let A_(c,p) be the actual pivot block of the linear constraint, and

 D_R(V)=product_c det(A_(c,p))^{-1}.

All these real small-chart determinants have the fixed positive
orientation. Let j_c^str(V;K_Vz) be the logarithm of
 det A_(c,p)/det D_pF_V(Phi_V(z);c), zero at z=0.
The local density, relative to coarse product Haar, is exactly

 ell_mu(W)=c(g)^{-n_s}h_0^{n_e-n_c}D_R(V)
  integral_(B_z) dz rho(exp(iPhi_V(z))V)
   exp[-G_R(exp(iPhi_V(z))V)/g^2]
   product_b j(Phi_V(z)(b))
   exp[sum_c j_c^str(V;K_Vz)] chi_extra.              (4.1)

For a merely measurable rho this holds almost everywhere, by the local
joint change of variables and Tonelli. This is a nonnegative measurable
function, possibly modified on a null set. One can use monotone bounded
approximations if needed. Formula(4.1) is the usual actual square ambient
straightening followed by pivot elimination; det D Phi is NOT substituted
for the constraint Jacobian. The unrearranged factor is the product of
inverse nonlinear pivot derivatives. Equation(3.5) and the local coarea
identity give the measure inequality

 nu:=F_*mu >= 1_D ell_mu m_C.                        (4.2)

No smoothness or absolute continuity of nu outside D is asserted.

When the literal input chart has
 rho(U)=Z_in exp[-S_M(U)/g^2+E_raw(U)],
put z=g xi, retain its exact product window, and let A_M(V) be the actual
constrained action-plus-gauge Hessian. With the exact remainder and
measure exponent I of composite_macrostep_integral_research.md, (4.1)
becomes

 ell_mu(W)=Z_in c(g)^{-n_s}h_0^{n_e-n_c}D_R(V)
  g^{n_z}(2pi)^{n_z/2}det A_M(V)^{-1/2}
  exp[-S_M(V)/g^2+E_raw(V)]
  E_(A_M(V)^{-1})[chi_window chi_extra exp I].        (4.3)

In particular I includes -g^{-2}R_3, every straightening logarithm,
every Haar logarithm, and E_raw at the exact reconstructed field minus
E_raw(V). The argument is Phi_V(g xi), not g Phi_V(xi). Additional
indicators are evaluated at that exact field. Neither a background-
dependent determinant nor a field-dependent orbit factor is absorbed
into a vacuum constant.

Equation(4.3) is a literal positive-real integral if the input density
representation holds at EVERY reconstructed field in its retained
window. A holomorphic continuation having the same germ elsewhere
does not supply that hypothesis.

5. Globally invariant positive extension of the local sector

The raw output 1_D ell_mu need not be invariant because D is a potential
ball. Simply averaging it loses its local normalization. The following
NORMALIZED orbit construction repairs this exactly, at the measure
level.

Let G_C be the full coarse-site gauge group, with normalized Haar dv.
Choose once a nonnegative continuous theta supported in D, positive on
a smaller neighborhood of I. Put

 m_theta(W)=integral_(G_C) theta(W^v)dv.

It is invariant, nonnegative, and positive precisely on the orbit
saturation of {theta>0}. Define, with value zero when m_theta=0,

 (A_g mu)(dW)=a_mu(W)m_C(dW),
 a_mu(W)= [integral theta(W^v)ell_mu(W^v)dv]/m_theta(W).
                                                               (5.1)

Only the REAL locally computed ell_mu is evaluated, and only where theta
is supported. This constructs a real measurable global function; it
is not a global analytic extension of the action or of E. It is linear
in mu and invariant under every coarse-site gauge transformation.

THEOREM 5.1. On the cone of full-gauge-invariant finite positive input
measures,

 0 <= A_g mu <= F_*mu,
 R_g mu:=F_*mu-A_g mu >=0.                            (5.2)

Both operators are linear and preserve gauge invariance. Moreover
 A_g+R_g=F_* exactly, including raw mass.

Proof. The full pushforward nu is invariant by(2.1). Its Haar-ac part
has an invariant nonnegative density f, chosen by compact gauge
averaging if necessary. From(4.2), f>=ell_mu almost everywhere on D.
For almost every W and almost every v, f(W)=f(W^v) and
 theta(W^v)ell_mu(W^v)<=theta(W^v)f(W).
Integrating gives a_mu(W)<=f(W) on m_theta>0. The singular part of nu is
positive and remains in R_g. Tonelli, or first truncating the positive
factor m_theta^{-1}, justifies this even near the boundary where
m_theta tends to zero. Linearity follows from the linear Haar-ac
projection, the linear fiber integral(4.1), and the fixed theta. The
remainder is the difference of two linear operators and is positive
by the inequality just proved. QED.

It would be incorrect to derive (5.2) by simply averaging the measure
inequality and then dividing it by m_theta. The proof specifically uses
the invariant Haar-ac density f, which bounds each orbit value before
the normalized averaging. No positive lower bound for m_theta, uniform
or otherwise, is needed for (5.2).

The remainder in (5.2) is an explicit operator difference. It need not
equal the literal rejection in(3.4), since(5.1) redistributes the local
sector along output gauge orbits. Both decompositions are exact and
positive; they should not be identified termwise.

6. When the invariant extension has exactly the local analytic germ

A purely measure-theoretic extension need not preserve a specified germ.
Here is a sufficient finite-regulator lemma which proves that property
for the actual local density, while exposing its nonuniform radius.

LEMMA 6.1 (finite orbit-overlap control). Fix a connected finite coarse
lattice and an analytic local density ell on a product potential ball
of radius r. Suppose its total infinitesimal gauge Ward identity holds
there and it is invariant under constant color conjugations. There is
epsilon>0, possibly depending on the finite graph, such that whenever
W and W^v both lie in the epsilon ball,

 ell(W^v)=ell(W).                                    (6.1)

Proof. Work first in a unitarily invariant matrix norm; pay the fixed
color-coordinate equivalence constants in r. Choose a spanning tree
with maximal root-path length D_tree and root o. Write v_x=h_x c with
c=v_o, h_o=I. Constant conjugation sends W to W^c without enlarging its
invariant norm. Along a tree edge xy,

 h_y=(W^v_xy)^{-1} h_x W^c_xy.

Unitary norms and a telescoping product give
 ||h_x-I||<=C D_tree epsilon.
Choose epsilon so this is within one fixed small logarithm chart.
Then h_x=exp(i lambda_x), with ||lambda_x||<=C D_tree epsilon.
The path h_x(t)=exp(i t lambda_x) connects I to h, and

 ||log[(W^c)^{h(t)}]/i||<=C(1+D_tree)epsilon.

Taking epsilon<=c r/(1+D_tree), with the fixed logarithm/color constants,
keeps the whole real path in the Ward domain. Integrating the Ward
identity and then using constant conjugation proves(6.1). No global
logarithm of the original v was assumed. QED.

For the actual CHOSEN real retained integral, this hypothesis is proved
for a product of invariant link/color BALLS, with chi_extra=1 (or with
extra indicators separately verified to transform covariantly). It is
not asserted for an arbitrary scalar-coordinate rectangle or arbitrary
extra indicators. The exact saved statements are
common_domain_macrostep_research.md:319--329, especially327, and
small_field_gauge_covariance_research.md:45,74,77--80,119--121.
Here is the real-window argument, before any analytic continuation.
For a small coarse gauge k, its block-constant current lift gives
V_*(W^k)=V_*(W)^(k-up), by physical-branch uniqueness on the common domain.
Writing R_k B(b)=Ad_(k-up(b_-))B(b) and D_k for its retained restriction,
the exact constraint equation and uniqueness give
Phi_(V_*(W^k))(D_k z)=R_k Phi_(V_*(W))(z).
Thus z maps by source-endpoint adjoints, each orthogonal with determinant
one, and the whole product of invariant color balls is preserved exactly.
The original rho is gauge invariant, the absolute contour penalty
transforms by conjugation, and Haar/pivot factors are the same exact
change-of-variable factors. Changing z to D_k z proves equality of the
LITERAL retained integrals. There is no moving-boundary derivative.
Differentiating gives its Ward identity near identity; its separately
proved analytic density then extends that identity across the connected
local analytic ball. Constant conjugation follows by the same argument.
The density version suffices, so no new logarithm branch is needed.
If the ball-window/extra-indicator hypotheses fail, Lemma6.1 remains a
conditional lemma and the measure extension theorem still holds, but
its germ matching is not claimed.

Choose theta supported in the epsilon ball and positive on its half
ball. For W in that half ball, m_theta(W)>0 and every contributing W^v
lies in the epsilon ball. Equation(6.1) makes every numerator value
ell_mu(W^v) equal ell_mu(W). Therefore

 a_mu(W)=ell_mu(W) near I.                            (6.2)

Although theta is only a real cutoff, (6.2) proves that the good measure
has the EXACT analytic local germ. No statement of global analyticity
for a_mu is made.

The proof's epsilon can shrink with the coarse graph diameter. Existing
common-b Ward continuation does not by itself remove that dependence:
it grants invariance along paths inside b, not arbitrary orbit endpoints.
A uniform orbit-overlap theorem would be an additional result.

7. Exact full histories and bounded gauge-invariant observables

At each finite stage j fix its actual completed F_j, contour gauge,
positive g_j, local chart/window and theta_j. Let A_j,R_j be(5.1)-(5.2).
For the original unnormalized finite Wilson measure

 mu_0(dU)=exp[-gamma_0 S_W(U)]m_E(dU), gamma_0>0,

and every word h=(h_0,...,h_(n-1)) in {good,remainder}^n, define

 mu_h=T_(n-1)^(h_(n-1)) ... T_0^(h_0) mu_0,
 T_j^good=A_j, T_j^remainder=R_j.

Every mu_h is a positive invariant finite measure. Induction using
A_j+R_j=F_(j*) proves the EXACT identity

 (F_(n-1) o ... o F_0)_*mu_0 = sum_(h in {g,r}^n) mu_h. (7.1)

There is no omitted history, error term or asymptotic normalization.
In particular

 Z_W=mu_0(1)=sum_h mu_h(1),
 integral f(F_(n-1)...F_0(U))mu_0(dU)=sum_h mu_h(f)    (7.2)

for every bounded measurable terminal coarse observable f.
The literal Markov split(3.4) supplies an alternative exact decomposition
without invariantization; if used, its noninvariant input projection
must be retained at later steps. The two history assignments differ.

For bounded gauge-invariant original observables O_1,...,O_m, apply the
same linear identities to the finite invariant signed measures
O_1...O_m mu_0. Their positive and negative parts remain invariant.
Equivalently, for bounded observables use
exp(sum t_l O_l)mu_0 on real compact source intervals and differentiate
the exact identities. This gives exact finite numerators as well as the
denominator, with parameters/windows held fixed during differentiation.
It gives no uniform source estimates as refinement or volume grows.

No homogeneity or analyticity is imposed on remainder histories. They
may contain singular parts and correlated mixed sectors. Consequently
(7.1) is genuine full-measure bookkeeping, but not a controlled mixed-
field RG theorem. It supplies an explicit positive remainder operator
rather than declaring the remainder zero.

8. Exact local homogeneous identification and vacuum convention

Suppose an all-good input has the actual density

 Z_j exp[-gamma_j S_(M_j)+E_(j,raw)], gamma_j=g_j^(-2),

on a real neighborhood D_in, and its holomorphic local representative
and Ward identities satisfy the established local hypotheses. Require
literal positive-g containment:

 exp(i Phi_(V_*(W))(g_j xi)) V_*(W) in D_in            (8.1)

for every real xi in the actual retained window and every W in the
chosen output neighborhood. Include any original additional indicator
exactly; to identify the already proved homogeneous product-window map,
those additional indicators must be absent or proved identically one.
Under (8.1), formula(4.3) is exactly that local homogeneous integral.
Applying Lemma6.1 and(6.2) preserves its analytic germ in the global good
submeasure. This proves a positive finite-g embedding, not merely a
formal or g->0 identification.

For each fixed finite number of stages, (8.1) and the finite overlap
shrinks can be met by choosing sufficiently small windows in group
angle and sufficiently weak couplings. A logarithmic retained threshold
T(g) is compatible because g T(g)->0. This assertion is only finite
parameter feasibility; it does not say the selected Notebook37 tuned
histories already obey a volume-uniform version of(8.1).

Let A_0 be the flat retained precision. The exact field-independent
factor extracted in one stage is

 N_j=c(g_j)^{-n_s}h_0^{n_e-n_c}g_j^{n_z}(2pi)^{n_z/2}
                     D_R(1)det(A_0)^{-1/2}.          (8.2)

The local output is
 Z_j N_j exp[-gamma_j S_(M_(j+1))+F_(j,raw)].
Here F_raw includes E at the pre-gauge background, the actual pivot log
ratio, the determinant log ratio, and the logarithm of the exact
retained Gaussian expectation; its value at zero is not discarded.
For a finite reference normalized by S_(M_(j+1))(0)=0, write exactly

 F_(j,raw)=beta_j S_(M_(j+1))+E_(j+1,raw)+c_j,
 c_j=F_(j,raw)(0)-E_(j+1,raw)(0).

Then

 gamma_(j+1)=gamma_j-beta_j,
 Z_(j+1)=Z_j N_j exp(c_j).                            (8.3)

A replacement E'_raw=E_raw+c at fixed local density requires
Z'=Z exp(-c). The orbit extension(5.1) is linear, so every scalar in
(8.2)-(8.3) is retained exactly also in the global good submeasure.
These are local factorization identities; no global definition of the
classical exponential or of the analytic inherited E is required.

9. Quantitative boundary: what this does and does not close

For the all-good terminal measure nu_good and full terminal measure nu,
let epsilon_bad=1-nu_good(1)/nu(1). Positivity gives for bounded f

 |nu(f)/nu(1)-nu_good(f)/nu_good(1)|
             <=2 ||f||_infinity epsilon_bad,          (9.1)

whenever nu_good has positive mass. This follows by writing the full
normalized law as the mixture of normalized good and complementary
laws. To use(9.1), one must prove a useful bound on epsilon_bad, or
control mixed histories directly. No such bound follows from the
identities in this note. A global all-good event can in fact be a poor
large-volume comparison; local observable control may require a
spatial mixed-history expansion rather than its total mass.

The precise remaining quantitative tasks include:
(i) uniform literal-chart and orbit-domain matching for the fixed
positive-g histories, or a collared/gauge-invariant sector construction
that avoids the volume-dependent overlap shrink in Lemma6.1;
(ii) coercive interacting estimates, normalization comparisons and
summable spatial weights for R_j, including histories with bad input
fields and their conditional means/constraint geometry;
(iii) a local factorization of mixed histories that controls boundaries
without incorrectly replacing compressed inverse precisions by blocks
of the full covariance;
(iv) observable/source bounds with the same exact denominator and
history decomposition.
Neither Gaussian bad-block tails around a selected homogeneous saddle
nor local analytic amplitude closure proves these statements.

The operators(5.1)-(5.2) give an exact positive finite embedding and a
specified remainder, with all factors and the original fine Wilson
measure intact. They do not yet furnish the uniform estimates needed
to call H2 fully closed, and they do not establish a continuum measure,
OS hierarchy or physical mass gap.
