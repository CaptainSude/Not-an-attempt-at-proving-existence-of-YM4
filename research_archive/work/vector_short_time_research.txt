2. A uniform flat counting-L2 bound for the ACTUAL composite C_K

Write D_r for the scalar forward difference on the lattice of spacing
r, as an ANGLE difference (no division by r). Let A_r be the straight
angle averaging map from unit bonds to r-bonds:

 (A_r v)(y,mu)=r^-d sum_(x in centered r-block at y)
                           sum_(h=0)^(r-1) v_mu(x+h e_mu).

Let b_ell be the scalar row given by the mean internal root-contour
sum in one primitive ell-block. The actual primitive arithmetic
differential is exactly

 C_ell=A_ell-D_ell b_ell.                          (2.1)

Its gauge identity is C_ell D_1=D_ell R_ell, where R_ell
restricts a scalar field to the new centers. The straight operators
satisfy A_ell^(r) A_r=A_(ell r). This follows directly by tiling
the larger centered block by the smaller ones and concatenating
the ell strings of length r.

Induction therefore gives

 C_(ell^k)=A_(ell^k)-D_(ell^k) b_(ell^k),
 b_(ell r)=b_ell^(r) A_r+R_ell b_r.                (2.2)

At a fixed final center y, the scalar row b_K(y,.) is a sum of
the rows b_ell^(ell^j)(y,.) A_(ell^j), 0<=j<k.
The restriction in (2.2) merely keeps that same nested center.
There is no sum over all intermediate centers at this step.

For clarity, the full scalar operator identity includes the final
restrictions explicitly:

 b_K=sum_(j=0)^(k-1) R_(ell^(j+1)->K)
                            b_ell^(ell^j) A_(ell^j).       (2.2a)

The restrictions are counting-L2 contractions. There is also a direct
stronger global estimate for A_r: its absolute row sums are r and
its column sums are r^(1-d). For a column, the two adjacent
longitudinal output intervals have complementary multiplicities
adding to r; the transverse output block is unique. This remains
true with the prescribed periodic occurrences. The Schur test gives

 ||A_r||_(2->2)<=r^(1-d/2).

Thus (2.2a), the fixed operator bound for b_ell, and
||D_final||<=2 sqrt(d) already give a uniform full C_K bound in d=4.
The row-and-incidence proof below is a second verification and also
records the localized information used later.

A row of A_r has coefficients at most r^(1-d) and at most
2r^d nonzero bond occurrences. Hence

 ||A_r(c,.)||_l2 <= sqrt(2) r^(1-d/2).             (2.3)

The row l1 norm b_* of the fixed primitive b_ell is finite.
In d=4 the triangle inequality gives

 ||b_K(y,.)||_l2 <= sqrt(2)b_* sum_(j>=0) ell^-j.

Thus each full C_K row has a bounded l2 norm, uniformly in K.
This is O(1), NOT O(K^-1). The sampled-center gauge identity
precludes the latter general claim.

Every fine bond belongs to the dependency sets of at most N_d
different final output rows, with N_d depending only on dimension.
Indeed an internal bond lies in one final block and can affect
only coarse bonds incident to that block; a crossing bond can affect
only the neighboring pair. A conservative N_d=4d covers oriented
and periodic lifted occurrences. Consequently, if R_* bounds the
row l2 norms,

 sum_c |C_K v(c)|^2
 <= R_*^2 sum_c sum_(b in supp C_K(c,.)) |v(b)|^2
 <= N_d R_*^2 ||v||_2^2.                          (2.4)

The color differential at flatness is the same scalar matrix in
each trace-orthonormal color component. Hence there is a fixed
C_0>=1 such that every consecutive flat composite segment has
counting-L2 norm at most C_0, including the empty segment.
The source periods at least 2K and their prescribed local lifts
preserve the stated finite incidence bound. No physical measure
factor has been inserted in this counting-space conclusion.

3. Uniform local magnetic jets of the full composite constraint

First consider one final output row in the two K-blocks near zero.
At stage j, put r=ell^j. The exact affine averaging identity gives
the stage background, in level-j lattice coordinates,

 w_j(z)=t (r^2/K^2) z_nu H in component mu.

Every intermediate background coordinate needed by this final row
has |z|<=c_ell K/r, by the exact nested dependency geometry. Hence

 ||w_j||_needed <= c_ell |t| r/K.                  (3.1)

Extend these needed stage background values, separately at each
stage, to globally bounded angle fields with the same supremum
bound, for example by clipping the coordinate outside a larger
fixed dependency box. These extensions need not themselves be
successive blockings. They are used only to estimate the product
of derivative matrices. On all entries contributing to the selected
row they coincide with the actual successive affine backgrounds,
so the selected product row is exactly the actual one.

Let F_j be the flat primitive matrix and E_j(t) the difference
between the primitive derivative at this extended stage background
and F_j. The primitive analytic finite-range bound gives, on one
fixed disk |t|<rho_0,

 ||E_j(t)||_(2->2) <= C_ell |t| ell^j/K.           (3.2)

Both the row and column incidences of a primitive matrix are fixed.
Thus its entrywise analytic estimate indeed implies this operator
bound, with no number-of-sites factor.

Expand the product of F_j+E_j by its subsets of marked stages.
Every intervening product of flat matrices is an actual flat
composite segment, bounded by C_0 from Section2. A term with n
marked stages therefore costs at most C_0^(n+1) times the product
of the n error norms. Summing yields

 ||product_j(F_j+E_j(t))||_(2->2)
 <= C_0 product_j(1+C_0||E_j(t)||)
 <= C_0 exp(C C_0 |t| sum_(j<k) ell^j/K)
 <= C_0 exp(C C_0 |t|/(ell-1)).                   (3.3)

This proves a uniform analytic l2 bound for the actual selected row.
Cauchy on a fixed smaller disk gives uniform row l2 bounds for
the Taylor coefficients C_0,C_1,C_2 of C_K(t) near that root.

For an arbitrary coarse row at y, translating the affine field
adds a constant Cartan potential. It is removed by the exact
site gauge exp(i t y_nu x_mu H/K). Gauge covariance expresses
that row by the centered row and input/output adjoint frames.
On its two K-blocks, derivatives of these frames through order two
are bounded by a fixed polynomial in |y|, independently of K.
For example the individual frame exponents are bounded by
C|t|(1+|y|)^2. Thus, for j=0,1,2,

 ||C_j(y,.)||_l2 <= C_j (1+|y|)^(2j).             (3.4)

Their support is still the same two final blocks. This statement
concerns polynomial jets and does not assert an analytic operator
family at a nonzero unbounded affine field.

Group fine bonds by their initial-site K-block and final bonds
by their initial coarse site. The latter fiber has fixed dimension
r_c=d dim(g). From (3.4) and finite incidence, C_j has uniformly
bounded finite-range block operator kernels with polynomial
growth of degree at most 2j in the block indices. In particular
C_0 and C_0^T have uniform weighted block Schur norms for every
fixed block exponent.

4. The base vector B_M has a direct uniform ultraviolet bound

At t=0, B_0=q_M+D_bD_b^T is a bounded positive operator with a
uniform fine-lattice exponential Schur bound. No spectral gap
is needed. Hence, for 0<=s<=1, its semigroup has a uniform
exponential matrix bound, by its convergent power series in
that Schur algebra.

The ordinary and relative scalar action bounds through order
four imply that the first and second background derivatives of
B have exponentially rooted fine-coordinate kernels uniformly
in M. The nearest-neighbor based-gradient term has the same
property uniformly in the center spacing K.

For each fine diagonal bond rooted at x, apply a Cartan gauge
which subtracts the constant potential t x_nu H/K^2. The
diagonal color heat trace is unchanged. In that gauge the
source field is t(z_nu-x_nu)H/K^2. Each first magnetic
vertex has the factor K^-2; each quadratic vertex has K^-4.
The remaining source-coordinate polynomials are absorbed by
the uniform fine exponential kernels.

The one-insertion/two-insertion heat formula therefore gives

 |[t^2] tr_g (exp(-sB(t)))_(xx)|
                  <= C K^-4 (s+s^2) exp(Cs), 0<s<=1.        (4.1)

This uses only local matrix gauge covariance and the polynomial
extension of its exponentially summable Taylor identities.
It is not an evaluation on a globally bounded nonzero affine
configuration. There are d K^4 bond roots in one block, so

 |[t^2] Tr_(block0,E) exp(-sB(t))|
                            <= C(s+s^2) exp(Cs).             (4.2)

Its integral with ds/s on (0,1) is uniformly bounded.

5. Uniform macroblock bounds needed for the constraint insertion

The B_j kernels, j=0,1,2, also have uniformly bounded

polynomial in the block indices for j>0. To see this directly,
the fine source polynomials are divided by K^(2j), while
positions in block X have magnitude at most CK(1+|X|).
This leaves at most a fixed polynomial in |X|+|Y|, uniformly
in K>=1. Fine matrix Schur bounds control each block operator
norm with no factor from the block dimension.

For well-separated blocks, the fine distance is at least
K(d_block(X,Y)-d). Thus a smaller fixed positive block
exponent is summable uniformly in K. The finitely many
neighboring block offsets are controlled by the full fine
operator norm. These observations also apply to the flat
matrices and to all the needed polynomially weighted products.

Set A(t)=a C_K(t)^T C_K(t) and V(t)=B(t)+A(t).
Section3 gives the same block bounds for A_j and V_j. At

The block semigroup power series and the one/two insertion

 ||([t^j] exp(-sE(t)))_(XY)||

with fixed constants; for j>0 one can also keep the usual

6. Exact localized trace cyclicity with a fixed coarse rank

Let P_0 project onto fine bonds rooted in block zero.
The exact Duhamel difference is

 exp(-sV(t))-exp(-sB(t))
  =-a integral_0^s exp(-(s-u)V(t))
                      C_K(t)^T C_K(t) exp(-uB(t)) du.       (6.1)

At each coefficient through t^2, cyclicity gives

 Tr_fine[P_0 E_V C^T C E_B]
             =Tr_coarse[C E_B P_0 E_V C^T].                (6.2)

IMPORTANT: P_0 is retained. The right side is the sum of ALL
coarse diagonal blocks, not the single coarse block-zero
density. Thus this does not assert an unjustified equality
between arbitrary fine and coarse local trace densities.

For each coarse root Y, the diagonal block on the right is
the product of a map from the fine block-zero Hilbert space
to its r_c-dimensional coarse fiber and a map back. By
(3.4),(5.1), all coefficients through degree two of these
two maps have norms bounded by

 C (1+|Y|)^p exp(-kappa'|Y|).

The absolute trace of their product is bounded by r_c times
the product of these operator bounds, independent of the
dimension d dim(g) K^4 of the intervening fine block.
Summing Y gives a uniform finite constant. Expanding the
degree-two coefficient of (6.2) therefore proves

 |[t^2] Tr_(block0,E)
       {exp(-sV(t))-exp(-sB(t))}| <= Cs, 0<s<=1.           (6.3)

One may justify (6.2) first on finite boxes/compact sources;
the displayed rooted sums are absolute and supply the limit.
Equivalently each factor through the finite P_0 is trace
class for fixed K, while the estimate uses the fixed coarse
fiber dimension to avoid the growing K^4 bound.
The algebra holds for the holomorphic bilinear transpose;
at the flat real point it is the usual adjoint.

Combining (4.2) and (6.3) proves the claim in Section1.
All constants in this argument are uniform in the preceding
depth M and the composite endpoint K=ell^k.

7. Scope and earlier dead ends

The invalid shortcut would be ||C_K||=O(K^-1) for the full
ordinary map. Sampled-center gauge transformations rule it out.
The actual weaker O(1) estimate proved in Section2 is enough.
It uses the COMPOSITE fixed-ell hierarchy. Direct-K contour
averaging can have very different raw row bounds; it was not
substituted for that hierarchy.

Bounding a local trace simply by its fine block dimension would
lose K^4. The constraint comparison instead keeps one explicit
C^T C insertion and takes the trace on its fixed-rank coarse
fiber with the fine block projector still in place.

The argument supplies only the absolute heat integral for
0<s<1, equivalently physical times 0<u<K^-2. Its semigroup
bounds use exp(Cs), not a uniform large-time gap. It neither
proves the continuum coefficient on intermediate times nor
the cancellation of the soft based-ghost contribution at
large times. The latter may require a combined signed integral.

There is no assertion about a continuously varying magnetic
flux on a finite periodic lattice. All affine probes above
are polynomial jets in the compatible infinite structural
system, as in Notebook33.
