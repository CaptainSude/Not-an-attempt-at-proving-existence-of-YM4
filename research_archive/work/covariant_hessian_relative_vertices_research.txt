LOCAL GAUGE COVARIANCE GIVES PHYSICAL RELATIVE HESSIAN VERTICES
17 October 2026. Root research proof for independent audit.

1. Precise statement

Let L(a) be a family of left-relative bond Hessians on the unit lattice,
in a fixed semisimple color representation. It may be the actual
effective S_M''; it is NOT required to be the bare Wilson Hessian.
Assume exact sitewise adjoint covariance, transpose symmetry, and
uniform exponentially rooted current-coordinate Taylor kernels at zero
through background degree two. The latter are the scalar-action kernels
through degree four, including the local relative-coordinate changes.
Let q=L(0) be translation invariant, color diagonal, and satisfy
  sum_r q_(alpha,beta)(r)=0,
  sum_r r_rho q_(alpha,beta)(r)=0
for every bond-component pair alpha,beta and every rho.
Thus its tail-coordinate Fourier symbol is O(|p|^2). For the actual
q_M this follows from its previously proved midpoint Maxwell expansion;
the analytic midpoint-to-tail phases preserve the order of vanishing.
Assume its exponentially weighted row norm and moments are uniform.
These inputs are available for actual S_M from the classical support
module, Ward identities and the inputs of vector_short_time_research.
For this application work on the chosen joint INFINITE classical
structural system, where the continuous symbol and its exact moments
are established. The same argument applies to explicit periodizations
of those kernels. It does not identify an arbitrary actual finite
winding kernel with that periodization or infer exact continuous
moment cancellation from a discrete finite momentum grid.

Set mu=1/K and consider a(t,e)=t mu A(e), where the physical field has
  ||A||_phys,1 = ||A||_infinity
                    +mu^-1 max_nearest |A(e')-A(e)| <= a_*.
Differences compare bonds of the same component. Smoothly sampled
compact physical fields satisfy this norm uniformly in K. The probe
can later be extended polynomially using localized source kernels.

Claim: coefficients L_j=[t^j]L(t mu A), j=1,2, have decompositions
  L_1=D0^T Z_1+Z_1^T D0+E_1,
  L_2=D0^T Z_2+Z_2^T D0+E_2,                          (1.1)
where D0 here means the ordinary componentwise gradient of a BOND
field (four copies of the scalar gradient), not the scalar gauge
generator. Their counting operator bounds are
  ||Z_1||<=C mu a_*,       ||E_1||<=C mu^2 a_*,
  ||Z_2||<=C mu^2 a_*^2,  ||E_2||<=C mu^2 a_*^2.       (1.2)
The constants are independent of K and old depth M. In particular,
with R0=Delta_bond+mu^2 I,
  ||R0^-1/2 L_j R0^-1/2||<=C_j a_*^j.                (1.3)

The stronger mu^2 bound on Z2 is not needed for (1.3). The same
decompositions hold for mixed second derivatives by polarization.
For smooth physical directions localized to bounded block collars,
the factors have exponentially rooted final-block operator bounds
with the powers of mu in (1.2). A detailed localization argument is
given in Section5; no sharp background cutoff is differentiated.

2. Local path gauges, used only through two formal coefficients

For every displacement r choose a path of length |r|_1 from x to x+r,
translation covariantly, with the reverse path chosen for -r. One
construction chooses a coordinate-order path when the first nonzero
component of r is positive, then defines the negative-r path by reversal.
The paths need not form a tree. Let W_xy(t) be adjoint transport along
that chosen path, with convention matched to the gauge covariance of L.
It is complex orthogonal coefficientwise and W_yx=W_xy^T.

Fix a matrix-row source site x. Define a formal gauge g_x(z) by the
chosen transport from x to z, so g_x(x)=I. This is a legitimate
sitewise gauge; path consistency beyond the endpoint values is not
required. A transformed edge is a closed product around the chosen
x-to-z and x-to-z+e paths and that edge. The loop length is at most
C(1+|z-x|), and a coordinate plaquette filling has area at most
C(1+|z-x|)^2.

Write its transformed link angle as t b1(z)+t^2 b2(z)+O(t^3).
The linear coefficient is the ordinary signed sum of t mu A around
the loop. Discrete Stokes and the physical first-difference assumption
give
  |b1(z)|<=C mu^2 a_* (1+|z-x|)^2.                   (2.1)
For b2, the second coefficient of a product/logarithm is a sum of
quadratic products of link first coefficients. There are at most the
square of the loop length such pairs. Thus
  |b2(z)|<=C mu^2 a_*^2 (1+|z-x|)^2.                 (2.2)
The same bounds hold for complex colors by a fixed finite-dimensional
norm. At mu<=1 all additional harmless polynomial factors can be
absorbed by the existing exponential source reserve.

These are formal jets. The unbounded gauge g_x is not inserted into
a common global analytic ball. Its first two coefficients, and b1,b2,
have polynomial growth. The exponentially rooted covariance identities
extend to them by truncating the gauge far away, applying the finite
identity, and passing absolutely in each Taylor coefficient. This is
the same polynomial-jet argument already used for the affine probe.

3. Comparison with the flat kernel dressed by parallel transport

Gauge covariance, g_x(x)=I and the endpoint value g_x(y) give
  L(t mu A)_(x alpha,y beta)
     = L(b(t))_(x alpha,y beta) W_xy(t),               (3.1)
with an immaterial inversion of both transport conventions if the
opposite gauge sign is used. Define
  L^P(t)_(x alpha,y beta)=q_(alpha,beta)(y-x) W_xy(t).
It is transpose symmetric because q is and W_yx=W_xy^T.

Expand L(b(t)) at zero using the already specified rooted kernels.
At first order its difference from q is L'[b1]. At second order it is
L'[b2]+(1/2)L''[b1,b1]. Equations (2.1)-(2.2), the exponential kernel
moments and the bound |[t]W_xy|<=C mu a_* |x-y| give
  ||[t](L-L^P)||_Schur<=C mu^2 a_*,
  ||[t^2](L-L^P)||_Schur<=C mu^2 a_*^2.               (3.2)
The L'' term is O(mu^4 a_*^2), and the product of L'[b1] with
the transport first coefficient is O(mu^3 a_*^2); both are bounded
as in (3.2). All Schur sums are in current fine coordinates with a
fixed color/component fiber. No final-block dimension is counted.

4. Ordered Laurent factorization and covariant completion

An earlier draft incorrectly wrote L^P as a common covariant-difference
quadratic form using only q(r)=q(-r)^T. Rotation audit caught that this
would discard the component-skew part of q(r). Even the forward-gradient
Maxwell kernel has such tail-coordinate shifts. The following ordered
factorization is the replacement; no componentwise evenness is assumed.

Apply the ordered Laurent divided-difference lemma of
low_kernel_spatial_transport_research.md Section2 twice to the symbol q.
The zero constant and linear jets make both divisions removable and give
  q=D0^T H0 D0,                                      (4.1)
where H0 acts on the component-gradient tensor and has uniform
exponentially summable kernels. For a monomial z^r each division costs
at most |r| and a smaller exponential; the two divisions therefore cost
only a fixed second exponential moment. This supplies uniform constants
in old depth M. Replace H0 by (H0+H0^T)/2, preserving (4.1), so H0 is
symmetric. It remains color diagonal.

Let D_A be the nearest-neighbor covariant COMPONENT gradient, and dress
each H0 entry with the reverse-consistent endpoint transport W_xy:
  H^P(A)_(x rho alpha,y sigma beta)
           =H0_(rho alpha,sigma beta)(y-x) W_xy(A).
Define Qcov(A)=D_A^T H^P(A) D_A. It is exactly covariant and symmetric,
and Qcov(0)=q. Path-product derivatives have polynomial length factors,
so H^P and Qcov have the same kind of uniform exponentially rooted
current Taylor kernels through order two. For a fixed small complex
current-angle ball, the path-product growth exp(C||a|| |r|) is absorbed
by the exponential reserve. Thus the polynomial-gauge argument of
Sections2--3 applies to Qcov as well as to L.

Both have the SAME flat q, hence the SAME Peierls comparison L^P.
Subtracting their two versions of (3.2) gives symmetric errors
  ||[t](L-Qcov)||_Schur<=C mu^2 a_*,
  ||[t^2](L-Qcov)||_Schur<=C mu^2 a_*^2.               (4.2)

Write D_(tA)=D0+t F1+t^2 F2 and H^P(tA)=H0+t H1+t^2 H2
through this order. Direct product expansion gives
  ||F1||<=C mu a_*,   ||F2||<=C mu^2 a_*^2,
  ||H1||<=C mu a_*,   ||H2||<=C mu^2 a_*^2.
The H estimates again use exponential path moments. Set
  Z1=(1/2)H1 D0+H0 F1,
  Z2=(1/2)H2 D0+H1 F1+H0 F2.                         (4.3)
Since H0,H1,H2 are symmetric, the first coefficient of Qcov is
D0^T Z1+Z1^T D0, and the second is
D0^T Z2+Z2^T D0+F1^T H0 F1. The counting norm of D0 is bounded by
a dimensional constant. Consequently Z1=O(mu a_*), Z2=O(mu^2 a_*^2),
and F1^T H0 F1=O(mu^2 a_*^2). Add the errors (4.2) to the zero-order
parts E1,E2. This proves (1.1)-(1.2) without the false difference form.

Finally ||D0 R0^-1/2||<=1 and ||R0^-1/2||<=mu^-1. These bounds
give (1.3), including mixed derivatives by polarization. This proof
uses exact covariance before estimating; the naive absolute bound
on L'(a) by C||a|| would have lost one power of K.

5. Source localization and physical weights

For source estimates choose a fixed smooth physical partition on
final-block collars, with bounded first derivatives and bounded overlap.
Local source spaces consist of complete fields a_j supported in those
collars with the physical norm in Section1. This is the same complete-
field localization convention as classical_local_vertex_bounds.

Every term constructing H1 or H2 reads source values only along its
path, and F1,F2 have nearest-neighbor support. If a localized source
label is far from both endpoints, that path must have correspondingly
large length. The exponentially decaying coefficient H0(r) pays that
length. Thus the factors in (4.3) and each transport-product term in
E_j have final-block matrix bounds with exp[-c tau(row,column,sources)]
and the displayed mu powers, after spending an exponential reserve.

For the remainder in (3.2), each original Taylor kernel connects the
matrix row, column and its current background sources exponentially.
The local gauge coefficients b1,b2 connect any further source labels
to x by the finite paths already described. If those paths are long,
one of the original background-source distances or the matrix
displacement is equally long; its exponential kernel pays the path.
Discrete Stokes rewrites b1 using plaquette differences, whose source
support is in the same smooth collar enlarged by one fine edge.
Second coefficients involve pairs on the same path and obey the same
tree bound. Polynomial path multiplicities are absorbed by the reserve.
Schur summation in fine coordinates therefore gives exactly the
uniform block-operator bounds, not a sum of K^4 independent entries.

Weights e^(theta f(x/K)), for Lipschitz physical f, may be included:
their changes along a path are at most exp(C theta mu |r|), absorbed
by the current exponential reserve. When moving an ordinary gradient
past the weight, the extra multiplication operator has norm O(theta mu),
which is absorbed in E1 or E2 at their stated order. This supplies the
weighted localized relative vertices needed between resolvents whose
elliptic control is Delta_bond+mu^2I.

6. Actual use and limits

For L=S_M'' the old-depth-uniform inputs are exactly the action
support/Cauchy bounds through order four and gauge covariance already
used in Notebook34's ultraviolet theorem. The flat separate-component
row sums follow from the quadratic Ward identity extended to linear
scalar parameters. Thus (1.1)-(1.3) apply uniformly in M.

The statement concerns bounded physical C1 fields and their localized
jets. It does not give the same bound for arbitrary rapidly oscillating
bounded physical potentials: their curvature can be O(K^-1) instead
of O(K^-2). Nor is it an estimate for the hard-projection Ghat.

For the actual vector soft completion, the affine on-shell identification
removes the multiplier and gives q_coarse D_c=0 through the required
jets. Then its first-two affine jets can be calculated from
  S_M''(exp(i A/K))+D_A D_A^T
       +Cbar_A^T [aI-S_MK''(M_K(exp(i A/K)))] Cbar_A.
The corrected Cbar has full norm and physical background derivatives
of order K^-1, while the displayed coarse Hessian has uniform local
bounds. These auxiliary off-shell formulas are to be checked against
the exact soft-vector determinant identity before using the new
relative vertices to claim a bounded coarse vector correction.
This note alone does not compute a one-loop logarithmic coefficient.
