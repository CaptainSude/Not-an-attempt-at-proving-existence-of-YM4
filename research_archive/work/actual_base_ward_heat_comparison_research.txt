MAIN RESULT. For the actual homogeneous classical action S_M,
the background-Feynman base operator
 B_M(a)=S_M''(exp(i a))+D_a D_a^T
has, on the selected infinite structural systems, a magnetic heat
coefficient differing from the fixed M=1 Wilson base by O(1/s),
uniformly in old depth M, for current heat time s>=1.
The proof uses total heat two-point kernels and the existing
gauge-invariant density/reanchoring identity. It does not cyclically
permute a partial block trace, differentiate a massless determinant,
or insert a finite-period varying magnetic flux.

This conclusion is conditional only on the explicitly listed classical
inputs in Section1. Those inputs are the previously proved current-unit
classical kernel bounds, full nonlinear Ward identities, flat Maxwell
normalization and ellipticity. The proof itself is Sections2--7.
Reference coefficient evaluation, finite-regulator drift/trajectory,
and full existence are separate.

1. Exact inputs and why their constants can be uniform in M

Work on Z^4 with a fixed compact simple semisimple color factor,
Hermitian link-angle variables and the established normalized
invariant trace. A finite direct sum can be treated factorwise.

For every old depth M, let S_M be the actual gauge-invariant
homogeneous classical minimum action, in CURRENT UNIT coordinates.
Use the following established inputs:

 (a) translations, the full hypercubic group, reality, and the
     exact nonlinear local Ward identity;
 (b) uniform exponentially rooted current-coordinate kernels of
     field orders two, three and four, with a fixed positive
     spatial exponent and all the finite moments used below;
 (c) q_M=S_M''(1) is positive and satisfies
       q_M(p)>=q_W(p),  q_M(p)D(p)=0,
       q_M,mid(p)=|p|^2 I-pp^T+O(|p|^4)
     uniformly in M. Here q_W=d_curl^T d_curl is the fixed
     normalized Wilson flat Hessian;
 (d) the ordinary-to-left-relative local exponential chart and its
     first two field jets are included in the kernel bounds.

The current input fiber has d dim(g) coordinates per site, independent
of M. The bounds in (b) are the actual classical current-unit bounds,
not the weaker statement that an arbitrary quantum interaction has
constants depending on a newly chosen auxiliary grouping J. No
J-dependent constant is hidden in the claim of uniformity in M.

Let S_W=S_1. The total nonlinear Ward extraction, applied to
R_M=S_M-S_W, has zero Maxwell coefficient by (c). It therefore gives,
in bond-midpoint Fourier conventions,
 R_2=O(|p|^4),
 R_3=O((max |p_i|)^3),
 R_4=O((max |p_i|)^2).                                  (1.1)
All constants and all needed momentum derivatives are uniform in M
by (b). Changing to source-site bond phases preserves these orders.

The claim concerns the chosen infinite structural systems, where the
continuous-momentum identities have been proved. It does not infer
(1.1) from a finite momentum grid, or identify unrelated finite winding
kernels with exact periodizations.

2. Transfer to the actual left-relative Hessian vertices

For a background ordinary angle a, use the actual local chart
 Xi_a(B)=log(exp(iB)exp(ia))/i.
Let J(a)B=D_B Xi_a(0)[B] and
T(a)[B,C]=D_B^2 Xi_a(0)[B,C]. Its local analytic expansion has
 J=I+J1(a)+J2(a,a)+O(a^3),     T=T1(a)+O(a^2).             (2.1)
In particular T(0)=0. Its coefficients have fixed finite color
norms and are local at the same bond.

The left-relative Hessian of R_M is exactly
 L_R(a)[B,C]
 =R_M''(a)[J(a)B,J(a)C]+R_M'(a)[T(a)[B,C]].              (2.2)
This formula retains the current times chart-curvature term.

At degree zero it is R_2. At background degree one it is
 R_3[a,B,C]+R_2[J1(a)B,C]+R_2[B,J1(a)C].                 (2.3)
At degree two its coefficient is
 (1/2)R_4[a,a,B,C]
 +R_3[a,J1(a)B,C]+R_3[a,B,J1(a)C]
 +R_2[J2(a,a)B,C]+R_2[B,J2(a,a)C]
 +R_2[J1(a)B,J1(a)C]
 +R_2[a,T1(a)[B,C]].                                   (2.4)
For mixed derivatives polarize this identity, with the corresponding
Taylor/derivative factors.

Local products in J1,J2,T1 only merge momenta. They introduce no
inverse momenta and no loss of vanishing degree. Thus (1.1)
implies the following uniform low-momentum bounds for the ACTUAL
relative Hessian differences:

 flat difference: total momentum degree at least 4;
 first background vertex difference: degree at least 3;
 second background vertex difference: degree at least 2.             (2.5)

All terms in (2.3)--(2.4) with R_2 have degree at least four;
those with R_3 have degree at least three. In particular dropping
the last term of (2.4) is unnecessary and would be unjustified.

The gauge-fixing term D_a D_a^T is identical for M and W and cancels
in the differences. Its first vertex is O(total momentum), and its
second vertex is O(1). The same baseline orders hold for the Wilson
relative action Hessian: its cubic total kernel starts at momentum
degree one, while its quartic kernel is bounded.

3. Fourier symbols and precise analytic estimates

Put B_M=B_M(0)=q_M+D D^T. At flatness
 B_W(p)=delta(p) I,      delta(p)=4 sum_mu sin^2(p_mu/2).
The source lower bound in (c) gives
 c delta(p)I<=B_M(p)<=C delta(p)I                         (3.1)
with fixed positive c,C, uniformly in M. The upper bound follows
from the uniform quadratic zero and bounded second moments.

Choose a fixed small momentum ball |k|<r0. Write rho=|k|.
Uniform analyticity, (1.1), and (2.5) imply, for derivatives of
total order r<=2,
 ||partial^r(B_M-B_W)(k)||<=C rho^(4-r),
 ||partial B_M(k)||<=C rho,   ||partial^2 B_M(k)||<=C.    (3.2)

For background direction a (including its bond/color index), define
V_a^M(k,p) so that the first vertex for a source e^(ip.x)e_a maps
a quantum Fourier mode k to k+p. Define
W_ab^M(k;p,-p) as the second mixed vertex with those two backgrounds;
it preserves quantum momentum k.
Let delta denote M minus W. Then, at p=0, for every external-p
derivative of order r<=2,
 ||partial_p^r delta V_a(k,p)|_0||<=C rho^(3-r),
 ||partial_p^r delta V_b(k+p,-p)|_0||<=C rho^(3-r),
 ||partial_p^r delta W_ab(k;p,-p)|_0||<=C rho^(2-r).       (3.3)
The shifted V_b derivative differentiates both its arguments; the
total analytic bound gives the same estimate.
For either baseline M or W one can use the convenient weaker bounds
 ||partial_p^r V_a|_0||+
 ||partial_p^r V_b(k+p,-p)|_0||<=C rho^(1-r),
 ||partial_p^r W_ab|_0||<=C rho^(-r).                    (3.4)
The negative powers in (3.4) for r=1,2 are only harmless upper
bounds on analytic functions, not asserted singularities.
In particular partial_p^2 W is actually bounded.

All these symbols and their required derivatives are uniformly bounded
outside the small momentum ball. At zero use continuity or integrate
on the punctured ball; the final estimates are integrable.

4. An exact heat two-point diagram identity

At each fixed s>0 consider the second Taylor kernel of the total
heat functional
 F_s^M(a)=Tr(exp[-s B_M(a)]-exp[-s B_M(0)]).
For compact sources this can be defined by its trace-class first-two
Duhamel variations. Exponentially rooted current vertices and the
bounded flat semigroup make those variations absolutely summable
at fixed s. Equivalently define the translation-invariant Taylor
kernel directly, without assigning an extensive vacuum trace.

The full-trace identity, for compact directions a,b, is
 D^2 F_s^M(0)[a,b]
 =-s Tr(e^-sB_M B_M''[a,b])
  +s^2 int_0^1 Tr(e^-s(1-v)B_M B_M'[a]
                           e^-svB_M B_M'[b]) dv.         (4.1)
It follows by differentiating
 DF_s^M[a]=-s Tr(e^-sB_M B_M'[a]).
The v integral supplies both ordered insertions; there is no
additional factor two. This is a TOTAL trace identity, not a
cyclic manipulation of the trace on one final block.

The external momentum kernel H_ab^M(s,p) is consequently
 -s int_BZ tr[e^-sB_M(k) W_ab^M(k;p,-p)] dk/(2pi)^4
 +s^2 int_0^1 int_BZ
   tr[e^-s(1-v)B_M(k+p) V_a^M(k,p)
             e^-svB_M(k) V_b^M(k+p,-p)] dk/(2pi)^4 dv.  (4.2)
This convention matches the input/output momenta of Section3;
the equivalent cyclicly reordered formula gives the same integral.

For fixed s the kernels have all finite spatial moments. Thus
their external derivatives at p=0 can be taken under (4.2).
The estimates below improve the naive exp(Cs) bounds uniformly
as s tends to infinity.

5. Elementary matrix heat estimates, with no spectral frames

Let E_M(t,k)=exp[-tB_M(k)]. Uniform ellipticity and Duhamel
differentiation give, for r=0,1,2 on the small punctured ball,
 ||partial_k^r E_M(t,k)||
 <=C_r rho^(-r) (1+t rho^2)^r exp[-c t rho^2].            (5.1)
For r=1 the sharper expression is C t rho exp[-c t rho^2];
for r=2 it is C(t+t^2 rho^2) exp[-c t rho^2].
No eigenbasis or derivative of a spectral projection is used.

For the difference use the exact formula
 E_M(t,k)-E_W(t,k)
 =-int_0^t E_M(t-u,k) [B_M(k)-B_W(k)] E_W(u,k) du.
Differentiating at most twice and using (3.2) gives
 ||partial_k^r(E_M-E_W)(t,k)||
 <=C_r t rho^(4-r)(1+t rho^2)^r exp[-c t rho^2],
                                      r=0,1,2.         (5.2)
For example a derivative hitting an exponential contributes
u rho, while one hitting the difference symbol reduces its
fourth-order zero by one. Two exponential derivatives give at
most u+u^2 rho^2. Every term has the displayed form.

At a fixed distance from zero all derivatives instead have a
polynomial-in-t times exp(-ct) bound, with constants uniform in M.
These estimates hold for all t>=0; in particular they produce
NO singularity when v approaches 0 or 1 in (4.2).

6. Uniform O(1/s) difference of the Maxwell heat kernels

Differentiate (4.2) twice in any external momentum coordinates,
then subtract M and W.

For the tadpole the differences are
 (E_M-E_W) partial_p^2 W_W
             +E_M partial_p^2(W_M-W_W).
The first is bounded by C s rho^4 exp(-cs rho^2);
the second by C exp(-cs rho^2). Including the outer factor s
and integrating in four dimensions gives O(s^-2)+O(s^-1),
uniformly for s>=1.

For the bubble, telescope the product into four terms:
replace either of the two V factors, or either of the two heat
factors. Apply the product rule for the two external derivatives.

In a vertex-replacement term, the two vertex factors have combined
degree at least 3+1=4. If r derivatives hit them, their product is
bounded by C rho^(4-r). The remaining 2-r derivatives of heat
factors contribute at most rho^(-(2-r)) times a fixed polynomial
in s rho^2, by (5.1). The two heat times sum to s. The entire
differentiated product is therefore bounded by
 C rho^2 P(s rho^2) exp[-cs rho^2].                       (6.1)

In a heat-replacement term, (5.2) gives its heat time t<=s
times rho^4 before differentiation, and the two ordinary vertices
supply rho^2. After the two derivatives the bound is
 C s rho^4 P(s rho^2) exp[-cs rho^2].                     (6.2)
This estimate is valid uniformly over v in [0,1]; no inverse time
simplex parameter appears.

For a fixed polynomial P, four-dimensional Gaussian scaling gives
 int rho^2 P(s rho^2)e^-csrho^2 dk<=C s^-3,
 int rho^4 P(s rho^2)e^-csrho^2 dk<=C s^-4.
Multiply by the bubble prefactor s^2: (6.1) is O(s^-1),
and (6.2) is also O(s^-1). The complementary momentum region
is exponentially small times a polynomial and obeys the same
bound for s>=1.

We have proved
 |partial_p^2(H_ab^M-H_ab^W)(s,0)|<=C/s, s>=1,           (6.3)
for every fixed external component/color choice, uniformly in M.
The stronger matrix row version costs only the fixed external fiber.

7. Conversion to the actual affine heat coefficient

The diagonal color density of exp[-sB_M(a)] is individually gauge
invariant because B_M is gauge covariant. At each fixed s its
Taylor kernels have the moments needed for the already proved
density Ward/reanchoring identity. It has zero linear jet for a
semisimple color factor.

For a normalized Cartan direction and alpha!=nu, let
 h_M(s)=[t^2] tr_E(exp[-sB_M(a(t))])_(0,0),
 a_alpha(t,x)=t x_nu H, other components zero,
where the affine expression is a polynomial Taylor-kernel evaluation.
That identity gives
 h_M(s)= (1/4) partial_(p_nu)^2
                         H_(alpha H,alpha H)^M(s,0).    (7.1)
The factor is fixed by beta=2 times the affine density coefficient
and beta=(1/2)partial_pnu^2 of the total Hessian. On the indicated
diagonal component the midpoint/source-site phases cancel.

The actual endpoint-K affine connection is a_alpha=t x_nu H/K^2.
Translation of a diagonal root changes it by a constant pure gauge.
Thus every diagonal coefficient in a final block is the same,
and summing its K^4 sites cancels the K^-4 from the squared source.
Its block heat coefficient is precisely h_M(s).
This uses neither a finite-torus flux family nor a local cyclic trace.

Combining (6.3),(7.1) proves the promised estimate
                    |h_M(s)-h_W(s)|<=C/s, s>=1.         (7.2)
The scalar ghost base D_a^TD_a is identical for M and W, so the
same comparison holds for the signed vector-minus-two-ghost base
combination without any scalar comparison error.

In particular
 int_1^(K^2) |h_M(s)-h_W(s)| ds/s <= C                  (7.3)
uniformly in M,K. The infinite upper limit is also finite.

8. Consequences, exact remaining inputs, and existence boundary

This proves the previously missing uniform-old-depth comparison for
the TRANSLATION-INVARIANT BASE. It complements, but does not replace,
the separate soft-penalty comparison on physical time 0<u<=1,
the actual short-current-time estimates, the soft infrared tail,
and the bounded coarse determinant corrections.

If those separately audited results are assembled, the actual
Maxwell coefficient differs by O(1), uniformly in M,K, from the
fixed Wilson base contribution on 1<=s<=K^2. A fixed-reference
calculation giving h_W,combined(s)=b_*/2+O(s^-delta), delta>0,
then gives b_* log K+O(1). This note has not evaluated b_* or its
normalization, and does not import it from a different massive,
Legendre or gauge-fixed reference.

The uniformity claim uses the actual classical current-unit
fixed-order kernel bounds in Section1. If only J-dependent quantum
activity bounds, a flat spectral bound, or the linear Ward identity
were available, this proof would not apply. In particular n=3,4
must be controlled by the full nonlinear Ward completion and by
uniform analytic moments, not by a formal pointwise O(p^m)
statement with uncontrolled constants.

No finite-regulator continuous-momentum identity, uniqueness of
joint structural limits, full large-field RG history, running
coupling trajectory, mass gap, or continuum reconstruction follows
from this heat comparison. These remain distinct existence inputs.
