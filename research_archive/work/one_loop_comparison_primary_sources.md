# Primary sources for the next determinant comparison

17 October 2026. Discovery and location note. The actual composite determinant has not yet been identified with either paper's background-field effective action. These citations do not supply our missing comparison bound.

## Lüscher–Weisz: background-field framework

[Background field technique and renormalization in lattice gauge theory](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-lat/9504006), Nucl. Phys. B452 (1995), 213–233, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(95)00346-T).

Read the primary abstract, introduction and early formalism. The paper establishes perturbative renormalization with a background field using BRS, background-gauge and shift symmetries. Sections 5–7 concern the lattice formulation, identities and renormalization; section 8 addresses universality. Section 2 explicitly treats generating functions as formal source and coupling series. Its object is a background-field vertex functional, with a Legendre transform distinguished from the connected generating functional. This is useful for fixing what a comparison must identify; it is not a nonperturbative finite-window theorem.

## Lüscher–Weisz: explicit lattice amplitudes

[Computation of the relation between the bare lattice coupling and the MS coupling in SU(N) gauge theories to two loops](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-lat/9505011), Nucl. Phys. B452 (1995), 234–260, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(95)00338-S).

Read the introduction, strategy and the section-location passages in the primary PDF. Section 4 provides the lattice background-field rules; section 5 computes the one-loop background two-point amplitude. Appendix B collects one-loop lattice integrals, including their small-external-momentum forms. Section 7 gives the coupling matching result. These are promising explicit reference amplitudes. The normalization and a massive/constraint infrared comparison must be checked before importing any coefficient; no numeric coefficient is transcribed from damaged PDF text here.
