STATUS
This note revalidates the published complete-density input, identifies
the still-unmatched interface to our exact homogeneous local macrostep,
and proves a finite cubical gauge lemma plus a regularity obstruction.
The latter are new elementary statements, not attributed to Bałaban.
They explain why a plaquette probability bound is useful but cannot
by itself put full Wilson samples in our physical C1-alpha background
ball. The source theorem does not make that implication either.

2. What current local results actually supply

Our current finite model is an exact homogeneous local integral with
constraint F_R=M_ell^s, R=ell^s; selected direct-R contour gauge;
retained independent-link window; actual Haar and pivot factors;
actual stationary classical action; vacuum factors; and an actual
normalized-total quotient/extraction. Its remainder estimates and
tuned weak trajectories are uniform in preceding homogeneous depth
and permitted finite volume, after R,J and the analytic ball are fixed.

Its finite orbit/gauge insertion is an exact equality on the stated
local sector, with the stipulated continuation of its germ. It has
not yet been identified with every sector of the full compact Wilson
pushforward. Its actual one-loop sign, even once established, closes
the local coupling condition; it does not establish that identification.

The following are concrete proof obligations, not claims that the
published statements are false or that they cannot be adapted.

A. Full operator and partitions.
Define a full compact-field pushforward with exactly F_R, including
all completions outside its chosen local mean/chart domain. Choose
a literal partition of unity whose good-sector term is exactly our
retained-window integral and whose complementary terms are specified.
A one-shot R constraint does not identify its window with the
intersection of s primitive windows. [119](0.1),(1.1)--(1.10)
locates the published full-operator interface.

B. Which averaging class is permitted.
[119] explicitly allows an averaging operation satisfying its
general axioms. Thus using a composite average is not automatically
excluded. Our fixed-R locality, endpoint covariance and selected
mean identities are useful checks. What is still needed is either
an exact verification of every averaging/variational hypothesis used
in the complete-density proof, with its fixed-R constants, or a
direct replacement proof. Merely renaming the paper's L as R does
not check any history-dependent statement.

C. Local backgrounds rather than one global physical chart.
Our current stationary estimates cover globally small physical
potentials and homogeneous constraints. A complete density needs
background minimizers on determining sets with collars, prescribed
exterior data, changing scales and holes. It must retain exterior
holonomies/transition functions rather than assume that all such
backgrounds have a single near-zero periodic potential. The exact
source regularity interface is the p.261 definition listed above.

D. Coefficients and analytic domains.
Our strict connected families have a gauge-invariant total on a
product ball. They do not automatically give separately invariant
coefficient extensions on the source's local orbit/current domains.
In particular the independent current J and multiscale background
slots cannot be supplied by a physical stationary-current theorem.
The saved source_stability_research.md correction remains in force:
do not resurrect its superseded localized-three-map necessity claim;
the deposited active source route uses global carriers. Whichever
route is chosen still needs its actual domain-containment theorem.

E. Boundary and large-field operations.
The present local norm is not a bound on a normalized replacement
on arbitrary bad components and their descendants. Such a replacement
needs the actual numerator/denominator cancellation, its compatibility
with remaining integrations, and a bound summable over component
geometry and scale age. A small exceptional probability is not that
normalized identity. Fixed-box conditional estimates can be a useful
first local input; uniformity as components grow is a separate issue.

F. Couplings must belong to the same sequence.
Our shooting follows our actual extracted finite coefficients.
The source theorem concerns its own RT construction and its own
coupling recursion. Uniformly weak numbers with the same asymptotic
size do not establish equality of the two recursions. A valid import
may instead establish the complete-density hypotheses for our
recursion directly. Then the source's proof, with verified constants,
could be reused. That is a transfer theorem to prove, not an appeal
to the universal expected one-loop coefficient.

G. Finite topology and chart overlap.
An atlas of local gauges must be incorporated into the exact
integration, including transition data and the induced boundary
constraints. The lemma below supplies one local chart. It does not
replace this atlas or its normalized integration rule.

This is a narrower and more useful target than re-proving every
published large-field lemma: first state one exact complete-density
induction class for our F_R and demonstrate its good-sector identity.
Then either map that class into the published one or prove its
boundary/normalized-replacement step. The current local theorem is
available for the good interior of that proposed induction.

3. A finite cubical tree-gauge lemma

Let G be a compact matrix Lie subgroup of U(r), use the operator norm,
and let Lambda_n={0,...,n}^d, d>=2, with all its nearest-neighbor
bonds and elementary plaquettes. Opposite bonds carry inverse matrices.
Assume
          max_p ||U(partial p)-I|| <= epsilon.                 (3.1)

Choose root0 and the coordinate path p(x) which increases coordinate1
first, then coordinate2, ..., coordinate d, from0 to x. These paths
form a rooted spanning tree. Set
          v(x)=U(p(x)),   v(0)=I,
          U^v(x,y)=v(x)U(x,y)v(y)^(-1).                       (3.2)
Then each tree bond is exactly I, and
          ||U^v(x,x+e_mu)-I||
              <= (sum_(nu>mu) x_nu) epsilon
              <= (d-1)n epsilon.                            (3.3)
This is a real compact-group statement; no commutativity is assumed.

Proof. U^v on that bond is the Wilson product around
p(x), the extra mu-edge, and reverse p(x+e_mu). Move the extra
mu-edge backwards across the later coordinate segments, one elementary
square at a time. Exactly sum_(nu>mu)x_nu adjacent edge exchanges
are required. The resulting boundary word cancels completely.
Each exchange multiplies the loop product by a conjugate of an
elementary plaquette or its inverse. Conjugation and inversion
preserve its distance to I. For unitary W_l,
          ||product_l W_l-I|| <= sum_l ||W_l-I||,             (3.4)
by telescoping; hence (3.3). Tree edges correspond to no exchanges.

Choose a fixed exponential-chart radius delta_G>0, at most1, so the
principal matrix logarithm of a group element within delta_G of I
lies in the Lie algebra chart. If (d-1)n epsilon<delta_G, all
transformed bonds have unique such logarithms. For physical mesh eta,
write U^v_b=exp(i eta A_b). The spectral estimate
|theta|<=2|exp(i theta)-1| on this neighborhood gives
          ||A||_infinity
               <= 2(d-1)n epsilon/eta
               = 2(d-1) ell epsilon/eta^2,
          ell=n eta.                                      (3.5)
The same construction is polynomial in the original link matrices
and their inverses before taking the local logarithm. It is therefore
real analytic, and has a holomorphic continuation on a neighborhood
of each compact configuration satisfying a strict chart margin.
No volume-independent complex neighborhood or derivative bound is
claimed merely from (3.3).

Consequences and exact scope:
* At a fixed finite current box, sufficiently small plaquettes put
  every orbit into a controlled local link-logarithm chart.
* On a physical box of fixed side ell as eta decreases, the
  sufficient potential bound is epsilon=O(eta^2 b/ell).
  An unscaled small number epsilon(g) alone is insufficient.
* This is a cubical filling estimate. On a general simply connected
  lattice complex the same proof uses the filling area of each
  tree fundamental loop. Simple connectivity alone gives no
  uniform bound on those areas.
* The lemma bounds the potential, not all its physical derivatives,
  not the retained relative fluctuation around a prescribed minimum,
  and not a normalized integral on an averaging fiber.

4. Plaquette smallness does not imply a uniform physical C1-alpha gauge

Here is a counterexample even with epsilon=eta^2 f, fixed arbitrarily
small f>0, on a fixed physical cube. Choose a nonzero Cartan generator
H with ||H||=1, and chi(k)=0 for even k,1 for odd k. Put
          U_1(x)=exp(i eta^2 f x_2 chi(x_1) H),
          U_mu(x)=I for mu!=1.                             (4.1)
All plaquettes other than the 12-plaquettes are I. With the
standard positive orientation,
          U(partial p_12(x))
                 =exp(-i eta^2 f chi(x_1) H).               (4.2)
Thus (3.1) holds with epsilon=eta^2 f. Adjacent 12-plaquettes
alternate between I and a matrix whose distance from I divided
by eta^2 is at least f/2 for sufficiently small eta.

Claim. Fix alpha in(0,1) and B<infinity. For sufficiently small eta,
there is NO gauge transformation putting (4.1) into
U^v=exp(i eta A) with
  ||A||infinity + ||nabla_eta A||infinity
                 + [nabla_eta A]_alpha <= B               (4.3)
on the whole cube (or on an interior subcube containing these
neighboring plaquettes). This is an obstruction to every gauge,
not just to the coordinate tree gauge.

Proof of the necessary regularity bound. For any A satisfying
(4.3), the plaquette at x can be written exactly in the local
jet variables
  a=A_mu(x), b=A_nu(x),
  c=nabla_mu A_nu(x), e=nabla_nu A_mu(x)
as
  P_eta=exp(i eta a) exp(i eta b+i eta^2 c)
                   exp(-i eta a-i eta^2 e) exp(-i eta b).
Its constant term is I and its coefficient linear in eta vanishes.
Hence (P_eta-I)/eta^2 extends analytically to eta=0, jointly in
(a,b,c,e), with uniformly bounded first jet derivatives on each
fixed bounded jet ball and 0<=eta<=eta0(B). For adjacent plaquettes,
the a,b differences are O(B eta), while c,e differences are
O(B eta^alpha). Therefore
  ||(P_eta(x)-I)/eta^2-(P_eta(x+e_r)-I)/eta^2||
                       <= C(B) eta^alpha.                  (4.4)
Taking norms cannot increase their difference. Under arbitrary gauge
transformation P_eta(x) is conjugated at x, so
eta^-2||P_eta(x)-I|| is pointwise gauge invariant. The adjacent
values in (4.2) differ by at least f/2, contradicting (4.4).

In the explicit tree gauge of Section3 the example is already fixed:
the 1-direction tree edges at x_2=0 and all other tree edges are I.
Its A_1=eta f x_2 chi(x_1)H has a first derivative of size
approximately f ell/eta near the far side of the cube. This is a
visible failure of that gauge; (4.4) proves the stronger impossibility
of uniformly C1-alpha coordinates in any gauge.

This counterexample does not contradict the complete-density source
theorem. A raw sampled field is not supposed to be a uniformly smooth
classical background at every fine mesh. The useful decomposition is
a regular constrained/minimizing background PLUS rough fluctuations
and explicitly handled exceptional regions. A local tree gauge alone
does not construct that decomposition or bound its normalized weights.

5. Flat torus holonomies are a different obstruction

On a periodic direction with N bonds and physical circumference
ell=N eta, take
        U_1(x)=exp(i theta H/N),  U_mu(x)=I (mu!=1).          (5.1)
Every plaquette is exactly I. The Wilson loop around that direction
is exp(i theta H), invariant up to conjugacy under periodic gauge.
If a periodic gauge has U^v_b=exp(i eta A_b) with ||A||infinity<=b,
the elementary unitary-product inequality gives
        ||exp(i theta H)-I|| <= N eta b = ell b.             (5.2)
For a nontrivial holonomy, arbitrarily small global physical
potential bounds cannot follow from zero plaquette curvature.
On each contractible cube, Section3 sets the flat field to I.
The local trivializations are joined by transition functions carrying
the nontrivial global data.

For two local gauges v_i,v_j the exact overlap relation is
        U^(i)(x,y)=h_ij(x) U^(j)(x,y) h_ij(y)^(-1),
        h_ij=v_i v_j^(-1).                                 (5.3)
An integration scheme may retain this transition/exterior data or
integrate it with the correct compact measure. It may not set it to
identity because the local plaquettes are small. Likewise a bad-region
complement need not be simply connected. This is why an atlas and
determining-set formulation can be natural even though the underlying
unconstrained finite lattice bundle was written with global links.

6. One useful scale-comparison fact from the actual local trajectory

Our weak finite trajectory has gamma_j=g_j^-2>=Gamma_w and
|gamma_(j+1)-gamma_j|<=D_abs. Therefore, for any two of its stages,
        gamma_i/gamma_j
             <=1+(D_abs/Gamma_w)|i-j|,
        g_i/g_j
             <=sqrt(1+(D_abs/Gamma_w)|i-j|).                 (6.1)
The reverse ratio obeys the same bound by interchanging i,j.
Proof: telescope the bounded increments and divide by the positive
lower bound. This does not require monotonicity at single steps.

Thus the new trajectory supplies some polynomial interscale
comparability, not only containment in a weak interval. It may help
with mixed-history radius arithmetic. It is not asserted to be
identical to every source inequality (2.6)--(2.9), whose precise
constants and domains still require matching.

7. Concrete H2 next theorem, separating two routes

Import route:
Specify a source-compatible full pushforward and its normalized
large-field replacement. Verify the actual Section2 induction class
on every admissible determining set, including boundary/current
analytic domains, using our homogeneous interior as an input.
Prove that its coupling extraction is our tuned one, or tune its
own sequence with the now available actual-coefficient method.
Only then invoke [122II] Theorem1 for the complete density.

Direct route:
For our chosen full compact operator, build a gauge-invariant
good/bad partition with exact summation to1. Prove a good-component
identity with the actual constrained background and retained window;
give a normalized bad-component replacement that is uniform in its
exterior data and summable in size, scale age and boundary geometry.
The tree gauge and a fixed-box conditional Laplace estimate are
concrete first lemmas. Neither supplies arbitrary-component or
history-uniform normalization. Whole-lattice RP/chessboard bounds
for bad plaquettes can provide probabilities, but do not identify
the retained relative-field restrictions or the analytic domains.

In both routes, there is a specific new obligation after a local
probability estimate: match a normalized conditional measure with
the stationary-plus-fluctuation coordinates and show that the
remaining sectors are accounted for without an uncontrolled
denominator or boundary factor. This is the theorem-level H2
interface. No conclusion here claims continuum existence.
