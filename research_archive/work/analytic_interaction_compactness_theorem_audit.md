## Original radius and convergence of total interactions

For each fixed finite carrier, bounded finite-dimensional holomorphic coefficients have a locally uniform subsequential limit. Countability of carriers and their finite scalar Taylor indices permits one diagonal subsequence, including all translates. For a finite selection of carriers, Fatou at radius r<b gives the weighted bound; increasing r to b and then exhausting the carriers preserves exactly the original open-b supremum norm. This does not assert uniform convergence all the way to the boundary, and no such assertion is needed.

The large-carrier tail meeting a compact field support is bounded by 2 |K| epsilon exp(-aQ). Every bounded-size connected torus carrier meeting that central support has a unique central lift once the volume is sufficiently large. Thus the same truncation handles winding carriers. It proves locally uniform convergence of the vacuum-normalized scalar totals on every fixed finite-coordinate polydisc. Their derivatives are therefore derivatives of one limiting analytic interaction, not unrelated kernel subsequences.

Translation, point-group and reality identities pass on the transformed compact fields. A compact gauge changes only a finite edge neighborhood; requiring both arguments to remain in a smaller product ball makes the gauge identity pass. Constant conjugation also preserves compact field support. The claims correctly concern totals rather than individual coefficients.

## Coherent complete-field families

At fixed endpoint L, a fixed collar has finitely many current coordinates. Complete C1alpha differences and Hölder quotients there are finite collections with fixed physical denominators. Their norms are lower semicontinuous under local uniform coefficient convergence. Overlap identities for the same global support label pass, and Fatou preserves the full rooted/cardinality bound.

The tails in either rooted tree length or support cardinality are uniformly small. The remaining labels form a finite set, since a bounded rooted tree keeps every support point in a finite ball. This proves local convergence of the complete fields. The uniform bounded holomorphic coordinate maps and their uniform Cauchy remainders also give the asserted Banach-valued analyticity of the infinite support sum.

For compact final support K, every nonzero coefficient has X meeting K. The rooted tree weight then gives exp(-kappa dist(i,K)) decay. On centered periodic representatives this yields uniform summable tails: torus distance from i to K is at least the centered distance from i to the origin minus the fixed radius of K. Fixed L adds only its fixed number of output coordinates per unit cell.

## Summable fields and substitution

Coordinate Cauchy on an interior rho-ball gives a bound by ||E_H||_b/(b-rho) times the sum of coordinate differences. Summing carriers at each coordinate yields the stated ell1 bound and local Lipschitz estimate without a volume factor. Thus truncating a_N(w) outside a large fixed region creates a uniformly small scalar-interaction error. On the region, local convergence of both maps and coefficients applies. The two truncations establish the actual scalar composition limit (4.2).

A useful explicit justification for finite compositions is available directly from (3.1). Coordinate Cauchy gives, on a strict interior ball,

    ||D_j a_i|| <= C/(b-rho) exp(-kappa dist(i,j)),

with only the fixed cell and output-fiber dimensions. Indeed only labels X containing j contribute, and their rooted tree length bounds dist(i,j). Summing i yields an ell1-to-ell1 Lipschitz constant. Hence decaying intermediate fields, not merely compact ones, can be truncated before applying the next map. This makes the final finite-composition claim precise under its stated common-interior and joint-extraction assumptions.

## Scope

The proof gives subsequential interactions and coherent maps at fixed parameters, and it permits identities to pass when the required finite collection is extracted jointly. Separate arbitrary subsequences need not compose. It does not establish uniqueness, quantum-update continuity for all input families, consistent infinite iteration, Gibbs measure convergence, or a continuum field theory.
