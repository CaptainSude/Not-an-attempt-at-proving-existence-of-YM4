# Two-frame placement on the original odd-L regulator

16 October 2026. This note proves the lattice arithmetic, placement, and counting needed by the amplification proposal. It does not by itself prove a Wilson slab trace formula, Schatten folding inequality, or same-limit reflection positivity.

## 1. Result and comparison with QED3

Keep the original coordinate tori

\[
 C_n=\mathbb R^4/n\mathbb Z^4,\qquad n=2L^M,\quad L\ge3
 \text{ odd}.
\]

There is no subsequence on which 4 divides n. Nevertheless, the two-frame placement can be carried out by choosing odd diagonal cell widths and half-integer coordinate cell widths. Coordinate walls then alternate between site and link planes. All necessary numbers of reflected slabs remain even.

The construction uses the same auxiliary period lattices as the QED3 fold, with one extra transverse direction:

\[
 a=n/2=L^M,\qquad
 \Gamma_D=\langle a e_-,\,2a e_+,\,n e_3,\,n e_4\rangle,
 \quad e_-=e_1-e_2,\ e_+=e_1+e_2,                       \tag{1}
\]

and, after coordinate straightening,

\[
 \Gamma_T=\langle a e_1,\,4a e_2,\,n e_3,\,n e_4\rangle. \tag{2}
\]

Both have covolume n^4. The initial regulator C_n is not replaced. D_n and T_n are auxiliary fold geometries at fixed bare coupling; no ultraviolet RG estimate on a changed coordinate-torus family is inferred.

The relevant original formulas are QED3, Section 3.3, Proposition 3.2 and Theorem 3.3, printed pages 23--25, especially (3.20)--(3.27); and Section 4.4, printed pages 38--40, especially (4.17)--(4.22). The original convenient choice has integral coordinate cell width and 4h dividing n. The construction below replaces that particular arithmetic choice, not its analytic proof.

## 2. Lattice and scale conventions

Work first on the unit lattice Z^4. A coordinate reflection at c belongs to the lattice symmetry group precisely when c is a half-integer. It is a site reflection when c is integral and a link reflection when c is strictly half-integral. A diagonal reflection in x_1-x_2=c or x_1+x_2=c preserves the lattice for integral c.

The Yang--Mills bare convention a_bare(Z+1/2)^4 is equivalent after translation and rescaling. Such a translation changes the plus-diagonal offset by an integer and leaves the minus-diagonal offset integral. The names site/link may interchange, but the alternating-wall construction and all period arithmetic are unchanged.

Fix a source box radius R_O, including whatever fixed link/plaquette enlargement is needed to read the full support of the source O. Choose

\[
 h=L^u>16R_O+16.                                        \tag{3}
\]

The original diagonal cells have common height width h, with offsets c_-,c_+ in Z and c_3,c_4 in (1/2)Z. Let V_n be the array of reflected copies of O in these cells. The support of O is assumed to lie strictly inside its original cell.

For an operator insertion supported in a coordinate box B=y+[-r,r]^4, choose two more fixed powers of L,

\[
 q=L^v,\quad b=qh,\quad w=b/2,
 \qquad
 H=L^z,\quad b\mid H,
\]

such that

\[
 w\ge2r+6h,\qquad H/w\ge16.                              \tag{4}
\]

These choices exist: first enlarge q, then enlarge H. Since b and H are odd, w is strictly half-integral and H/w is an even integer. The scales depend on O and r but not on M or the center y. For every sufficiently large M,

\[
 h\mid b\mid H\mid a.                                   \tag{5}
\]

Write k=H/h and A=a/b. Both are odd integers.

## 3. The first fold and its fundamental domains

In diagonal height coordinates

\[
 u=x_1-x_2,\qquad v=x_1+x_2,\qquad x_3,\ x_4,
\]

the original periods n e_1,n e_2 have first two height vectors (n,n),(-n,n). Reducing u modulo n leaves v modulo 2n. Thus the cell-index brick

\[
 0\le j_-<n/h,\quad0\le j_+<2n/h,\quad
 0\le j_3,j_4<n/h                                       \tag{6}
\]

is a fundamental set of diagonal cells for C_n.

Straightening in the u direction preserves the cross-section generated by n e_+, n e_3,n e_4 and replaces the closing vector by (n/2)e_-=a e_-. The next v straightening leaves the geometric periods unchanged. This gives precisely (1). The brick (6) also represents D_n.

Every height count is even:

\[
 (n/h,\,2n/h,\,n/h,\,n/h)
   =(2a/h,\,4a/h,\,2a/h,\,2a/h).                       \tag{7}
\]

All straight closing vectors are lattice vectors. The reflections and reflected copies have consistent parity under the periods: in the diagonal wall group the translation subgroup is generated by h e_-, h e_+, 2h e_3,2h e_4, and both original and adapted period lattices lie in this subgroup.

The diagonal cell volume is h^4/2, since the diagonal height map has determinant two. Therefore the source-copy count is

\[
 N_n=2n^4/h^4.                                         \tag{8}
\]

The same statements hold with h replaced by H, giving the coarse-cell count

\[
 N'_n=2n^4/H^4,\qquad N_n/N'_n=k^4.                    \tag{9}
\]

These are geometric identities. The corresponding partition-function inequalities still require the normalized slab representation.

## 4. Source residues and two clean link planes

Under the original diagonal wall group, the centers of reflected copies of O have at most four residues modulo h in each of coordinates 1 and 2, and at most two in each transverse coordinate.

To verify this, if the original center is x and

\[
 A_-=x_1-x_2-c_-,\quad A_+=x_1+x_2-c_+,
\]

its first two image coordinates are

\[
 \begin{split}
 x_1'&=x_1-A_-\epsilon_- -A_+\epsilon_+
                  +h(z_-+z_+),\\
 x_2'&=x_2+A_-\epsilon_- -A_+\epsilon_+
                  +h(z_+-z_-),
 \end{split}                                          \tag{10}
\]

where epsilon_-,epsilon_+ belong to {0,1} and z_-,z_+ are integers. In a transverse direction the image is

\[
 x_\mu'=x_\mu-2(x_\mu-c_\mu)\epsilon_\mu+2h z_\mu.
\]

These formulas prove the stated residue counts. Signed coordinate permutations preserve an axis-aligned box radius.

For clean planes c,c+1 with c in Z+1/2, a source-center residue t forbids c if either plane meets [t-R_O,t+R_O] modulo h. The two forbidden intervals combine into an interval of length 2R_O+1. Among the h half-integer classes modulo odd h, it excludes at most 2R_O+2 classes. Four center residues therefore exclude at most 8R_O+8 classes, fewer than h by (3). Consequently two adjacent clean link planes exist in every coordinate direction. If strict extra margins are desired, incorporate them in R_O before (3).

This assertion concerns the full source boxes, not just their centers. Every source copy is wholly on one side of each clean plane. The two reflections generate translation by 2e_mu. This is only the geometry required by the amplification proposal; reflection invariance of a limiting tilted state remains an analytic conclusion to be proved.

## 5. Coarse diagonal images of the insertion box

Choose coarse lower offsets

\[
 c'_\pm\in c_\pm+h\mathbb Z,\qquad
 c'_\mu\in c_\mu+h\mathbb Z\quad(\mu=3,4)
\]

so that

\[
 \begin{split}
 (y_1-y_2)-c'_-&\in[2w,2w+h),\\
 (y_1+y_2)-c'_+&\in[4w,4w+h),\\
 y_\mu-c'_\mu&\in[w,w+h),\qquad \mu=3,4.
 \end{split}                                          \tag{11}
\]

Every interval has length h, so these choices always exist. By (4), B is strictly inside the coarse cell of height width H. Every coarse wall belongs to the original h-wall family because H/h is integral. Hence its reflection preserves V_n.

The coarse wall group has elements

\[
 g=\tau_{H(z_-e_-+z_+e_++2z_3e_3+2z_4e_4)}
             \theta_-^{\epsilon_-}\theta_+^{\epsilon_+}
             \theta_3^{\epsilon_3}\theta_4^{\epsilon_4}.
\]

Put m=H/w, an even integer at least 16. Expanding (11) gives

\[
 gy=y+w\kappa(g)+s(\epsilon),\qquad |s(\epsilon)|_\infty<2h,
                                                               \tag{12}
\]

with the integer index

\[
 \begin{split}
 \kappa(g)
 ={}&(-2\epsilon_- -4\epsilon_+,\ 
       2\epsilon_- -4\epsilon_+,\ 
       -2\epsilon_3,\ -2\epsilon_4)\\
 &+m(z_-+z_+,\ z_+-z_-,\ 2z_3,\ 2z_4).
 \end{split}                                                   \tag{13}
\]

The map g -> kappa(g) is injective. The sixteen parity vectors in the first line are distinct, and any coordinate of their difference has absolute value at most six. In an equality of two indices, the translation contribution is a multiple of m>=16 in each coordinate, so it must vanish. The parity vectors then agree; the linear equations in the second line force every z difference to vanish.

## 6. Alternating coordinate walls avoiding all source boxes

Choose coordinate cells of side w with offsets o_mu in (1/2)Z. Because 2w=b is an odd integer, their walls alternate between site and link planes. Every wall is a valid lattice reflection.

Modulo h, the wall residues alternate between o_mu and o_mu+h/2. Equivalently, avoiding all walls is the problem of choosing o_mu modulo h/2 while avoiding the projected source-center residues. There are exactly h half-integer classes on that circle, because its circumference is h/2 and their spacing is 1/2.

A source box of radius R_O excludes at most 4R_O+2 of these classes: an interval of length 2R_O on a half-integer mesh has at most 4R_O+1 points, and the extra one is a safe endpoint allowance. With four residues, at most 16R_O+8 classes are forbidden. Inequality (3) therefore leaves an allowed class. The transverse directions have fewer forbidden classes.

For each coordinate choose an allowed representative in

\[
 I_\mu=(y_\mu+r+2h-w,\ y_\mu-r-2h).                    \tag{14}
\]

Its length is w-2r-4h>=2h, more than the residue period h/2, so every class has a representative in it. Equations (12) and (14) show

\[
 gB\subset o+w\kappa(g)+(0,w)^4                         \tag{15}
\]

strictly, for every coarse group element g. Thus every coarse image of the insertion box lies in a coordinate cell, and distinct group elements give distinct coordinate-cell indices.

By construction every reflected source box also lies inside one coordinate cell: no coordinate wall intersects any of its coordinate projections. Hence the second fold never cuts a source term into pieces across a wall.

The avoidance uses the source-support radius R_O; it does not require enlarging h when r, the insertion-box radius, increases. Only w and H increase with r.

## 7. Exact second-frame fundamental domain and counts

The first coordinate height period of Gamma_D is a. Its cross-section at x_1=0 is

\[
 \Gamma_D\cap\{x_1=0\}
       =\langle4a e_2,n e_3,n e_4\rangle.                \tag{16}
\]

Indeed, a first-two-coordinate period is

\[
 a u(1,-1)+2a v(1,1).
\]

Its first component vanishes iff u=-2v, in which case its second component is 4av. Straightening in x_1 therefore gives exactly Gamma_T in (2), not a guessed rectangular comparison torus.

All periods of both lattices belong to the coordinate wall group's translation subgroup b Z^4, because b divides a. Thus they preserve the orientation/conjugation parity of reflected copies.

In units of the coordinate cell width w=b/2, Gamma_D has index-period generators

\[
 (2A,-2A,0,0),\quad(4A,4A,0,0),\quad
 (0,0,4A,0),\quad(0,0,0,4A),\qquad A=a/b.              \tag{17}
\]

The brick

\[
 0\le j_1<2A,\quad0\le j_2<8A,\quad
 0\le j_3,j_4<4A                                      \tag{18}
\]

represents both Gamma_D and Gamma_T. For Gamma_D, first reduce j_1 using the first generator in (17), which may shift j_2. Then reduce j_2 using the second generator minus twice the first, namely (0,8A,0,0). This is also a uniqueness algorithm, since a period with zero first component has second component a multiple of 8A. The transverse reductions are independent.

The numbers of consecutive coordinate slabs are

\[
 (a/w,\ 4a/w,\ n/w,\ n/w)=(2A,8A,4A,4A),              \tag{19}
\]

all even. The coordinate-cell count is therefore

\[
 N''_n=(2A)(8A)(4A)^2=256A^4=n^4/w^4.                 \tag{20}
\]

There are exactly N'_n distinct coordinate cells containing coarse insertion boxes, modulo the torus periods. To prove no collisions were lost on quotienting, suppose

\[
 \kappa(g_1)-\kappa(g_2)=\gamma/w,\qquad\gamma\in\Gamma_D.
\]

Every gamma is a translation in the coarse diagonal wall group, because H divides a. Its action changes kappa(g_2) by gamma/w and leaves its parity bits unchanged. Injectivity of (13) then forces g_1=tau_gamma g_2. These are the same coarse-cell orbit on D_n. Conversely, such period translations give precisely the same coordinate-cell orbit. This proves the exact count.

All shortest periods grow with a, while r,h,w,H are fixed. Hence the support boxes and any fixed enlargement embed for all sufficiently large M.

## 8. Operator cuts after the coordinate fold

Suppose the original insertion crosses a clean link plane c and is supported in B. Every coarse diagonal image has a coordinate crossing direction, possibly exchanging directions 1 and 2. It still crosses a link plane: an integer-offset diagonal reflection maps half-integer coordinate levels to half-integer levels. It remains clean because the coarse reflections preserve V_n.

After the second fold, each folded factor originates from a single coordinate cell, so all copies of its insertion have the same time direction. Coordinate reflections at site or link walls preserve the link-plane type of the insertion cut.

If d is the location of that cut relative to the lower coordinate-cell wall, then 0<d<w and d belongs to (1/2)Z. In successive cells its cut positions are

\[
 o+d,\quad o+2w-d,\quad o+2w+d,\quad o+4w-d,\ldots
\]

The gaps alternate between

\[
 2(w-d),\qquad 2d,                                    \tag{21}
\]

both positive integers. For general coarse images, the residual shift s(epsilon) in (12) consumes the 2h reserve in (14), so only strict positive distance from the coordinate walls has been proved. This is sufficient: d belongs to (1/2)Z and 0<d<w, hence each integer gap lies in the fixed set {1,...,2w-1}, uniformly in the insertion center. Their sum is 2w=b, an odd integer, so one gap is even and the other odd. There is one cut per time cell, and the total number J of cuts is one of the even integers in (19).

Thus the amplification proposal can choose the Schatten exponent J even. The reflected-slab comparison torus associated to a gap l_j has time length J l_j, an integer tending to infinity. Its aspect ratio is bounded above and below by constants depending on the fixed insertion geometry: l_j/w lies in a fixed compact subset of (0,infinity). No divisibility by four is required for this pressure comparison.

Because each source box belongs to one coordinate cell and none crosses the original clean insertion plane, its coordinate-fold copies cross none of the corresponding insertion planes. Hence the sources can be assigned wholly to the slabs between operator cuts.

At one cut, insertion boxes in distinct spatial cells are disjoint. This is the geometric support condition for placing local operators on disjoint spatial link sets. Any physical-Hilbert-space projection and resulting norm estimate remain part of the transfer construction.

There is also an exact source-count identity useful for that norm estimate. Let m_c be the number of original source copies assigned to coordinate cell c. Since no source box crosses a wall,

\[
 \sum_{c\bmod\Gamma_D}m_c=N_n.
\]

The full coordinate fold of that cell has exactly N''_n m_c source copies. If its transfer estimate bounds these bounded source factors by exp(|t|N''_n m_c||O||_infinity), the chessboard exponent 1/N''_n and the product over cells leave exp(|t|N_n||O||_infinity). Exactly N'_n cells carry an insertion. Combining the lower denominator bound with the N'_n-th amplification root therefore gives the source factor exp(2|t|k^4||O||_infinity). This is conditional source-cost bookkeeping, not a proof of the transfer estimate. The possible integer gap lengths belong to a finite set depending on w, so fixed-coupling pressure convergence can be used uniformly over these auxiliary rectangular geometries without claiming a quantitative finite-size rate.

## 9. The remaining analytic interfaces

The arithmetic above is sufficient for the following conditional replacement of QED3's placement step:

* The first fold uses integer diagonal widths h,H, even height counts, and the unchanged original regulator C_n.
* The second fold uses half-integer coordinate width w, alternating boundary types, the explicit fundamental domain (18), and even counts (19).
* Distinct coarse insertion boxes remain distinct after quotienting; sources cross neither the second-frame walls nor the operator cuts.
* The numbers of source, amplified, and coordinate cells are exactly (8), (9), and (20).

To turn these facts into partition-function inequalities one still needs the normalized Wilson slab representation, including diagonal boundary feature spaces and gauge projection.

For the coordinate mixed-type step, a compatible target is the half-step factorization

\[
 B=V^{1/2}C^{1/2},\qquad A_{\rm link}=B^*B,\quad
 A_{\rm site}=BB^*,
\]

on the appropriate physical Hilbert spaces: a link-to-site half-step is B and the reverse is B*. A slab of half-integer length may therefore have unlike boundary types. For an even number p of reflected copies, its adjoint pair gluing should give the ordinary Wilson partition function of integer time length p w. This statement requires exact gauge-projector and normalization checks; the arithmetic does not prove it. In particular, positivity of the integral kernel of C^{1/2} is not being assumed. On the short coordinate period a, which is odd, a reflection's two antipodal mirrors are of opposite site/link types. Their mixed-boundary positive gluing, rather than only the corresponding even-period statement, is part of this same check.

QED3's Proposition 3.2 already formulates slabs between potentially different boundary Hilbert spaces and uses even Schatten exponents. Its Section 3.3 subsequently restricts to an integer cell width for convenience. The extension needed here is thus consistent with the abstract slab identities, but those identities must be established for pure Wilson fields rather than imported as a fermionic theorem.

Additional obligations remain the diagonal Wilson slab fold, source-weight insertion estimates, and the outer-layer/clean-plane amplification argument of the companion note. Even after these are proved, the conclusion is same-limit lattice diagonal RP; continuum rotations and nontriviality are separate questions.

## 10. A numerical arithmetic example

Take L=3, h=81, q=27, b=2187, w=1093.5, H=19683. Then H/w=18. Conditions (3)--(4) hold, for example, for R_O=3 and r=10. For every M>=9, H divides a=3^M.

At M=9 one has N'_n=32 coarse diagonal cells and A=a/b=9. The coordinate cut counts are (18,72,36,36), all even, and N''_n=256*9^4. Increasing M multiplies every linear count by an odd power of three and preserves all the parity and divisibility assertions.

This example is only an arithmetic check; the proof above works for every fixed odd integer L>=3 by choosing sufficiently large powers.
