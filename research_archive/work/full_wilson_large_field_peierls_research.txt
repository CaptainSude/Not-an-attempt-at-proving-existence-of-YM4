FULL WILSON PEIERLS BOUNDS FROM SITE-PLANE CHESSBOARD ESTIMATES
17 October 2026. Rotation audit research. Independent audit requested.
This concerns the actual positive normalized Wilson measure, not a
Gaussian approximation. No repository or output file was modified.

MAIN PROVED RESULT. Fix a cell side b>=3. On a periodic unit lattice
with side lengths n_i=2m_i b, the probability that any prescribed
m distinct cells each contain an interior plaquette with
1-Re tau(U_p)>=delta^2 is at most z_b(g,delta)^m, where
 z_b(g,delta)
 =min{1, exp[-delta^2/g^2
          +b^d(d dim(G) log(1/g)+C_(G,d))]}.             (0.1)
The constants are uniform in the torus volume and in the selected
cells. The actual source family n=2L^M, L odd, admits this geometry
with b=L^u for every fixed u>=1 and M>=u.

A finite shifted-grid/coloring argument also controls every prescribed
set of distinct bad plaquettes, with a fixed exponent loss depending
on b,d. Connected clusters consequently have Peierls tails when z_b
is small. These statements use the full normalized Wilson measure.

WHAT IS NOT PROVED. These curvature events have not been identified
with complements of the actual retained-coordinate windows about
a nonlinear constrained minimizer. Raw link windows cannot have
this property: every single-link marginal of the unfixed Wilson
measure is exactly Haar, independent of g. The theorem does not
supply a full large-field RG expansion or its analytic source bounds.

1. Measure, exact energy, and regulator geometry

Let G=SU(N), m_G=dim G, tau=Tr/N, and let all independent links carry
normalized Haar measure. The same proof works for a compact connected
matrix group with the analogous faithful trace norm and local Haar
ball bound. Work on
 Lambda=product_i Z/n_i Z,    n_i=2m_i b, m_i>=1, b>=3.
Let V=|Lambda|, E=dV, P=binom(d,2)V, with elementary unoriented
plaquettes counted once. The side lengths are at least six, so there
are no period-one or period-two graph degeneracies.

For a group matrix W define
 h(W)=1-Re tau(W)=(1/2)||W-I||_tau^2>=0,
 ||X||_tau^2=tau(X^*X).
The action, partition function and probability are
 A(U)=sum_p h(U_p),
 Z_g=integral exp[-A(U)/g^2] product_e dU_e,
 mu_g(dU)=Z_g^-1 exp[-A(U)/g^2] product_e dU_e.            (1.1)
This is the actual real positive Wilson measure, with no gauge fixing.

Fix a translated grid of cubes of side b with vertices at
s+b j, j_i in Z/(2m_i). In each cell use only observables whose link
support is strictly inside the geometric cube: every endpoint of
a used link has all coordinates strictly between the corresponding
two walls. Let P_j^int consist of elementary plaquettes all of whose
vertices have this property. This set is nonempty for b>=3.
The basic bad event is
 B_j(delta)={max_(p in P_j^int) h(U_p)>=delta^2}.          (1.2)

All coordinate walls are SITE planes with integer offsets. Their
reflections preserve the lattice. The number 2m_i of cells in every
direction is even. These are the only reflection/period requirements
used below. No diagonal reflection or alternating site/link scheme
is needed. For n=2L^M and b=L^u, every count is 2L^(M-u), which is
even although L is odd. The proof works at the original regulator.

2. Exact site-plane reflection and slab kernels

Here is a direct proof specialized to the needed positive Wilson
case; it avoids assuming a chessboard theorem for an unverified
boundary convention.

A coordinate site plane never cuts the relative interior of an
elementary link or plaquette across both sides. A plaquette either
lies in the plane or belongs wholly to one of the adjacent closed
half-spaces, perhaps touching the plane. On a torus a reflection
uses two opposite site planes. Conditional on links lying in these
planes, the two open half configurations are independent copies
with reflected weights. Consequently the reflected quadratic form
is an integral of an absolute square with a nonnegative boundary
weight. This proves the required site reflection positivity.

For the full arbitrary-even-count chessboard inequality, cut in one
coordinate into p=2m slabs of width b. Boundary variables are the
tangential links lying in one site plane. Use their product Haar
space H=L^2(boundary links). All boundaries are canonically identified
by integer translation in the cut direction.

For a bounded slab observable f supported strictly inside the slab,
define K_f(U_left,U_right) by integrating all remaining slab links,
with:
 - the full Wilson factor for every plaquette assigned to the slab,
   including those touching a boundary but not lying in it;
 - one half of the action of every plaquette entirely on each boundary;
 - the factor f.
Each link not in a boundary is integrated once in this kernel.
Every plaquette has exactly one of the indicated assignments. Since
the domain is compact and |f| is bounded, K_f is a bounded integral
kernel, hence Hilbert--Schmidt.

Gluing p kernels with the boundary Haar integrations gives exactly
 Tr(K_(f_0)...K_(f_(p-1)))
  =Z_g[product_j f_j].                                  (2.1)
No gauge-volume factor appears. Each boundary link is integrated once,
each boundary plaquette receives two halves, and every other plaquette
and link is assigned once. Reflection interchanges left and right,
preserves h(U_p), and inverts a link if its chosen orientation reverses.
Haar invariance under inversion gives
 K_f^*=K_(theta conjugate(f)).                           (2.2)
Our indicators are real.

Schatten Hölder for the even integer p gives
 |Tr product_j K_(f_j)|
 <= product_j (Tr(K_(f_j)^* K_(f_j))^(p/2))^(1/p).        (2.3)
The right member for a fixed j is the partition integral of p
alternating reflected copies of that slab. This is an exact
ordinary Wilson torus with the original period pb, not a different
action. Odd p would not give this direct alternating gluing.

Apply (2.3) successively in all d coordinate directions.
At each stage, a slab source is the product of the sources in its
constituent cells. Orthogonal affine coordinate reflections commute
on the underlying unoriented graph, with the corresponding link
orientation inversions. Thus the final factor indexed by an original
cell is the full reflected dissemination of that cell's observable.
This sequential proof handles intersections of cut planes by exact
one-axis graph gluings; no independent corner normalization is inserted.

Let N_c=V/b^d be the number of cells, and let Theta_j map the reference
cell to cell j by the usual alternating reflections. Even cell counts
make this pattern periodic. For bounded cell observables f_j define
 D(f_j)=product_i Theta_i f_j, where the original cell observable is
first pulled back to the reference cell. The result is
 |Z_g[product_j Theta_j f_j]|
 <=product_j Z_g[D(f_j)]^(1/N_c).                        (2.4)
For real indicators all terms are nonnegative.

After dividing by Z_g, factors with f_j=1 are precisely Z_g^(1/N_c).
Therefore, for a selected set S of cells,
 mu_g(product_(j in S) Theta_j f_j)
 <=product_(j in S) (Z_g[D(f_j)]/Z_g)^(1/N_c).            (2.5)
This exact normalization is important: the denominator is the full
Wilson partition function, not an independently chosen small-field
partition function.

3. A volume-uniform lower bound for the full partition function

There are constants epsilon_0>0 and c_G in (0,1] such that
 Haar{U:||U-I||_tau<=epsilon}>=c_G epsilon^m_G,
                    0<epsilon<=epsilon_0.              (3.1)
This follows by integrating the smooth strictly positive Haar density
in any fixed local exponential chart. It is a finite-dimensional
compact-group fact; no background or volume enters.

If every link is in this epsilon ball, unitarity and a four-term
product telescoping give ||U_p-I||_tau<=4epsilon. Consequently
 h(U_p)<=(1/2)(4epsilon)^2=8epsilon^2.
For 0<g<=min(epsilon_0,1), restrict the full partition integral to
the box ||U_e-I||_tau<=g for every link. Then
 Z_g >= (c_G g^m_G)^(dV) exp[-8 binom(d,2)V].             (3.2)
In particular
 -(1/V)log Z_g
 <= d m_G log(1/g)+C_(G,d),
 C_(G,d)=8 binom(d,2)+d log(1/c_G).                      (3.3)
The lower bound is crude but sufficient. It is not the assertion that
the small-link box carries most of the measure. It is only a lower
bound for the normalization, which is then distributed over N_c
copies by (2.5).

4. Prescribed bad cells under the full normalized measure

For the event (1.2), every reflected copy remains a cell-interior
plaquette-energy event. Distinct cell interiors contain distinct
plaquettes. On its full dissemination, each cell therefore contributes
at least delta^2 to A(U), so
 Z_g[D(1_B)]<=exp[-N_c delta^2/g^2].                     (4.1)
The remaining product Haar integral is at most one.

Combine (4.1) and (3.3):
 (Z_g[D(1_B)]/Z_g)^(1/N_c)
 <=exp[-delta^2/g^2
             +b^d{d m_G log(1/g)+C_(G,d)}].              (4.2)
The same bound holds for any cell event forcing its interior action
to be at least delta^2, and for any reflected placement within the cell.
Using (2.5) proves
 mu_g(intersection_(j in S) B_j(delta))<=z_b(g,delta)^|S|. (4.3)
If the right exponential exceeds one, use the trivial bound one.
The claim is uniform in volume and in the set S; the cells need only
be distinct, since strict interior support already separates them.

More generally, if event E_j forces interior action at least e_j>=0,
the right side is
 product_(j in S) min{1,exp[-e_j/g^2+
                 b^d{d m_G log(1/g)+C_(G,d)}]}.          (4.4)
This does not require a particular shape of the interior event.

For fixed b and delta(g)=g R(g), (4.2) is
 exp[-R(g)^2+C_b log(1/g)+C_b].
It tends to zero when R(g)^2/log(1/g) tends to infinity, for example
R(g)=(log(1/g))^a with a>1/2. A norm threshold
||U_p-I||_tau>=delta instead gives e_j=delta^2/2.
Constants are not uniform in a cell side b growing with the cutoff;
the b^d entropy cost is explicit.

5. Arbitrary prescribed plaquettes and cluster bounds

The fixed-grid support condition can be removed at a finite cost.
Let S be any prescribed collection of distinct elementary plaquettes.
Write a positive plaquette as (x;mu,nu), mu<nu. Color it by
 (x mod b, mu,nu).
There are
 Q_b=b^d binom(d,2)                                    (5.1)
colors. Some color contains at least |S|/Q_b plaquettes. Fix this color,
with anchor residue a, and use the grid shifted by s=a-(1,...,1).
Every anchor in that color lies at relative position (1,...,1) of
a cell. Its plaquette vertices have coordinates 1 or 2, so are strictly
interior for b>=3. Distinct anchors of the fixed color belong to
distinct cells; the fixed orientation prevents two selected
plaquettes with the same anchor.

The event that all plaquettes in S have h>=delta^2 implies the
corresponding bad-cell event on this deterministic color subset.
Equation (4.3) thus gives, for z_b<=1,
 mu_g(h(U_p)>=delta^2 for all p in S)
       <=z_b(g,delta)^(|S|/Q_b).                        (5.2)
No independence and no random coloring are used. The grid may depend
on the prescribed set through its selected color; translation
invariance and (2.5) hold for every one of the finitely many shifts.

For the fixed cell graph with nearest-neighbor adjacency, degree at most
2d, a canonical spanning-tree traversal bounds the number of connected
sets of size m containing a chosen cell by (2d)^(2m).
If its bad cluster has size at least m, it contains such a connected
set of size exactly m. Therefore
 mu_g(|bad cluster at j|>=m)
     <=[(2d)^2 z_b(g,delta)]^m.                          (5.3)
The same argument works for the plaquette graph with a fixed finite
adjacency degree Delta_d, using z_b^(1/Q_b).
For adjacency by shared vertices, Delta_d<=16 binom(d,2) is a safe
dimension-only bound. It gives
 mu_g(|bad plaquette cluster at p|>=m)
     <=[Delta_d^2 z_b(g,delta)^(1/Q_b)]^m.                (5.4)
Periodic identifications cannot increase the degree or this counting
bound. These are Peierls probability bounds, not a claim of full
stochastic domination by independent Bernoulli variables.

In particular a fixed local bad event is uniformly rare as g tends
to zero. The probability of ANY bad cell in a growing torus is only
bounded by N_c z_b; the theorem does not claim that the entire lattice
is in one small-field sector with a volume-independent probability.

6. Why this does not automatically control retained-window complements

There are three distinct issues.

(a) Raw links are not rare near the identity.
For a link with distinct endpoints, gauge invariance at one endpoint
makes its marginal left-invariant under G. Thus it is exactly Haar:
 mu_g(U_e in A)=Haar(A)                                  (6.1)
for every measurable A and every g. The probability that a raw link
lies outside a shrinking identity window tends to one, not zero.
Pure-gauge configurations have every plaquette equal to I while their
individual links can be arbitrarily far from I. Therefore no
plaquette Peierls estimate can directly imply rarity of an unfixed
raw-link window complement.

(b) A gauge-fixed fluctuation window is a different observable.
The actual retained variable xi is produced after a background-dependent
nonlinear constrained chart, a chosen contour gauge, and a coarse
constraint. Its bad event ||xi||>R is not, from the material proved
here, an event forcing one cell's Wilson energy to exceed c g^2 R^2.
A proof would need a quantitative geometric/energetic implication
for that exact chart, perhaps after selecting a suitable local
background and allowing gauge transformations. A local tree gauge
can control potentials from small plaquettes, but it does not
automatically identify those potentials with xi, its minimizing
background, or the needed C1alpha domain.

(c) Curvature smallness does not fix global holonomy sectors.
On a periodic lattice set U_1(x)=exp(i theta H/n_1) and all other
links to I. All plaquettes equal I, while the noncontractible
Wilson line is exp(i theta H), which can be nontrivial. Thus
plaquette control alone cannot place the entire field in a global
trivial-holonomy chart. A complete construction must retain or
control these sectors rather than declaring them part of an
energetically suppressed plaquette event.

The theorem also assumes the positive bare Wilson measure. It has not
been extended here to a complex analytic source measure, arbitrary
conditioned boundaries, or a general effective action after many RG
steps. Reflection positivity and exact plaquette assignment must be
proved anew if those objects replace (1.1).

7. Consequence for H2 and precise missing interfaces

PROVED:
 - actual normalized finite-volume Wilson probabilities;
 - uniform-volume prescribed bad-cell and bad-plaquette bounds;
 - original odd-L torus compatibility;
 - local bad-cluster Peierls tails at sufficiently weak coupling;
 - thresholds delta=gR with R^2 dominating log(1/g), at fixed b.

NOT PROVED:
 - a deterministic implication from the actual retained-window
   complement to the bad-energy events above, with constants uniform
   in the full RG history;
 - an exact decomposition and convergent summation of all large-field
   components, including gauge/holonomy sectors and interfaces;
 - complex-source, locality and polymer norm bounds for that
   decomposition compatible with the iterated small-field map;
 - passage from these bare probabilities to tightness, reconstruction,
   nontriviality or a mass gap.

Thus this is a genuine full-measure H2 input, but not a closure of H2.
It isolates an explicit rare geometric event and the exact missing
bridge to the retained-coordinate cutoff.
