UNIFORM AFFINE INFRARED TAIL VIA LOCAL CUBIC RESOLVENTS
18 October 2026. Research proof, provenance; independent audit requested.

Result. Uniform localized relative physical vertices, together with
flat relative weighted resolvent control, imply an O(1) affine heat
tail in final physical time u>=1. The proof retains the local block
trace throughout; it makes no cyclic permutation across that block.
It applies to the actual soft scalar/vector pair through their
identified first two affine jets. The coarse determinant remainders
are separate O(1) inputs. No intermediate-time plateau or logarithmic
coefficient is inferred.

1. Spaces and exact sufficient inputs

Fix d=4, mu=1/K and compatible tori with current side KN, or the
infinite lattice. The Hilbert space has fixed color/component fiber
per fine site or positive bond. Partition it into centered final
blocks indexed by Z^4 (or its N-periodic quotient), with projections
P_x. Bond assignment is by initial site. Harmless fixed collars may
be used for source spaces.

The reference operator is
  L=I+K^2 Delta,
with the componentwise flat lattice Laplacian. Let X0 be the actual
flat physical operator (K^2 times its current-counting matrix).
We use the following precise inputs, uniform in K and old depth M.
For the actual vector's spatial estimates we work on the chosen joint
infinite structural system or on explicit periodizations of its
kernels. Actual arbitrary finite-regulator gap and heat estimates are
separate facts; no exact infinite moment cancellation is inferred from
a finite momentum grid or from an unrelated finite winding kernel.
The abstract argument below is also finite-volume uniform whenever
all three inputs hold on the finite system in question.

(I) X0 is positive self-adjoint and X0>=c0 L.

(II) For bounded physical 1-Lipschitz weights f, W=exp(theta f),
  ||L^-1/2 (W X0 W^-1-X0)L^-1/2|| <= C theta
for all sufficiently small fixed theta. Scalar/bond weights use their
site or initial site, respectively. The same assertion may be proved
first for truncated distances. Its constants do not count block
fiber dimension.

(III) Let a_j be complete bounded physical C1 source fields supported
in a fixed smooth collar of block j, with norm a_j^*=||a_j||phys,1.
Write V_j for the first variation and V_jk for the mixed second
variation of X at zero. For any weight W as in (II),
  ||L^-1/2 W V_j W^-1 L^-1/2|| <= C a_j^*,
  ||L^-1/2 W V_jk W^-1 L^-1/2|| <= C a_j^* a_k^*.
Moreover, let f_j be physical distance to the fixed physical cube or
collar of block j, and use W_j=exp(theta f_j) and bounded truncations.
This is a 1-Lipschitz function of fine-site position divided by K,
not the piecewise constant distance between block labels. Then
  ||L^-1/2 W_j V_j W_j L^-1/2|| <= C a_j^*.
For V_jk the analogous two-sided bound holds centered at j and at k,
with right side C a_j^* a_k^*.

The two-sided weights in the last input are important: they attach a
source to both matrix ends. A mere unlocalized relative operator
bound is insufficient for the local trace statement.

These inputs may be stated for bilinear holomorphic jets. No complex
self-adjointness is required. Only X0 and L are positive.

2. Applicability of the inputs to the actual soft pair

For the soft scalar T_s=Delta_A+mu^2 Pi_A, Section3 of
soft_scalar_coarse_reduction_research.txt gives (I). The weighted
relative proof in its Section5 gives (II). Its D/J derivative
expansion gives (III), with physical sources even needing only their
sup norm. J is normalized and block local, so its source derivatives
carry no K^4 factor.

For the soft vector use the flat domination established in the
mean-zero and soft-vector modules: the operator dominates a fixed
multiple of Delta_bond+mu^2 I in counting units. The added DJ term
is positive. This gives (I) for X0=K^2 V0.

For its action term, the covariant_hessian_relative_vertices proof
gives, after physical rescaling, first-two jets of the form
  Dphys^T Z + Z^T Dphys + E
with uniformly bounded localized Z,E; the second-order Z may have
an additional beneficial factor mu. These factors have exponential
rooted final-block bounds. Conjugation commutators of Dphys with W
are bounded O(theta). Multiplication by W_j on both sides is allowed
by that rooted decay, with a smaller theta. Since
||Dphys L^-1/2||<=1, this supplies (III).

The covariant D_A D_A^T term has the same elementary derivative
factorization. The soft averaging/coarse-Hessian term has uniformly
bounded physical operator vertices: each corrected angle average
and its localized physical derivatives has counting size O(mu).
Its pair supplies mu^2, canceled by K^2. Its coarse fiber is fixed,
and the old-depth action/source bounds are uniform. Its supports and
coarse sums give (III) directly. The soft-vector note separately
identifies these auxiliary first-two jets with the actual affine
stationary jets, retaining the required residual/current terms.

For (II), use precisely the repaired ordered Laurent factorization in
Section4 of covariant_hessian_relative_vertices_research.txt:
  q_M=D0^T H0 D0,
where BOTH the constant and linear flat momentum jets vanish and H0
is symmetric with uniform exponentially summable current kernels.
Separate component row sums alone do not give this factorization,
and no common componentwise difference-square identity is used.
For a physical 1-Lipschitz weight, conjugating each outer ordinary
gradient changes it by a bounded multiplication operator O(theta mu);
conjugating H0 changes its norm by O(theta mu), using its exponential
first moment. Retain the latter change BETWEEN the two gradients.
After multiplying by K^2 and sandwiching with L^-1/2, the outer-gradient
errors are O(theta+theta^2), and the middle error is O(theta mu).
The flat covariant-gradient term has the same estimate. The bounded
physical block penalty has fixed physical range; its coarse q_MK
factor has uniform exponential moments. Its error is O(theta).
This proves (II) without assuming the weighted absolute row norm of
X0 is uniformly bounded (it generally grows as K^2).

Thus this is a consequence of the LOCALIZED covariant relative-vertex
proof and the soft-vector identification, not merely of the earlier
unlocalized inequality ||X0^-1/2 V_j X0^-1/2||<=C.
The actual polynomial-jet identification is only needed at each fixed
K. Its constants need not be uniform in K.

3. A dimension-free block Schatten estimate

The elementary massive lattice heat bound implies
  Tr(P_x L^-3 P_x)<=C,                                  (3.1)
uniformly in K,N. One may integrate the flat heat trace against
t^2 exp(-t)/2. Per final block that trace is at most
  C{K^4/(1+K^2 t)^2+N^-4};
its integral is bounded, including the finite zero mode. The same
calculation applies to the infinite lattice. Fixed color/component
multiplicity is absorbed in C.

Importantly, compression and taking powers are not interchanged.
For eigenvectors u_i of P_x L^-1 P_x in its finite-dimensional block,
scalar Jensen for the spectral measure of L^-1 gives
  <u_i,L^-1u_i>^3 <= <u_i,L^-3u_i>.
Summing proves
  ||P_x L^-1/2||_S6^6
   =Tr[(P_xL^-1P_x)^3] <= Tr(P_xL^-3P_x)<=C.             (3.2)
The analogous right-ended norm has the same bound. Therefore every
operator of the form
  L^-1/2 A L^-1/2,       ||A||<=C_A,
satisfies
  ||P_x L^-1/2 A L^-1/2 P_y||_S3 <= C C_A,              (3.3)
by Schatten Holder with exponents 6,infinity,6.
There is no factor proportional to the fine block rank K^4.

4. Uniform contour and resolvent source kernels

Choose 0<lambda0<c0/4 and a fixed angle 0<phi<pi/2.
Let Gamma be the standard positively oriented Dunford contour
consisting of the two rays lambda0+r exp(+-i phi), surrounding the
positive spectrum to its right. At a finite regulator it can first
be closed beyond the largest eigenvalue.

For z on Gamma,
  ||L^1/2(z-X0)^-1 L^1/2||<=C_Gamma.                    (4.1)
Indeed X0>=c0 L, and the spectral norm of
X0^1/2(z-X0)^-1X0^1/2 is uniformly bounded on these rays.
Input(II) and a sufficiently small theta then give, by a relative
resolvent Neumann expansion,
  W(z-X0)^-1W^-1=L^-1/2 A_(z,W)L^-1/2,
  ||A_(z,W)||<=C,                                      (4.2)
uniformly on the whole contour. The sign in the resolvent is
irrelevant for these norm estimates.

Write R_z=(z-X0)^-1. Its first-two variations are the usual finite
resolvent products R_z V_j R_z and
R_z V_jk R_z+R_z V_j R_z V_k R_z+
R_z V_k R_z V_j R_z (with the corresponding Taylor/derivative
convention). Equations(4.1)--(4.2) and input(III) put each product,
including its weighted versions, in the form in (3.3), with uniform
middle-operator norm.

Here is explicit source localization avoiding fractional-weight
commutators. For a first variation, conjugating with a TWO-SIDED
weight centered at j gives exactly
  W_j (R_z V_j R_z) W_j
   =(W_j R_z W_j^-1)(W_j V_j W_j)
                         (W_j^-1 R_z W_j).
Each factor has the relative normalization just described, so its
outer factors are L^-1/2 and its middle is bounded. If a second
source k is present, mark the occurrence of j and move W_j through
all other factors. For example,
  W_j(R_z V_j R_z V_k R_z)W_j
   =R_+ (W_j V_j W_j) R_- (W_j^-1 V_k W_j) R_-.
Input(III) bounds the unmarked conjugated vertex. The mixed primitive
vertex V_jk uses its two-sided weighted bound. Repeating with k as
the marked source gives both endpoint-to-source estimates.

Combining these estimates with (3.3) proves, for n=0,1,2,
  ||P_x (delta_(j1)...delta_(jn) R_z) P_y||_S3
   <=C_n product a_j^* exp[-c tau(x,y,j1,...,jn)].        (4.3)
For n=0 choose a one-sided distance weight from y. For n=1 the
two-sided weight gives exp[-theta(dist(x,j)+dist(y,j))].
For n=2 take the geometric mean of the separate j and k estimates.
The resulting four edges form a connected graph on the marked
vertices, so their length dominates its minimum connecting-tree
length. Here tau allows auxiliary vertices in the connecting graph.
The infimum and supremum of each physical f_j on a fixed block differ
by at most a dimensional constant. Hence the fine-site weights just
defined imply the displayed block-label exponential factors, up to a
fixed multiplicative constant. Fixed collar sizes change only C_n.
All statements hold for periodic distances as well. Truncating weights
and taking limits is legitimate because every bound is uniform.

5. Third resolvent power and the genuinely local trace

For u>0, functional calculus gives
  exp(-uX)= (2/u^2)(1/(2pi i))
                integral_Gamma exp(-uz)(z-X)^-3 dz.     (5.1)
For a scalar eigenvalue this is the residue of the third-order pole:
the residue is u^2 exp(-u lambda)/2. Thus the displayed coefficient
and sign are fixed.

Differentiate (z-X)^-3=R_z R_z R_z through order two.
Each term is a product of THREE differentiated-resolvent factors;
the orders n1+n2+n3 sum to at most two. Insert the block resolution
of identity between those factors. For the trace in block0,
Schatten Holder gives
  |Tr(P0 R_z^(n1) P_i R_z^(n2) P_j R_z^(n3) P0)|
   <= ||P0 R_z^(n1)P_i||_S3
      ||P_i R_z^(n2)P_j||_S3
      ||P_j R_z^(n3)P0||_S3.                           (5.2)
The root blocks remain where they belong. Nothing is moved cyclically
through P0.

Equation(4.3) and coarse convolution, spending an exponent, imply
  |delta_(j1)...delta_(jn) Tr(P0 R_z^3 P0)|
    <=C_n product a_j^* exp[-c' tau(0,j1,...,jn)]         (5.3)
for n<=2. The sums over i,j are absolutely convergent in trace norm,
uniformly in K,N,z. This also constructs the local infinite trace;
one does not take an infinite extensive trace.

On Gamma, Re z=lambda0+r cos phi. Hence for u>=1,
  integral_Gamma |exp(-uz)| |dz|
                  <= C exp(-lambda0 u)/u.
Combine (5.1),(5.3) to get
  |delta_(j1)...delta_(jn) Tr(P0 exp(-uX)P0)|
   <= C_n u^-3 exp(-lambda0 u)
       product a_j^* exp[-c' tau(0,j1,...,jn)] .          (5.4)
The factor u^-3 is harmless on u>=1; it is not claimed to be the
small-time asymptotic. Differentiation, contour integration, block
summation and local limits are justified by the displayed absolute
majorants. At finite K one may first use bounded compact sources
and the actual local germ, then extend the coefficient by these bounds.

6. Affine source and the actual infrared tail

Choose a fixed smooth physical partition of unity on bounded final
block collars with bounded first derivatives and finite overlap.
Decompose the affine physical field
  A_mu(x)= (x_nu/K) H,       mu!=nu,
as a sum of complete supported fields a_j. Their physical C1 norms
satisfy a_j^*<=C(1+|j|).
Equation(5.4) for n=2 is summable with these polynomial weights.
Consequently the formal affine Taylor coefficient satisfies
  |[t^2] Tr_0 exp[-u X(t A_aff)]|
                         <= C exp(-c u),       u>=1,   (6.1)
uniformly in K and old M along the stated infinite structural
systems. This is a Taylor-kernel evaluation, not an unbounded
background inserted into a fixed sup ball.

For the actual soft scalar and vector matrices, their first two
stationary affine jets agree with these computed jets up to a fine
gauge. The diagonal color traces are individually gauge invariant.
Thus (6.1) bounds the ACTUAL affine coefficient, with no uniform-K
stationary-response norm. The fixed-K polynomial-jet identification
is used before the uniform explicit-representative estimate.

Writing X_s=K^2 T_s and X_v=K^2 V, the combined physical infrared
tail therefore satisfies
  integral_1^infinity
   |[t^2]{Tr_(0,E) exp(-u X_v(t))
                      -2 Tr_(0,S) exp(-u X_s(t))}| du/u <= C.     (6.2)
Equivalently this is the current-time tail s>=K^2 of the soft-pair
Maxwell heat formula. The same result holds for either factor
separately. Constant normalization terms have zero affine coefficient.

No finite torus affine-flux family is asserted. At finite volume the
source bounds hold for explicit periodizations, or any finite model
for which (I)--(III) have separately been verified. The actual local
polynomial coefficient is obtained in the chosen infinite structural
system through the already proved fixed-K identification; arbitrary
actual finite winding kernels are not identified with periodizations.

7. What this removes and what it leaves

Together with the exact soft factorization and the O(1) coarse
Maxwell corrections, (6.2) removes the large-physical-time tail from
the logarithmic calculation. It leaves the intermediate regime
1<<s<<K^2 and the short-current-time reference contribution.
The proof does not establish a plateau value, its sign, or a
bounded comparison with a chosen continuum reference on that
intermediate regime.

For fidelity, the genuinely required new analytic input is(III)
with source attachment, not just global relative-form boundedness.
Sections2--5 explain precisely how the existing localized
covariant-vertex and soft-factor bounds supply it.
