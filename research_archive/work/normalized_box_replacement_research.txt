1. Fixed model and the two proved inputs

Work with the normalized positive bare Wilson measure

 mu(dU)=Z_W^{-1} exp[-S_W(U)/g^2] product_e dU_e

on a finite compatible four-dimensional periodic lattice, normalized
Haar on each independent link. Fix a cubical box Omega=[0,R]^4, R>=2,
and a finite collection I of its embedded translates Omega_i whose
VERTEX SETS are pairwise disjoint. No local periodic identification
is allowed inside a box. All constants below may depend on R and the
fixed group and on the fixed chessboard side b>=3, but not on |I|,
the surrounding torus size, or the placement of the boxes. Periods
are divisible by 2b, as in the full Wilson Peierls theorem.

For each box let E_circ,i be its nonboundary edges. The exterior sigma
field X is generated by all links outside their union, including EVERY
box boundary link. Denote this exterior configuration by omega, its
actual marginal by mu_X, and the box interior coordinates by u_i.
Every plaquette touching E_circ,i lies wholly inside Omega_i. Therefore
no plaquette depends on interiors of two boxes. The exact bare Wilson
conditional law is

 mu(domega,du)=mu_X(domega) product_(i in I) Q_i^omega(du_i),       (1.1)

where Q_i^omega is the normalized compact box Wilson law with its
actual boundary edges held fixed. It depends only on that box boundary.
Additive exterior/boundary action constants cancel in its normalization.
This is exact conditional independence, not an approximate decoupling.

Use the actual boundary-only tree gauge and then the rooted forest
gauge of Notebook38. Let B_i(omega) be the event that at least one
boundary plaquette has h(U_p)>=epsilon_R^2. On B_i^c, the boundary-only
gauge puts the data in the fixed small-boundary neighborhood. Let
W_i(omega) be the corresponding forest-coordinate window

 ||x-x_*(V_i(omega))||_infinity <= g T,                         (1.2)

about the unique global conditional minimizer. This is the ACTUAL
window in fixed-box forest coordinates, with the invariant edge/color
norm; it is not a window about the homogeneous coarse-constrained RG
minimizer. The boundary gauge is determined by omega. Lifting the
window back to the original coordinates defines a measurable event.
It is gauge invariant, because the full tree representative and its
unique minimizer transform by constant root conjugation.

The proved normalized conditional theorem gives

 Q_i^omega(W_i^c)<=a:=C exp(-c T^2) on B_i^c,                   (1.3)

for 0<g<=g0 and 1<=T<=r/g. Choose a fixed T0>=1 large enough that
C exp(-c T0^2)<=1/2, and henceforth T0<=T<=r/g. Then a<=1/2.
The local event has positive conditional mass in any case; the
restriction supplies the useful uniform denominator lower bound.

The full Wilson boundary estimate gives, for every S subset I,

 mu_X(intersection_(i in S) B_i)<=beta^{|S|},                   (1.4)
 beta=min{1,N_partial z_b(g,epsilon_R)^(1/Q_b)},
 N_partial=24 R^3, Q_b=6 b^4,
 z_b(g,delta)=min{1,exp[-delta^2/g^2+
                   b^4(4 dimG log(1/g)+C_(G,4))]}.

Indeed boundary events are exterior measurable, so their mu_X
probabilities equal their full mu probabilities. No independence
of different boundaries is assumed. Equations(1.1)--(1.4) are the
only model-specific inputs to the normalized operation below; all
are proved in fixed_box_wilson_tail_fragment.tex and
full_measure_box_bridge_fragment.tex, with the full bare normalization.

2. Definition and exact exterior/partition normalization

Define, for every omega,

 p_i(omega)=1_(B_i^c) Q_i^omega(W_i^c),
 P_i^omega = Q_i^omega(. | W_i) on B_i^c,
 P_i^omega = Q_i^omega on B_i.                                (2.1)

On good boundaries, 1-p_i>=1-a>=1/2. On bad boundaries, p_i=0;
the law is LEFT UNCHANGED. Every kernel is Borel: the finite compact
conditional integrals, boundary gauges and local minimizer/window
are measurable. Conditional Wilson densities are strictly positive,
so the normalization on the good window is nonzero.

For any subcollection J subset I, define the replacement of those
boxes only by

 nu_J(domega,du)=mu_X(domega)
          product_(i in J) P_i^omega(du_i)
          product_(i notin J) Q_i^omega(du_i).                 (2.2)

Write nu=nu_I. These are probability measures and have EXACTLY the
original exterior marginal mu_X. Their Radon--Nikodym derivative is

 dnu_J/dmu = product_(i in J, B_i^c)
                    1_(W_i)(u_i)/(1-p_i(omega)).              (2.3)

To prove this, condition on omega and multiply the normalized kernels.
Each factor has conditional Q_i expectation one, so the product does
too. Consequently the same operation on the UNNORMALIZED Wilson
measure retains its total mass Z_W exactly:

 integral exp[-S_W/g^2] product_(i in J,B_i^c)
               1_(W_i)/(1-p_i) product_e dU_e = Z_W.          (2.4)

There is no missing free-energy/vacuum normalization. The factors
(1-p_i)^{-1} are exact boundary-dependent conditional denominators,
not a substitution of a Gaussian determinant. Their logs obey

 0<=-log(1-p_i)<=p_i/(1-a)<=a/(1-a),                         (2.5)

and read only the boundary of the corresponding fixed box. The
remaining sharp window is also confined to that box. The full
Radon--Nikodym product may grow exponentially with |J|; this does
not change the conditional expectation identity and is not used as
a uniform global density bound.

The replacement measure is gauge invariant. The original conditional
law transforms covariantly with the exterior, B_i is invariant, and
the lifted W_i is invariant under the full tree-gauge convention.
Thus the Radon--Nikodym function(2.3) is gauge invariant. No gauge
volume, Haar Jacobian or conditional normalization has been dropped.

The local kernels can also be applied as independent resampling
operators to a configuration: keep omega fixed and redraw box i from
P_i^omega. They commute for distinct boxes, since no redraw changes
any other box's boundary, and each is idempotent. Applying all of
them once to mu gives nu. The statement is about these fixed boxes;
it is not a resampling assertion for overlapping components.

3. Exact sparse failure expansion and an explicit coupling

On good boundaries with p_i>0 define

 F_i^omega=Q_i^omega(. | W_i^c).

Where p_i=0, including bad boundaries, choose any probability kernel
for F_i (for example Q_i); its value is multiplied by zero. Then

 Q_i^omega=(1-p_i)P_i^omega+p_i F_i^omega                     (3.1)

for every omega. Multiplying these identities gives the exact positive
failure expansion

 mu=sum_(S subset I) M_S,
 M_S(domega,du)=mu_X(domega)
    [product_(i in S)p_i product_(i notin S)(1-p_i)]
    product_(i in S)F_i^omega(du_i)
    product_(i notin S)P_i^omega(du_i).                       (3.2)

Every term is a positive finite measure. Its mass is the expectation
of the displayed Bernoulli weight; the masses sum to one. The empty
term is product_i(1-p_i(omega)) times nu, not a constant multiple of
nu unless that exterior function happens to be constant. Formula(3.2)
therefore does not condition or alter the exterior law illicitly.

Here is a coupling realizing the expansion. Sample omega with law
mu_X. Independently across boxes, conditional on omega, sample
Y_i~P_i^omega, an independent Bernoulli mark D_i with parameter p_i,
and, when needed, Z_i~F_i^omega. Put

 X_i=Y_i if D_i=0,  X_i=Z_i if D_i=1.                         (3.3)

Then (omega,X) has law mu and (omega,Y) has law nu. On a marked
box, X_i is outside W_i and Y_i is inside, so X_i!=Y_i almost surely;
on an unmarked box they are identical. On bad-boundary boxes p_i=0,
so they are identical and have the original conditional law. Thus D_i
is exactly the discrepancy indicator for this coupling.

Taking independent uniforms U_i, independent of omega, and defining
D_i=1_{U_i<=p_i(omega)} realizes the marks together with iid Bernoulli(a)
variables A_i=1_{U_i<=a}, with D_i<=A_i pointwise. Hence discrepancy
marks are STOCHASTICALLY DOMINATED by iid Bernoulli(a), not merely
bounded on prescribed intersections. In particular, for every S,

 P(D_i=1 for all i in S)=E product_(i in S)p_i<=a^{|S|}.       (3.4)

This stronger statement is special to discrepancy marks of this
explicit conditional coupling. It is not asserted for the boundary
bad events, which can be correlated.

4. Local comparisons uniform in the total number of boxes

Let A subset I. Include the entire exterior omega but only the
interiors in A. The conditional original/replacement laws are product
Q_i and product P_i. The second is exactly the first conditioned on
all good-boundary W_i, whose conditional probability is
q_A(omega)=product_(i in A)(1-p_i). Therefore, with total variation
convention ||lambda-lambda'||TV=sup_events|lambda(E)-lambda'(E)|,

 ||mu_(X,A)-nu_(X,A)||TV
       =E_[mu_X][1-product_(i in A)(1-p_i)]
       <=a |A|.                                             (4.1)

The equality follows either by integrating the positive and negative
parts of the conditional density, or from the coupling: no discrepancy
has probability q_A; if a discrepancy occurs the original field lies
outside the replacement support in at least one box. For the marginal
on A alone the same expression is an upper bound. This distinguishes
a genuinely local estimate from global TV, which can approach one
when the number of boxes grows.

For every bounded observable f depending on omega and the interiors
in A,

 |mu(f)-nu(f)|<=2 ||f||infinity a |A|.                         (4.2)

More generally define its box oscillation
 osc_i(f)=sup |f(omega,u)-f(omega,u')|,
where configurations differ only in interior i. Telescoping along
the coupling yields

 |mu(f)-nu(f)|<=sum_(i in I) osc_i(f) E p_i
                   <=a sum_(i in I) osc_i(f).                 (4.3)

This applies to an extensive collection with a finite/summable total
oscillation and has no unnecessary factor |I| for a local observable.
All exterior observables have zero error exactly.

The joint-with-exterior relative entropy also has an exact formula:

 D(nu_(X,A)||mu_(X,A))
   =E_[mu_X] sum_(i in A) [-log(1-p_i)]
   <=|A| a/(1-a).                                            (4.4)

It follows by substituting(2.3); all window indicators equal one
nu-almost surely. This is an optional quantitative normalization bound,
not a statement that the reverse relative entropy is finite.

5. Bad boundaries and genuinely sparse uncontrolled boxes

The replacement does not claim to improve an arbitrary boundary.
Since the exterior marginal is unchanged, for every S,

 nu(intersection_(i in S) B_i)=mu(intersection_(i in S)B_i)
                                      <=beta^{|S|}.           (5.1)

In the replacement measure the actual Notebook38 box defect is exactly
B_i almost surely: on good boundaries its interior window is enforced.
This improves the joint defect bound from (a+beta)^{|S|} to beta^{|S|}
for that measure, without discarding any bad-boundary conditional law.

On the coupling define the uncontrolled mark C_i=1_{B_i} OR D_i.
These alternatives are disjoint at each i. Expand a prescribed
intersection over the subset J assigned the bad-boundary alternative.
Conditional independence of D_i and p_i<=a give

 P(C_i=1 for all i in S)
 <=sum_(J subset S) a^{|S|-|J|}
                      mu_X(intersection_(i in J)B_i)
 <=(a+beta)^{|S|}.                                          (5.2)

Put epsilon=min(1,a+beta). This joint-inclusion estimate does not by
itself assert stochastic domination of C by iid Bernoulli(epsilon).
It does give, by expanding the finite product, for t>=0,

 E exp[t sum_(i in A) C_i]
       <=[1+epsilon(e^t-1)]^{|A|}.                           (5.3)

The corresponding discrepancy moment has a in place of epsilon,
conditionally on omega as well. For k>=1,

 P(sum_(i in A) C_i>=k)
       <=min{1, binom(|A|,k) epsilon^k}
       <=min{1,(e |A| epsilon/k)^k},                         (5.4)

with the probability zero for k>|A|. One may instead optimize the
Chernoff bound from(5.3).

Choose any deterministic adjacency graph on I of maximum degree
Delta>=2, for example a fixed separated box grid with nearest-neighbor
adjacency. Canonical spanning-tree traversals give at most Delta^(2m)
connected m-subsets containing a root. Thus

 P(|C-cluster at i|>=m)<=[Delta^2 epsilon]^m,                  (5.5)

and analogous bounds hold for D with a and for replacement defects
with beta. The statements are uniform in |I| and the surrounding volume;
only the fixed geometry and degree enter. They do not construct the
source RG determining sets or control arbitrary growing components.

For T(g)=(log(1/g))^alpha, alpha>1/2, the parameter conditions hold at
small g and both a and beta are smaller than every fixed power of g.
Consequently every fixed-region comparison and every prescribed set
of discrepancies has the corresponding superpolynomial weak-coupling
bound, with constants fixed before the surrounding volume grows.

6. Exact sources and normalization identities

The normalized operation retains the complete measure, not only a
ratio evaluated at zero source. For bounded measurable single-box
sources f_i(omega,u_i) and a bounded exterior source f_X(omega),

 nu(exp[f_X+sum_i f_i])
   =integral mu_X(domega) exp f_X
                         product_i P_i^omega(exp f_i),        (6.1)

where, on a good boundary,

 P_i^omega(exp f_i)=Q_i^omega(1_Wi exp f_i)/(1-p_i),           (6.2)

and on a bad boundary it is Q_i^omega(exp f_i). The original source
functional satisfies exactly

 mu(exp[f_X+sum_i f_i])
   =integral mu_X(domega) exp f_X
       product_i[(1-p_i)P_i^omega(exp f_i)
                              +p_iF_i^omega(exp f_i)].        (6.3)

This is the source-marked positive failure expansion for real sources.
For bounded complex sources the same finite identities hold absolutely;
positivity is then only asserted for the underlying real measures.
The denominators are the exact zero-source conditional window masses.
No factor depending on the exterior is absorbed into a vacuum constant.

For any bounded source F depending on omega and interiors in A, with
||F||infinity<=M, the coupling gives

 |mu(e^F)-nu(e^F)|<=2 e^M a |A|.                             (6.4)

For real F, both expectations are >=e^-M, so

 |log mu(e^F)-log nu(e^F)|<=2 e^(2M) a |A|.                   (6.5)

For bounded observables O_l, their finite generating functions in
complex parameters are entire and can be differentiated under the
integral. This gives exact finite joint moment/source formulas with
windows and g held fixed. It does not supply a uniform complex
zero-free radius, renormalized observable bounds or a continuum source
hierarchy. Cross-box sources can use(6.4) and the full expansion(3.2),
without falsely factoring them as in(6.1).

6.1 A fixed local complex source disk and connected cumulants

Let O_1,...,O_q be bounded observables depending on the same exterior
and a fixed set A of interiors. Put M_l=||O_l||infinity; omit zero
observables. Set F(t)=sum_l t_l O_l and choose any fixed

 0<s0<log2, d0=2-exp(s0)>0.

On the weighted source ball sum_l |t_l|M_l<=s0,

 |Z_mu(t)-1|, |Z_nu(t)-1|<=exp(s0)-1<1,
 Z_lambda(t)=lambda(exp F(t)).                              (6.6)

Thus both generating functions are nonzero there. Their unique analytic
logs taking value zero at t=0 are on the same disk branch. The straight
segment joining their values also lies in this disk; integrating 1/z
along it and using(6.4) gives

 |log Z_mu(t)-log Z_nu(t)|
       <=[2 exp(s0)/d0] |A|a.                              (6.7)

This is a bounded-observable statement with a fixed local support; it
is not a zero-free claim for unbounded or renormalized physical fields.

For a bounded O whose dependence is included in A, its normalized
source numerator satisfies on the same ball

 | mu(O exp F)/Z_mu - nu(O exp F)/Z_nu |
       <=[4 ||O||infinity exp(s0)/d0^2] |A|a.              (6.8)

Indeed the numerator difference is at most 2||O||exp(s0)|A|a,
the denominator difference at most 2exp(s0)|A|a, every denominator
has modulus at least d0, and |nu(O exp F)|<=||O||exp(s0).
The displayed constant uses d0+exp(s0)=2.

Let k=(k_1,...,k_q) be a nonzero multiindex, n=sum k_l. Cauchy on
radii s0/(q M_l) gives for the connected cumulants

 |partial^k[log Z_mu-log Z_nu](0)|
 <=[2exp(s0)/d0]|A|a k! (q/s0)^n product_l M_l^(k_l).     (6.9)

The bound displays the dimension of this source polydisc. Alternatively,
set all inactive source variables to zero and choose radii
r_l=s0 k_l/(n M_l) on active variables. The sharper bound replaces
k!(q/s0)^n by k!(n/s0)^n/product_(k_l>0)k_l^(k_l).
In particular it is at most n!(e/s0)^n, since
k!<=product k_l^(k_l) and n^n<=e^n n!. These are exact fixed-order
cumulants of the two finite measures. Parameters/windows are not
varied during differentiation. Uniformity as a cutoff changes applies
only if the displayed observable bounds and fixed local support remain
uniform; no renormalized field scaling is inferred.
7. Interpretation as a restricted normalized replacement theorem

The theorem is a finite-region operation on the ACTUAL positive bare
Wilson measure over an arbitrary finite union of vertex-disjoint
fixed boxes. It exactly preserves the exterior marginal, the raw
partition mass and gauge invariance; explicitly normalizes each
admitted interior window; leaves every bad-boundary conditional law
unchanged; and accounts for the removed configurations through an
exact positive sparse failure expansion and coupling. Local errors,
discrepancy moments and cluster tails are uniform in the number of
boxes. These conclusions are stronger than merely conditioning the
whole lattice on one simultaneous good event, which would generally
reweight its exterior distribution.

The restriction is substantive. Box size, geometry and the conditional
minimizer are fixed. This is not the homogeneous nonlinear averaging
fiber, does not identify its window with the RG retained window, and
has not been proved for an arbitrary effective action or complex
measure after iteration. No uniform constants for growing boxes,
collared determining sets, scale ages or arbitrary exterior topologies
are supplied. Small boundary curvature is checked using actual
bare-measure probabilities and the fixed boundary gauge; it is not
assumed for all exterior fields. The bad-boundary boxes are therefore
kept in the operation rather than dropped.

This is a natural rigorous stopping theorem and a concrete input for a
future full mixed-history replacement construction. It does not close
the complete RG existence, OS reconstruction, nontriviality or mass-gap
problem.
