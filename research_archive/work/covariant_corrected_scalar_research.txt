2. Exact primitive correction and its gauge differential

Fix U and W=M_ell(U). For a small bond-angle increment x define

 r_U(x;y,z)=(1/i)log[P(e^{ix}U;y,z)P(U;y,z)^-1],
 alpha_U(x;y)=ell^-d sum_(z in B_ell(y)) r_U(x;y,z),
 a_U(x;y)=exp(i alpha_U(x;y)).

At x=0 every r and alpha is zero and a_U=1. Put

 Z_U x= D_x alpha_U(0)[x],
 C_U x= (1/i)D_s[M_ell(e^{isx}U)W^-1]_(s=0).             (2.1)

Define the actual relative corrected output near this reference by

 Fbar_U(x;c)=a_U(x;c_-)^-1
                    M_ell(e^{ix}U)(c) a_U(x;c_+).

Its value at zero is W. Direct differentiation in the left-relative
target coordinate gives the exact ANGLE identity

 Cbar_U=C_U-D_W Z_U.                                    (2.2)

This is an identity between tangents at U. The reference U is held
fixed while x varies. It is not the derivative of the fixed-identity
corrected map at U, and it does not change the original nonlinear
constraint imposed on the actual integral.

For a fine site gauge e^{is phi}, endpoint covariance gives

 (1/i)D_s[P(U^{e^{is phi}};y,z)P(U;y,z)^-1]_(s=0)
                     =phi(y)-Ad_(P(U;y,z))phi(z).

The fine left tangent of that variation is D_U phi. Therefore

 Z_U D_U=r_ell-B_U,
 (B_U phi)(y)=ell^-d sum_(z in B_ell(y))
                            Ad_(P(U;y,z)) phi(z),          (2.3)

where r_ell is restriction to new centers. Ordinary covariance
C_U D_U=D_W r_ell and (2.2) imply

 Cbar_U D_U=D_W B_U.                                    (2.4)

No logarithm-Jacobian factor appears in (2.3): the relative contour
increment equals identity at x=0, so its logarithm differential is
identity. This is the reason the moving-reference correction is
simpler here than the fixed-identity induced gauge map.

3. Exact local gauge covariance

Let O_E(g),O_S(g),O_c(g) be adjoint frames on the fine bond source
sites, fine scalar sites and new centers, respectively. For a fixed
gauge g in the common domain,

 e^{i O_E(g)x} U^g=(e^{ix}U)^g.

The relative contour product at U^g is conjugated by g(y).
Similarity equivariance of the local logarithm gives

 Z_(U^g) O_E(g)=O_c(g) Z_U,
 Cbar_(U^g) O_E(g)=O_Ec(g) Cbar_U,
 B_(U^g) O_S(g)=O_c(g) B_U.                             (3.1)

The ordinary C and D have their usual corresponding covariance.
These identities use simultaneous transformation of the reference
and varied configuration. The endpoint gauges a_U are not assumed
to satisfy a multiplicative cocycle in the reference.

All adjoint frames are orthogonal for the invariant real trace
pairing and complex orthogonal for its holomorphic bilinear
continuation. The identities hold on the common complex germ.

4. Composite tangent identity at ordinary backgrounds

Use C_j,Cbar_j,Z_j,B_j at U_j=M_ell^j(U), and write D_j for
the scalar generator there. Define ordered products

 C^(t)=C_(t-1)...C_0,
 Cbar^(t)=Cbar_(t-1)...Cbar_0,
 B^(t)=B_(t-1)...B_0,

with identity products at t=0. Then C^(k) is exactly the derivative
of the actual ordinary composite constraint. The intertwining
(2.4) gives

 Cbar^(t) D_0=D_t B^(t).                                (4.1)

Set Z^(0)=0 and recursively

 Z^(t+1)=B_t Z^(t)+Z_t C^(t).                            (4.2)

Multiplying C_t=Cbar_t+D_(t+1) Z_t, and using
Cbar_t D_t=D_(t+1)B_t, proves by induction

 C^(t)=Cbar^(t)+D_t Z^(t).                              (4.3)

At t=k these are precisely the general tangent identities needed
by the new all-site determinant bridge:

 C-Cbar=D_c Z,       Cbar D_all=D_c B,
 Z D_all=r_K-B.                                        (4.4)

The last identity follows either from the same recursion or from
Z_tD_t=r_ell-B_t and the ordinary intertwining. Its sign is fixed
by (1.1), not absorbed into an unnamed convention.

All factors are at ORDINARY intermediate references U_j. They are
not evaluated at the fixed-identity corrected trajectory.
Covariance (3.1) telescopes with the ordinary reference frames, so
the composite Cbar, B and Z have the same exact joint covariance.

5. Own-block support and the constant row Gram matrix

Each primitive B row reads scalar sites only in its own block.
For a fine site x in a final K-block B_K(y), let
x=x_0,x_1,...,x_k=y be its unique nested sequence of block centers.
The composite product has exactly one scalar ancestry for x.
Consequently

 B(U;y,x)=K^-d Ad_(P_hier(U;y,x)),                        (5.1)
 P_hier=P_(U_(k-1);x_k,x_(k-1)) ... P_(U_0;x_1,x_0),

and B(U;y,x)=0 outside B_K(y). The factors are ordered from the
final center down to x. Each is an actual group element, even
when it is itself a selected group mean of path transports.

The background dependencies are also contained in the SAME final
block. A stage-j internal contour uses only stage-j bonds whose
endpoints lie in the relevant parent block. Every such ordinary
bond reads the two descendant fine blocks at its endpoints; their
union is contained in that final block, by nested centered tiling.
Induction down the hierarchy proves the assertion. No derivative
of B reads a fine background bond crossing its final block boundary.

The ordinary C and corrected Cbar rows read only the two final
endpoint blocks. For Cbar this follows either from the product
of primitive two-block maps or (4.2)--(4.3). The scalar Z row
also stays in its own final block: Z_t reads internal stage bonds,
and C^(t) on those bonds reads only descendant blocks inside
that parent block. These are full nonlinear coefficient-support
claims, not merely support at U=1.

The final scalar blocks partition the fine sites, so distinct B
rows have disjoint scalar supports. In the invariant bilinear
pairing each Ad(P_hier) obeys Ad(P_hier)Ad(P_hier)^T=I.
Thus, for real AND complex backgrounds in the germ,

                    B B^T=K^-d I.                       (5.2)

This is exact, not an O(background) estimate. In dimension four,
the scalar-kernel projector therefore has the explicit local form

             P_B=I-K^4 B^T B.                           (5.3)

It is symmetric and idempotent, orthogonal on real fields, and
holomorphic on the complex germ. It has one-final-block background
and matrix support, and exact scalar gauge covariance
P_B(U^g)=O_S(g)P_B(U)O_S(g)^T.
The normalized term (1/2)logdet BB^T is identically zero.

6. Flat values, analytic domains and uniformity

At U=1 the primitive contour derivative in Z is exactly the root
term of the actual ordinary arithmetic differential. Subtracting
the two endpoint terms in (2.2) leaves

 (Cbar_1 x)(c)=ell^-d sum_(z in B_ell(c_-))
                                 x([z,z+ell e_mu]),
 B_1=Q_(0,ell).                                         (6.1)

Thus at the full endpoint

 Cbar(1)=A_K^st,        B(1)=Q_(0,K).                    (6.2)

Here Cbar is an angle map: its primitive factor is ell times the
normalized physical-potential average. The scalar average has no
extra ell factor. Products in (6.2) are straight-string and
site-block averages by exact centered-block tiling.

At fixed K, each composite coefficient reads finitely many links
in one or two final blocks and is a composition of analytic local
maps. A sufficiently small link-angle product ball therefore works
uniformly in volume and in unrelated old depth M. Its radius and
derivative constants may depend on K. Cauchy bounds preserve the
same finite support. Source tori with the prescribed local lifts
and nested blocks preserve all incidence counts.

There is a stronger available physical-background bound if desired.
For U=exp(iA/K) in the previously proved small globally bounded
physical-potential ball, the ordinary intermediate angles are
O(ell^j ||A||/K). Each fixed-ell primitive moving operator differs
from its flat value by that amount in the physical absolute Schur
norm. Summing ell^j/K gives a bounded geometric series. Therefore
B has uniform physical bounds and

 ||B-Q_(0,K)||_(count,2->2)<=C ||A||_infty K^-2
                                                       (d=4). (6.3)

The corresponding angle-bond Cbar bound has counting scale K^-1.
These stronger estimates use the actual ordinary-reference
trajectory theorem; they are not necessary for fixed-K volume
uniformity or the exact algebra (5.2).

The older FIXED-identity corrected induced scalar map is a different
object. Its one-step logarithm Jacobians in ng-one-step-gauge give

7. Basis-free consequence for the general tangent determinant bridge

Assume the separately proved finite tangent identity, with a
bilinear orthonormal frame V of ker B,

 D_actual=N_0[logdet G_B-(1/2)logdet Q_B
                              +(1/2)logdet BB^T],
 G_B=V^T D_all^T D_all V,
 Q_B=H_res+D_all P_B D_all^T+a Cbar^T Cbar.                (7.1)

No global integrability of Z is required for that tangent identity.
The actual residual H_res and the actual ordinary C remain those
of the original constrained stationary problem. Our construction
has not replaced that problem by a different corrected nonlinear
constraint.

Let G_all=D_all^T D_all and define on ALL scalar sites

 Ghat_B=P_B G_all P_B+K^-2(I-P_B).                        (7.2)

Its two projector subspaces reduce it. Hence

 det Ghat_B=det G_B (K^-2)^(rank B).                      (7.3)

Rank B is fixed by (5.2), and its scalar power disappears under
N_0. Equations (5.2),(7.1)--(7.3) give

 D_actual=N_0[logdet Ghat_B-(1/2)logdet Q_B].             (7.4)

Both ambient matrices in (7.4) are exactly gauge covariant.
For Ghat_B use the covariance of P_B and D_all; for Q_B use
the residual covariance together with Cbar and P_B.
This removes the need to choose gauge-covariant frames V.
In particular, once their real flat gaps and a fixed-K uniform
complex perturbation neighborhood have been established, their
local diagonal logarithm traces are individually gauge invariant
by the same conjugation argument as Notebook33.

At flatness G_B is the scalar Laplacian on functions with zero
average in each K-block. Elementary block Neumann Poincare gives
gap cK^-2, and the complement in Ghat_B has gap K^-2.
The Q_B flat gap, uniform old-M action inputs, and resulting
analytic/logarithm bounds are independent spectral tasks; they
must not be inferred merely from locality or covariance here.

Unlike the earlier globally based factors, this formulation has

It still does not compute the large-K vector Maxwell coefficient
