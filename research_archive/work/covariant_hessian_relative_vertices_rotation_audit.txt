STATUS. Sections 2--3 of the source have a sound formal local-gauge
mechanism. Its original Section4 identity (4.1) was false and cannot
be used. The repair below proves the desired estimate with an
explicit gradient-gradient term and one additional hypothesis:

1. Demonstrated error in the original identity

A transpose-symmetric convolution obeys q_ab(r)=q_ba(-r).
It need not obey q_ab(r)=q_ba(r). The expression
 sum_(a,b) q_ab(r) [F_r v_a]^T F_r v_b
depends only on the a,b-symmetric part of q(r).
Thus the claimed exact difference-square expression discards its
odd-r, skew-component part. Reversing r and exchanging a,b does
not fix this. The standard forward-difference Maxwell symbol
|d(p)|^2 I-d(p)d(p)^* already has the relevant shifted off-diagonal
entries. This is the same issue repaired earlier by ordered Laurent
factorization in low_kernel_spatial_transport_research.md.

2. Correct flat factorization

Let q have an exponentially summable matrix kernel with a uniform
row AND column norm at exponent sigma>0. Suppose
 q(1)=0 and every first Laurent derivative of q at 1 is zero.
The site-tail Fourier symbol of actual q_M has these properties:
q_mid(p)=|p|^2 I-pp^T+O(p^4), uniformly in M, and the analytic
midpoint phase change preserves vanishing through degree one.

For a matrix Laurent series F define
 B_j F(z)=
 [F(1,...,1,z_j,...,z_d)-F(1,...,1,1,z_(j+1),...,z_d)]/(z_j-1).
The singularity is removable. Telescoping twice gives
 q(z)=sum_(rho,tau) (z_rho^-1-1) H_rho,tau(z)(z_tau-1),
 H_rho,tau=-z_rho B_tau B_rho q.                       (2.1)
No symmetry at fixed displacement is needed.

A Laurent monomial quotient costs at most |r_j| terms of exponent
length at most |r|. Spend sigma/4 twice. The combined block kernel
norm at exponent sigma/2 is bounded, for example, by
 16 exp(sigma/2)/(e^2 sigma^2) times the original norm.
Replace H by (H+H^*)/2, where * interchanges its full gradient/bond
component indices AND reverses displacement. Since q=q^*, (2.1)
is unchanged. H is now a symmetric exponentially local operator.
Color diagonality of q is preserved by this construction.

In operator notation, with D0 the ordinary componentwise gradient
of a bond field,
                  q=D0^T H D0.                        (2.2)

3. A covariant comparison operator

Choose reverse-consistent lattice paths between every pair of sites
as in source Section2. Let W_xy(a) be adjoint transport. Define
 H^P(a)_(x,rho,alpha;y,tau,beta)
       =H_(rho,alpha;tau,beta)(y-x) W_xy(a).
Because H is color scalar and symmetric, H^P is symmetric and
sitewise gauge covariant. Its zero, first and second background
Taylor kernels have uniform exponentially rooted bounds: each
differentiation marks one or two edges of a path of length |x-y|,
and polynomial multiplicities are paid by an exponential reserve.

Let D_a be the nearest-neighbor covariant gradient acting on each
bond component at its source site, with the same gauge convention.
Then
                  K(a)=D_a^T H^P(a)D_a                 (3.1)
is symmetric, exactly covariant, analytic through the required
orders, and K(0)=q. It has the same type of uniform rooted current
Taylor bounds as the source family L(a). Positivity of H or K is
not required.

The source's local formal path-gauge comparison applies separately
to L and K. More explicitly, at each row x use the same formal
g_x with g_x(x)=I. For a(t)=t mu A, with
 ||A||_infty+mu^-1||nearest differences A||_infty<=a_*,
the transformed link coefficients obey
 |b_1(z)|<=C mu^2 a_* (1+|z-x|)^2,
 |b_2(z)|<=C mu^2 a_*^2 (1+|z-x|)^2.                    (3.2)
The first is discrete Stokes; the second follows by counting
quadratic products in the loop product/logarithm. The same estimates
hold for complex color components.

Since L and K have the same flat kernel, their endpoint-dressed
flat comparison is the same q^P. Therefore the source Section3
argument, applied twice, yields
 E_j^rem=[t^j](L(tmu A)-K(tmu A)),
 ||E_1^rem||_Schur<=C mu^2 a_*,
 ||E_2^rem||_Schur<=C mu^2 a_*^2.                       (3.3)
This route avoids a potentially delicate termwise identification
of the different transport paths. Equivalently their difference
is made of closed loops, whose first two jets have (3.2).

The formal g_x need not lie in a single globally bounded analytic
gauge domain. Truncate its polynomial jets far from x, apply the
actual finite covariance identity, and pass in each of the first
two Taylor coefficients using the exponential source moments.
No unbounded gauge transformation is performed on a finite analytic
functional. Boundary truncation terms have polynomial size and an
exponentially vanishing rooted tail.

4. Corrected coefficient decomposition and relative bound

Write
 D_a=D0+t U1+t^2 U2+O(t^3),
 H^P(a)=H+t H1+t^2 H2+O(t^3).
Nearest-neighbor products and path moments give
 ||U1||<=C mu a_*,       ||U2||<=C mu^2 a_*^2,
 ||H1||<=C mu a_*,       ||H2||<=C mu^2 a_*^2.           (4.1)
The H_j are symmetric. Exact expansion of (3.1) gives
 L1=D0^T H1 D0+D0^T Z1+Z1^T D0+E1,
 L2=D0^T H2 D0+D0^T Z2+Z2^T D0+E2,                    (4.2)
where
 Z1=H U1,
 Z2=H U2+H1 U1,
 E1=E1^rem,
 E2=U1^T H U1+E2^rem.                                 (4.3)
These formulas retain the gradient-gradient term and every
multiplicity. Consequently
 ||Z1||<=C mu a_*,    ||E1||<=C mu^2 a_*,
 ||Z2||<=C mu^2 a_*^2,||E2||<=C mu^2 a_*^2.             (4.4)

For R0=Delta_bond+mu^2 I, ||D0 R0^-1/2||<=1 and
||R0^-1/2||<=mu^-1. Equations (4.1)--(4.4) prove
 ||R0^-1/2 Lj R0^-1/2||<=C_j a_*^j, j=1,2.             (4.5)
Mixed second coefficients follow by polarization in the complex
directions. Constants are uniform in M and K, given the stated
uniform action-kernel inputs. One could absorb half of H_j D0
into Z_j using the fixed bound on D0, but (4.2) records the
derivative structure more transparently.

5. Localization audit

The first two coefficients of D_a and H^P(a) use only their
nearest-neighbor edges and the chosen endpoint paths. Path
multiplicities are polynomial. The H and original L exponential
kernels pay all resulting endpoint/source trees after decreasing
the exponent. In (3.2) a far source on a gauge path requires its
endpoint z, or the matrix endpoint y, to be at comparable distance
from the fixed row x. Thus the original rooted exponential bounds
pay the additional paths in the remainder as well.

For fields supported in fixed smooth physical collars, complete
field C1 norms provide the mu^2 curvature factor in (3.2).
No sharp indicator is differentiated. Source labels can be rooted
on those collars, and the matrix block bounds follow by summing
fine-coordinate Schur estimates, with no K^4 fiber count.
Conjugating with exp(theta f(x/K)) costs exp(C theta mu times
path length), paid by the same reserve. A gradient commuted past
this weight produces O(theta mu); cross terms retain the
required powers in (4.1)--(4.4), including the H_j term.

6. Application scope and verdict

The repaired proof controls the actual action Hessian family
through second physical-background order, using the actual
uniform support/Cauchy bounds, covariance and q_M normalization.
It does not control an arbitrary hard block projection, prove a
uniform bound for rough physical potentials, or by itself identify
the full soft-vector determinant correction. That last comparison
still needs its exact operator identity and the separately proved
Cbar/source estimates.
