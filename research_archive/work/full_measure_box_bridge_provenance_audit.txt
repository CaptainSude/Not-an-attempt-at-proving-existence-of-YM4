PASS. No mathematical correction requested.

1. Boundary gauge.
The boundary cubical complex is S3. Its two-skeleton has trivial
fundamental group because adding cells of dimensions >=3 does not
change pi1. Therefore the finite graph's fundamental loops are in
the normal closure of boundary plaquette relations. Choosing one
finite word for each non-tree edge gives a finite L_R. The unitary
telescoping estimate is valid for the normalized Hilbert--Schmidt
norm: multiplication and conjugation by unitaries preserve it.
No bound on the conjugating words is necessary. This is correctly
restricted to fixed geometry, and uses boundary plaquettes only.

2. Boundary-to-interior representative.
The union of the chosen boundary spanning tree and the boundary-rooted
forest is a spanning tree of the full graph. An arbitrary extension
of the boundary gauge differs from another by a gauge transformation
identity on the boundary. Unique forest normalization removes that
difference. Under an original unrestricted gauge transformation, the
full-tree representative changes only by conjugation at its root.

The revised fbw definition explicitly uses the Ad-invariant edge-max
Lie-algebra norm. Its boundary neighborhood can be reduced to an
Ad-invariant product ball. Uniqueness of the conditional minimizer
then proves its equivariance, and the principal logarithm respects
conjugation. Hence the resulting full-box event is gauge invariant.
The norm convention is essential; a maximum of unrelated scalar
color coordinates would not give exact gauge invariance.

3. Threshold normalization.
h(P)<epsilon_R^2 implies ||P-I||_tau<sqrt(2)epsilon_R.
The boundary gauge estimate therefore puts every boundary edge within
sqrt(2)L_R epsilon_R, exactly the margin used in the fragment.
The boundary threshold is fixed once R is fixed. No mesh-uniform
physical regularity is inferred from it.

4. Conditional independence.
Conditioning on the complement of all integrated interior edge sets
fixes every boundary edge and its gauge. Every plaquette incident to
one integrated set lies entirely in that box. Pairwise vertex-disjoint
boxes have no shared such plaquette, so the conditional action is
the sum of the separate box actions plus a fixed constant. Product
Haar factorizes. The forest-window failures are therefore independent
under this conditioning, and each good-boundary conditional
probability is bounded by a_g=Ce^-cT^2.

5. Correlated bad boundaries.
For a subset J of boxes, selecting one offending boundary plaquette
per box has at most N_boundary^|J| choices. Selected plaquettes are
distinct by vertex disjointness. The earlier deterministic coloring
bound gives z_b^(|J|/Q_b) for any such selection, with
Q_b=b^4 binom(4,2). This bound does not assume independent boundary
fields. The shifted-grid construction in fwp is valid for b>=3 and
the stipulated torus periods.

Expanding the choices of boundary-bad versus good-boundary interior
failure, conditioning the latter, and dropping unused good-boundary
indicators gives exactly
 [a_g + N_boundary z_b^(1/Q_b)]^m.
No factors of m or unexplained decorrelation terms are missing.

6. Weak-coupling asymptotics and scope.
The fixed epsilon_R^2/g^2 cost dominates the finite b^4 log(1/g)
normalization cost. The logarithmic conditional window with power
a>1/2 is also superpolynomially rare. The joint bracket has the
claimed asymptotic at fixed R,b. Constants depend on the box and
cannot be read as uniform for boxes growing with refinement.

The event belongs to the complete bare Wilson measure and uses a
conditional unconstrained minimizer. It is not the nonlinear
coarse-average fiber event. The fragment correctly retains the
unproved average-fiber normalization, mixed-history, physical
regularity and external-source interfaces. No continuum measure
claim follows from this local full-measure estimate.
