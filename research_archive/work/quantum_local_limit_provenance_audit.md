## 1. Fixed final carriers contain every spatial matrix index

The strong statement proposed by root is valid for the actual strict precision expansion. Each precision matrix B_Y has both rows and columns in Y. A covariance path in

    C0 B_(Y1) C0 ... B_(Yr) C0

begins and ends at cells of the marked Gaussian marginal M. Every intermediate C0 endpoint is a row or column of one of the displayed B_Y factors and hence belongs to its declared background support Y. The retained background union X contains all these Y, and the final connected carrier H contains M union X. Therefore a term contributing to one fixed H has all its matrix indices inside H. The baseline C0 entry is evaluated as an entry; its value is not expanded into unrecorded random-walk paths.

The inverse of the tree-deformed baseline marginal is a finite matrix indexed by M itself. It likewise has no external index. Interaction fluctuation supports are included in the marked set, and their background supports are retained in the final union. Thus the candidate's conditional paragraph about potentially hidden endpoints can be replaced by this actual support-incidence fact. At fixed H there is no remaining unbounded spatial index sum. Winding terms cannot retain a fixed small lifted final carrier once the period is large and the deterministic hull choices agree locally.

## 2. Strict marked-order tails really follow from the construction

Choose a common dilation radius 1+delta>1 inside every strict sufficient budget. Every nonempty cutoff-log polymer has at least one q/w mark. Molecule order, forest edges and the number of polymers in a Mayer term are bounded by the number of such occurrences. In the density correction, each nonconstant inverse, determinant or exponential factor begins at positive precision order. The baseline finite marginal density is evaluated directly. This is the needed normal tail statement; a bound only on the final undilated output would not prove it.

The external logdet correction has its separate strictly geometric trace series. It is treated by the same precision-order argument, independently of the nonempty marked Gaussian logarithm.

## 3. Local labels must not permit escape at fixed support

After spatial and order truncation, finitely many choices remain if there are finitely many aggregated label types per carrier. This is true of the actual construction when equal-carrier exponent functions are summed before exponentiation: there is one precision coefficient per Y and the fixed finite base/inherited types per interaction support. The canonical support series may be used to prove convergence of these aggregate functions before invoking the Gaussian expansion.

If a formulation allows an unbounded auxiliary label set with the same declared support, it must impose a uniform summable label-tail condition or perform this aggregation. Without it, the asserted continuity is false as stated for arbitrary labels: on one fixed singleton, put a fixed nonzero multiplier on label n=N and zero on every other label. Every fixed label converges to zero and the norm stays bounded, but the total interaction does not converge to the zero interaction. This is not an obstruction to the actual aggregated construction; it explains exactly why its labeling convention should be recorded.

## 6. Explicit repair for general finite histories

The fixed-representative limit in Section4 starts from a covariant infinite family. An arbitrary actual finite sequence may instead converge coefficientwise to a noncovariant representative. Local normal continuity alone does not identify the two canonical coefficient constructions merely from equality of their limiting total class.

Here is a finite exact repair when the actual finite totals have the established J-cell translation invariance. Average the finite carrier family over the finite torus translation group. The anchored norm does not increase. Its raw total is exactly unchanged: translation invariance of the normalized total also gives invariance of the raw total, since its value at zero is unchanged. The averaged family is covariant. Extract its local limit jointly with the original one; normalized-total compactness shows that the covariant limit represents the same class as the original limit.

At every finite regulator, the original and averaged input totals give exactly equal normalized finite Gaussian outputs, with the same other data and branch. The continuity lemma supplies local limits for both coefficient constructions, so their normalized limiting totals agree. The averaged covariant limit can now be compared with its canonical periodizations, since these have eventual fixed-carrier equality and the same uniform bounds. Their output limits are the same construction from the same local data.

This argument gives the varying-history extension as a statement about classes, even if canonical coefficient arrays differ. It uses exact finite translation invariance, compatible domains and windows, the common classical system, and the same local-normal continuity theorem. It does not identify arbitrary unrelated finite histories lacking these hypotheses. If the finite averaged arrays require a further subsequence, their scalar limiting class is independent of that choice by the already proved covariant quotient descent.
