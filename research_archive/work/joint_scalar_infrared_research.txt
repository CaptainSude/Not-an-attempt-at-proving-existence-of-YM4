JOINT SCALAR INFRARED LIMIT: A REGULATED RESULT AND AN EXACT OBSTRUCTION
17 October 2026. Research proof, provenance.

Status. This note proves an ordered positive-mass scalar limit and an
exact failure of the reverse order. The latter occurs on a genuinely
periodic constant connection and uses no affine magnetic flux on a
torus. The exact physical-quotient identity then forces compensation
in the physical determinant. None of this proves the desired full
vector/ghost magnetic comparison.

1. Spaces and mass convention

Fix K=ell^k, final torus side N, and dimension four. Current fine sites
have physical spacing epsilon=1/K and side KN; coarse sites have
spacing one and side N. Color coordinates are trace orthonormal.
For unitary links U let G_all(U) be the covariant scalar lattice
Laplacian on ALL sites. The globally based scalar G_b(U) is the
principal minor obtained by deleting all color coordinates at one
common physical base o. Its diagonal is unchanged by the deletion.

The common PHYSICAL mass delta>0 is inserted as

 H_f,delta=K^2 G_all,f+delta^2 I,
 H_c,delta=G_all,c+delta^2 I.

Their principal minors are denoted H_f,delta^b,H_c,delta^b. Define
the normalized scalar half-ratio

 S_delta,N^b = (1/2) N_0[
               logdet H_f,delta^b-logdet H_c,delta^b].

Multiplying the fine massless operator by K^2 changes only a
background-independent determinant normalization. At positive delta
the physical fine mass in counting units is delta/K; using the same
counting mass on both lattices would be a different comparison.

This mass insertion is a specified scalar comparison family.
It is NOT automatically an exact mass deformation of the whole
finite physical-quotient identity of Notebook34.

2. Exact base correction and its positive-mass bounds

For either lattice put H=epsilon^-2 G_all+delta^2 I and R=H^-1.
Block Jacobi/coarea gives exactly

 det H^b = det H det R_oo,                               (2.1)

where R_oo is a dim(g)-by-dim(g) color block. This identity holds
on the real positive domain and extends to its analytic germ.
Consequently

 S_delta,N^b=S_delta,N^all
       +(1/2) N_0[logdet(R_f)_oo-logdet(R_c)_oo].          (2.2)

At real backgrounds H is between delta^2 I and
(16 epsilon^-2+delta^2) I. In particular R_oo has eigenvalues
between (16 epsilon^-2+delta^2)^-1 and delta^-2.
On a sufficiently small complex product neighborhood of flatness,
depending on delta and epsilon but not N, accretivity and
invertibility of R_oo persist. The normalized logarithm of this
fixed color block is uniformly bounded on a smaller such domain.
This follows directly from the resolvent identity and the stated
lower bound for R_oo; its first two derivatives are uniformly bounded
for bounded link directions. For example

 D R[v]=-R (D H[v]) R,
 D^2 R[v,w]=R H'[v]R H'[w]R+R H'[w]R H'[v]R-R H''[v,w]R,

and differentiation of logdet R_oo uses the usual finite color
trace. Every norm on the right is uniform in volume at fixed
delta,epsilon. There is no assertion of uniformity as delta->0.

There is also a rooted exponential version. Conjugate by a site
weight whose nearest-neighbor ratio differs from one by O(kappa).
For kappa small depending on delta,epsilon, the change in H has
operator norm at most delta^2/4. The weighted inverse is bounded
by 2/delta^2. Spending a smaller exponent gives ordinary absolute
row and column Schur bounds. The source derivatives H',H'' are
local. Their resolvent chains begin and end at o, so the first
two coefficient kernels of logdet R_oo are exponentially summable
in all source distances from o, with any fixed polynomial moments.
In particular their total second-moment norm is at most
C_delta,K, independently of N.

This proves two distinct useful consequences:

 (a) A base receding from a fixed localized source changes the
     first-two local coefficients by an exponentially small amount.
 (b) Averaging the based scalar functional over simultaneous coarse
     translations of its base gives a translation-covariant functional.
     Its difference from the unbased functional has an anchored
     second-moment norm at most C_delta,K/N^4.

For (b), translate the rooted base kernel, sum over the N^4 possible
coarse bases, and divide by N^4. Reanchoring a Hessian row at one
fixed output cell converts this to the full rooted source sum;
polynomial displacement weights are absorbed by the same exponential
bound. The fine and coarse bases are translated together. No
claim is made that this finite averaging leaves the unaveraged
finite functional exactly unchanged.

The same statements hold after composition with the actual fixed-K
stationary branch whenever its already proved localized source
kernels are used. Constants are then uniform in old M under the
Notebook33 inputs, still depending on K and delta. This is a
positive-mass result, not a bound on the separately massless factors.

3. A well-defined ordered Maxwell limit

For delta>0 the unbased diagonal logarithm has exponentially localized
Taylor kernels. This follows from the same resolvent proof, applied
to the logarithm integral; the spectral lower bound controls its
large resolvent parameter and small parameter ends. Its color trace
is gauge invariant. Thus its Hessian has the usual zero gradient
moments, and its affine curvature coefficient is defined by
absolutely convergent polynomial moments of its localized kernel.

There is no need for an affine flux family on a finite torus.
One can first use compactly supported link directions, pass to the
infinite kernel, and then evaluate polynomial directions by these
exponential moments. Equivalently use symmetric short lifts of
finite periodic kernels, then let N grow. At fixed delta,K, winding
terms in the unbased scalar kernel disappear exponentially in N;
the averaged base correction vanishes by Section2. The two operations
give the same localized second moment. Inserting the actual
stationary branch uses the fixed-K structural compactness and
polynomial-jet identification from Notebook33; every such limit
has the same scalar coefficient below.

Normalize the Maxwell functional by beta(F)=2[t^2] of the localized
constant-curvature density. The Cartan probes are current links
exp(i t x_nu H/K^2), coarse links exp(i t y_nu H), mu!=nu,
tau(H^2)=1. The unpinned scalar heat coefficient already proved is

 h(s)=(C_H/3) s^2 exp(-8s) I_0(2s)^2 I_1(2s)^2,
 h(s)=O(s^4) at zero, h(s)=alpha_g+O(s^-1) at infinity,
 alpha_g=C_H/(48 pi^2).

The K^4 fine sites per final block cancel the curvature factor K^-4.
In common physical heat time u the two coefficients are h(K^2u)
and h(u). Therefore the thermodynamic positive-mass scalar coefficient
is exactly

 beta_delta(K)=(1/2) integral_0^infinity
          exp(-delta^2 u)[h(K^2u)-h(u)] du/u.             (3.1)

The first limit N->infinity is at fixed delta>0 and fixed K.
The paired integrand without its mass factor is absolutely
integrable: at zero use h(s)=O(s^4), and at infinity use cancellation
of alpha_g. Hence

 lim_(delta->0) beta_delta(K)=alpha_g log K.              (3.2)

Indeed integrating on epsilon<u<T and changing variables gives
the unhalved difference
integral_T^(K^2T)h(v)dv/v-integral_epsilon^(K^2epsilon)h(v)dv/v.
The limits are 2 alpha_g log K and zero.

There is a useful reference-only uniform mass error. For K>=1 and
0<delta<=1/2,

 |beta_delta(K)-alpha_g log K|
               <= C delta^2[1+log(1/delta)],             (3.3)

with C independent of K. On 0<u<1 use the boundedness of h and
1-exp(-delta^2u)<=delta^2u. On u>=1,
|h(K^2u)-h(u)|<=2C/u. Splitting at u=delta^-2 gives (3.3).
This uniform bound concerns the explicit scalar reference after
the thermodynamic limit. It does not interchange that limit with
mass removal for the original finite based determinants.

4. Exact failure when the mass is removed first

Consider one charged scalar q on a periodic n^4 lattice, with a
constant link phase exp(i t q A) in direction mu. This is a valid
periodic flat connection for every t. Its unbased eigenvalues are

 lambda_p(t)=2 sum_nu[1-cos(p_nu+t q A delta_mu,nu)],
                         p in (2pi/n)Z_n^4.

Let V=n^4 and delete one scalar site to obtain G_n^b(t). Translation
invariance and Jacobi give, initially away from zero eigenvalues,

 det G_n^b(t)=V^-1 product_(p!=0)lambda_p(t)
        [1+lambda_0(t) sum_(p!=0)lambda_p(t)^-1].         (4.1)

Both sides continue analytically through t=0. Since
lambda_0(t)=q^2 A^2 t^2+O(t^4),

 [t^2]logdet G_n^b(t)=q^2 A^2 sum_(p!=0)
  [cos(p_mu)/lambda_p
        -2 sin^2(p_mu)/lambda_p^2+1/lambda_p],           (4.2)

with lambda_p=lambda_p(0).
The first two summands equal partial_pmu(sin(p_mu)/lambda_p).
They are O(|p|^-2); their Brillouin-zone integral is zero, since
the boundary flux after removing a small radius-epsilon ball is
O(epsilon^2). Their grid sums converge to that integral:

 n^-4 sum_(0<|p|<=epsilon) |p|^-2 <= C epsilon^2+O(n^-2).

The same integrability estimate applies to lambda_p^-1. Thus

 lim_(n->infinity) V^-1[t^2]logdet G_n^b(t)
        =q^2 A^2 g_4 >0 for qA!=0,                     (4.3)
 g_4=integral_[-pi,pi]^4 dp/(2pi)^4
                       [2 sum_nu(1-cos p_nu)]^-1.

At any fixed positive counting mass m, the unbased normalized
log-determinant coefficient instead tends to the integral of
partial_pmu[sin(p_mu)/(lambda_p+m^2)], which is zero by periodicity.
Its base correction has O(1) derivatives by Section2, so disappears
after division by V. Taking m->0 afterward still gives zero.

The two limit orders therefore differ already at the quadratic
constant-link jet. Rank of the deleted block alone does not make
its massless derivative contribution negligible. Equation (4.1)
shows precisely the contribution of the soft global mode.

As a numerical check, the expression (4.2)/V for qA=1 is about
0.1517945, 0.1534983, 0.1544102, 0.1547282, 0.1548420 at
n=8,12,20,32,48. These checks are not used in the proof.
