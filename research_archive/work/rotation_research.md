# Rotation restoration: counterexamples and a conditional scalar theorem

Research note, 16 October 2026. Results proved below are separated from inputs not proved for four-dimensional Yang–Mills. This is not a Yang–Mills construction or a certification of the complete supplied manuscript.

## 1. Conclusions

1. Replacing Gaussian domination in the **unweighted \(L^2\) norm** by unspecified factorial Schwartz bounds is false, already for scalar Gaussian laws. An explicit counterexample is anisotropic derivative white noise.
2. Restricting to tests away from coincidences removes that first example, but does not repair the generalization. A fourth-order hypercubic differential descendant of a massive free scalar is reflection positive at the required mirrors and has excellent Schwartz moment bounds, yet is anisotropic away from coincidence.
3. A conditional scalar rotation theorem is proved below: entire angular moments of type at most \(\kappa N\), with **\(\kappa<4\)**, are rotation independent if they have the specified imaginary-angle positive kernels in coordinate and 45-degree frames and their leading coefficients can be detected across every split.
4. The threshold is strict for a reason. The differential-descendant example has angular type \(4N\), with maximal even-order frequencies \(r_{2k}=8k\).
5. For a renormalized scalar such as \(\operatorname{tr}F^2\), \(\kappa=3\) in four dimensions is a **power-counting target**, not an estimate established here.

The counterexamples concern an observable declared to transform as a scalar. An anisotropic descendant can belong to a covariant underlying theory while failing the asserted scalar transformation law.

## 2. The precise role of closedness in the supplied proof

Theorem A of the supplied 18-page rotation note assumes \(dF=0\), Gaussian \(L^2\) domination, translations and signed coordinate permutations, and reflection positivity across \(t=0\) and \(t+x=0\) [R, p.2].

Closedness is used as follows:

- Pullbacks preserve the null tests \(d^*\eta\) [R, p.2, equation (10)].
- The angular coefficient forms annihilate those tests. The no-contact lemma therefore extends their frequency bound from disjoint planar supports to all \(L^2\) tests [R, p.10, §4.5].
- The same lemma detects a nonzero coefficient on separated supports, so it can be placed across a time cut with any prescribed split [R, p.10].
- Appendix B proves this no-contact lemma using planar locality and codifferentials [R, pp.17–18].

The spectral cone [R, pp.4–6], source and insertion estimates [R, pp.13–16], and analytic continuation on separated planar configurations [R, pp.7–9] do not themselves use \(dF=0\). Thus an appropriately specified theorem about separated kernels can replace closedness by direct coefficient detection. This does **not** establish the needed kernel bounds for composite Yang–Mills observables.

There are two different contact sets. The supplied argument initially separates the projections onto a chosen \((t,x)\) plane. Its exceptional set is the union of **planar** coincidence sets, larger than the full spacetime coincidence set. They must not be silently identified. Strictly time-ordered OS test supports avoid both.

## 3. Counterexample I: anisotropic contact noise

Let \(d\ge2\), and let \(W_1,\ldots,W_d\) be independent real Gaussian white noises. Define
\[
 X(f)=\sum_{j=1}^d W_j(\partial_j^2f),\qquad f\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R^d).
\]
The covariance and characteristic functional are
\[
 Q(f,g)=\sum_j\int \partial_j^2f\,\partial_j^2g,\qquad
 \Phi(f)=\exp[-Q(f,f)/2].
\]
The covariance multiplier is \(P_4(p)=\sum_jp_j^4\).

### Proposition 3.1

The law is translation and hypercubic invariant and reflection positive across every affine coordinate or 45-degree diagonal mirror. It is not fully rotation invariant. It has Gaussian bounds in a Schwartz seminorm and factorial moments, but no Gaussian domination by \(C\|f\|_2^2\).

**Proof.** Translation and signed-permutation invariance follow from the multiplier. Every specified reflection has a signed-permutation linear part and therefore preserves the law.

Let \(\mathcal A_+\), \(\mathcal A_-\) be the sigma-algebras generated by fields supported on opposite open sides of such a mirror. Derivatives preserve support; thus \(Q(f,g)=0\) for tests on opposite sides. Gaussianity gives independence of \(\mathcal A_+\), \(\mathcal A_-\). For positive-side \(L^2\) observables,
\[
 \mathbb E[\overline{A(\theta X)}B(X)]
 =\overline{\mathbb EA}\,\mathbb EB.
\]
Every finite matrix of these values is positive semidefinite. This proves full observable reflection positivity, not merely linear-field positivity.

The multiplier takes values \(1\) and \(1/2\) at \(e_1\) and \((e_1+e_2)/\sqrt2\). Fourier-localized real Schwartz tests distinguish the rotated covariance. Finally,
\[
 \mathbb E e^{X(f)}
 =\exp\left[\frac12\sum_j\|\partial_j^2f\|_2^2\right].
\]
High-frequency tests with fixed \(L^2\) norm exclude a fixed \(L^2\) domination constant. Schwartz continuity gives the other bounds; see §5. \(\square\)

**Qualification.** The example is not reflection invariant at an arbitrary tilted plane. Independence across an arbitrary plane alone would not prove full observable reflection positivity there. Only the required finite mirror families are asserted.

### Proposition 3.2: the physical quotient is trivial

For any required mirror, \(\|[A]\|_{\rm OS}^2=|\mathbb EA|^2\). Every centered positive-side observable is null, the OS Hilbert space is one dimensional, and its translation Hamiltonian is zero.

This obstructs invariance of the **full Euclidean probability law**, but not reconstruction of the trivial Wightman theory from its separated correlations.

The covariance kernel is \(\sum_j\partial_j^4\delta\). Every Wick pairing in \(S_N\), \(N\ge1\), is supported on a coincidence set. Thus all its nonvacuum moments vanish on the region of pairwise distinct spacetime points.

Adding this independent noise to an isotropic massive free scalar leaves all correlations on that noncoincidence region unchanged. More generally, for positive-side observables \(A(\phi,X)\), conditional expectation over \(X\) defines the OS isometry
\[
 [A]\longmapsto[\mathbb E_X A(\phi,X)].
\]
Independence of the noise on opposite sides proves equality of inner products. Its range contains the ordinary scalar cylinder observables, so the completed quotient is the same as for \(\phi\). The added anisotropic contact sector is physically invisible in this exact sense.

## 4. Counterexample II: anisotropy away from coincidence

Let \(\phi\) be the centered massive free Euclidean scalar, \(m>0\), with covariance
\[
 \widehat C_m(p)=(|p|^2+m^2)^{-1}.
\]
Set
\[
 P(\partial)=\sum_{j=1}^d\partial_j^4,\qquad
 Y(f)=\phi(P(\partial)f).
\]
Then
\[
 \widehat C_Y(p)=\frac{P_4(p)^2}{|p|^2+m^2}.
\]

### Proposition 4.1

\(Y\) is translation and hypercubic invariant, reflection positive at the coordinate and diagonal mirrors, and has Gaussian/factorial Schwartz bounds. Its two-point function is not rotation invariant even modulo contact terms.

**Proof.** The local operator \(P\) commutes with each required reflection and preserves halfspace support. Pulling observables of \(Y\) back to observables of \(\phi\) therefore proves reflection positivity, first for cylinders and then by \(L^2\) closure.

Rotate the first two coordinates by \(\pi/4\). If the covariance difference were supported only at \(x=0\), its Fourier transform would be a polynomial. Thus \(|p|^2+m^2\) would divide \(P_4(Rp)^2-P_4(p)^2\). Restricting this polynomial identity to \(p=(z,0,\ldots,0)\) would imply that \(z^2+m^2\) divides \(-3z^8/4\). Evaluating at \(z=im\) disproves this. The covariance difference is nonzero away from coincidence. \(\square\)

The distribution fact used here is elementary: a distribution supported at one point is a finite sum of delta derivatives, so its Fourier transform is a polynomial.

### Nontrivial OS sector and sharp spectral cone

Write \(t=x_1\), \(k\in\mathbb R^{d-1}\), \(\omega(k)=\sqrt{|k|^2+m^2}\), and for a smooth test compactly supported in \(t>0\), put
\[
 Lf(k)=\int_0^\infty e^{-\omega(k)t}\widehat f(t,k)\,dt.
\]
Up to Fourier normalization,
\[
 \|[Y(f)]\|_{\rm OS}^2
 =\int\frac{dk}{2\omega(k)}
 \left(\omega(k)^4+\sum_{a=1}^{d-1}k_a^4\right)^2|Lf(k)|^2.
\]
Integration by parts proves the formula; no boundary terms occur. The multiplier in parentheses is at least \(m^4\). There are many nonzero OS vectors. The one-particle energy is still \(\omega(k)\), and the free Fock reconstruction retains the sharp spectral cone. Thus this example survives in the physical quotient.

The underlying free theory remains covariant. The field \(Y\), declared to be a scalar, is not: rotations mix it with other fourth derivatives. This distinction is precisely what the counterexample demonstrates.

### Exact angular type and saturation of four

Let \(U_\theta f(x)=f(R_\theta^{-1}x)\) and \(P_\theta=U_{-\theta}PU_\theta\). Invariance of \(\phi\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E\prod_jY(U_\theta f_j)
 =\mathbb E\prod_j\phi(P_\theta f_j).
\]
In a coordinate plane \(P_\theta\) has frequencies \(0,\pm4\). Every \(N\)-point angular moment is therefore entire of type at most \(4N\).

The eighth harmonic at order two is nonzero. On planar momentum \(p=(z,0)\),
\[
 P_\theta(p)=z^4(\cos^4\theta+\sin^4\theta)
 =z^4(3+\cos4\theta)/4,
\]
whose square has a nonzero \(\cos8\theta\) coefficient. Fourier-localized real tests retain that coefficient. The Gaussian even moment of one such test is \((2k-1)!!\) times its variance to the \(k\)th power, so its frequency \(8k\) coefficient is nonzero. Hence \(r_{2k}=8k\).

The imaginary-angle Gram property also holds without evaluating Schwartz functions at complex points. The differential operator \(P_{i\beta}\) preserves supports and commutes with the **antilinear** coordinate reflection \(J\). Indeed \(\theta P_\zeta\theta=P_{-\zeta}\) and \(\overline{P_{i\beta}}=P_{-i\beta}\). Ordinary reflection positivity of \(\phi\) applied to \(\phi(P_{i\beta}f)\) gives the desired kernel. The same argument works in the \(\pi/4\) frame because \(P_{\pi/4}\) is even under its coordinate reflection. Thus type \(4N\) is compatible with the two-frame positivity comparison and surviving anisotropy.

The leading coefficients are detectable on separated configurations too. At order two, the frequency-eight multiplier has a nonzero degree-eight numerator whose restriction to a planar axis is a nonzero multiple of \(z^8\); it is not divisible by \(z^2+m^2\). Its coefficient kernel is therefore nonzero off coincidence. Choose a displacement where it is nonzero. At order \(2k\), arrange \(k\) pairs with that displacement and send their centers far apart. Massive free covariances and all their derivatives decay exponentially between different pairs. The leading coefficient consequently tends to the nonzero product of the \(k\) within-pair coefficients, while all cross-pair contractions tend to zero. It is nonzero at some separated configuration. These coefficient densities are real analytic away from coincidences, translation invariant and permutation symmetric. The argument of §7 then supplies detection across every split. Thus the example also tests the detection condition, rather than exploiting its absence.

## 5. Strong factorial Schwartz bounds hold for both examples

Covariance continuity gives a Schwartz seminorm \(p_s\) and \(A<\infty\) such that
\[
 |C(f,g)|\le A^2p_s(f)p_s(g).
\]
Wick's rule yields
\[
 |S_N(f_1,\ldots,f_N)|\le A^N\sqrt{N!}\prod_jp_s(f_j),
\]
with odd moments zero. Here \((2n-1)!!\le\sqrt{(2n)!}\) follows from \(\binom{2n}{n}\le4^n\).

Bounds also hold on **arbitrary tensor Schwartz tests**. Choose an integer \(s>\max(d,4)\), and define
\[
 q_{N,s}(\Psi)=
 \max_{|\alpha_j|\le s}
 \sup_{x_1,\ldots,x_N}
 \prod_j(1+|x_j|^2)^{s/2}
 |\partial_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots\partial_N^{\alpha_N}\Psi|.
\]
There is \(B_s\), independent of \(N\), with
\[
 |S_N(\Psi)|\le B_s^N\sqrt{N!}\,q_{N,s}(\Psi).
\]
For the noise, a pairing differentiates each variable at most twice and identifies paired positions; the weighted integrals are finite. For the descendant, move at most four derivatives per variable onto \(\Psi\). The remaining pair kernels are \(C_m\ge0\), with \(\|C_m\|_1=m^{-2}\). Each weighted pair integral is uniformly finite. Derivative-component choices grow only exponentially in \(N\), in addition to the Wick factor. Both total derivative order and weight order grow linearly in \(N\).

Thus the examples have strong Gaussian/factorial regularity on the full tensor space. They do not exploit moment indeterminacy or undefined distributions at coincidence.

## 6. Conditional scalar angular theorem

This is an implication among stated analytic and positivity hypotheses. It does not assert that Yang–Mills supplies them.

Let \(\mathcal T_N\) be an admissible class of labelled \(N\)-tuples closed under the operations below; it can consist of ordinary test tuples or of separated configurations with transverse smearing. Include the empty tuple. Let \(J\) be the antilinear coordinate reflection and \(\mathcal T^+\) the positive-side tuples of all finite lengths.

For \(T\in\mathcal T_N\), let \(M_N(T;\zeta)\) be an angular moment, and \(\widetilde M_N\) its counterpart in the \(\pi/4\)-tilted frame.

### Hypotheses

**A. Entire angular moments with a strict type bound.** There is a fixed \(0\le\kappa<4\) such that each \(M_N(T;\cdot)\) is entire, \(\pi/2\)-periodic, and
\[
 |M_N(T;\theta+i\beta)|\le C_{N,T}e^{\kappa N|\beta|}
 \quad(0\le\theta\le2\pi,\ \beta\in\mathbb R).
\]
Occurrence of a nonzero frequency \(\ell\) implies occurrence of \(-\ell\), possibly at a conjugated tuple. Also \(M_0=1\).

**B. Exact frame relation.**
\[
 \widetilde M_N(T;\zeta)=M_N(T;\zeta+\pi/4).
\]
This is the identity for a pushed-forward law; it does not assume equality of the two laws.

**C. Two imaginary-angle positive kernels.** For every real \(\beta\), both
\[
 K_\beta(\chi,\eta)=M_{a+b}(J\chi,\eta;i\beta),\qquad
 \widetilde K_\beta(\chi,\eta)=\widetilde M_{a+b}(J\chi,\eta;i\beta),
 \quad a=|\chi|,\ b=|\eta|,
\]
are positive semidefinite on the union of positive-side tuples of **all lengths**, including the empty tuple. Positivity merely at \(\beta=0\), or separately at each fixed length without mixed-length Cauchy–Schwarz, is insufficient.

**D. Detection across every split.** Let \(r_N\) be the largest absolute value of an occurring nonzero frequency at order \(N\), or zero if none occurs. If \(r_{a+b}>0\), there are positive-side tuples \(\chi,\eta\) of lengths \(a,b\) for which the coefficient of \(-r_{a+b}\) in \(M_{a+b}(J\chi,\eta;\zeta)\) is nonzero. This is required for **every** split, including an empty side.

### Theorem 6.1

Under A–D, every \(M_N(T;\zeta)\) is independent of \(\zeta\).

**Proof. Step 1: finite spectrum.** For the ordinary Fourier coefficient
\[
 a_\ell(T)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}M_N(T;\theta)e^{-i\ell\theta}\,d\theta,
\]
shifting the contour to height \(\beta\) gives
\[
 |a_\ell(T)|\le C_{N,T}e^{\kappa N|\beta|+\ell\beta}.
\]
Choose the sign of \(\beta\) opposite to \(\ell\), and let \(|\beta|\to\infty\). Then \(a_\ell=0\) if \(|\ell|>\kappa N\). Quarter-turn periodicity restricts frequencies to \(4\mathbb Z\). Fourier uniqueness and holomorphic uniqueness give a finite sum for every complex angle. Therefore
\[
 r_N\in4\mathbb Z_{\ge0},\qquad r_N\le\kappa N.
\]

**Step 2: mixed-length positivity gives convexity.** When \(r_{a+b}>0\), use the tuples in D. The squared modulus of their mixed entry has a nonzero leading exponential of rate \(2r_{a+b}\) as \(\beta\to+\infty\). Cauchy–Schwarz gives
\[
 |K_\beta(\chi,\eta)|^2
 \le K_\beta(\chi,\chi)K_\beta(\eta,\eta).
\]
The diagonal factors are \(O(e^{r_{2a}\beta})\) and \(O(e^{r_{2b}\beta})\). Hence
\[
 2r_{a+b}\le r_{2a}+r_{2b}. \tag{6.1}
\]
For \(r_{a+b}=0\) this is automatic.

**Step 3: even-order maxima are multiples of eight.** For \(r_{2a}>0\), restrict the kernels to length \(a\), multiply by \(e^{-r_{2a}\beta}>0\), and take \(\beta\to+\infty\). Every finite matrix converges to a positive semidefinite matrix. The first limit has entries \(a_{-r_{2a}}(J\chi,\eta)\). By B, the second is the same kernel multiplied by
\[
 e^{-ir_{2a}\pi/4}=(-1)^{r_{2a}/4}.
\]
If this sign were negative, the kernel and its negative would both be positive semidefinite; every entry would be zero. Detection for the equal split contradicts this. Thus
\[
 r_{2a}\in8\mathbb Z_{\ge0}. \tag{6.2}
\]
The statement also holds when \(r_{2a}=0\).

**Step 4: integer convexity.** Put \(b_j=r_{2j}\). Equation (6.1), with split \((j-1,j+1)\), gives
\[
 2b_j\le b_{j-1}+b_{j+1}\quad(j\ge1).
\]
So \(d_j=b_j-b_{j-1}\) is nondecreasing. Since \(b_0=0\) and \(b_1\ge0\), all \(d_j\ge0\). By (6.2), every \(d_j\) is a multiple of eight. If \(d_{j_0}>0\), then \(d_j\ge8\) for all \(j\ge j_0\), and
\[
 b_j\ge b_{j_0-1}+8(j-j_0+1).
\]
But \(b_j\le2\kappa j\), and \(2\kappa<8\), a contradiction. All \(b_j=0\).

**Step 5: remaining orders.** Equation (6.1) for \((a,b)=(0,N)\) yields
\[
 0\le2r_N\le r_0+r_{2N}=0.
\]
All nonzero frequencies vanish. \(\square\)

### Limits of the conclusion

If all test tuples are included and moments determine the law, this proves rotation invariance of the law. If only separated kernels are included, it proves invariance **only on that domain**. Extension across collisions and OS reconstruction remain separate issues.

The argument needs no uniform control of \(C_{N,T}\) in \(N\), only the uniform strict slope \(\kappa<4\). Further growth estimates can still be necessary for reconstruction or moment determinacy.

## 7. A sufficient geometric condition for detection

D is an input to verify, not an automatic consequence of positivity.

Suppose each nonzero coefficient has a nonzero continuous, real-analytic density on the relevant distinct-planar-point domain, is symmetric under simultaneous permutations, and is invariant under common translations. Suppose all reflected/conjugated labels are allowed and reality supplies both frequency signs.

Then it is nonzero at some configuration with distinct times: such configurations are dense and open. Order the times and, for any split \(a+b=N\), translate a gap between the two groups to zero. Reflect/conjugate the first group. Permutation and translation invariance give precisely a nonzero coefficient at \((J\chi,\eta)\) with both tuples positive. For an empty group translate all points to one side. Small product neighborhoods preserve a nonzero smeared value when smearing is needed. This proves D.

What must first be established is that the coefficient is an actual moment density on that domain and that its imaginary-angle continuation has the required Gram formula. The supplied proof addresses those tasks in [R, §§4.3–4.4, pp.8–9] and [R, Appendix A.7, p.16].

## 8. Analytic input that could give the type bound

One possible sufficient starting point is an ordered-tube construction with scalar insertions smeared only in transverse variables and a bound
\[
 |S_N(Z;f)|\le C_{N,f}
 \big[\min_{j<N}q(z_{j+1}-z_j)\big]^{-\kappa N},
 \qquad q(z)=\operatorname{Re}z_t-|\operatorname{Im}z_x|.
\]
For a scalar there is no tensor rotation cost. Imaginary rotations preserve the tube and satisfy
\[
 q(R_{-i\beta}z)\ge e^{-|\beta|}q(z).
\]
On narrow configurations whose full rotation orbit is covered by the ordered coordinate frames, this gives angular type \(\kappa N\). Gluing, holomorphic uniqueness and identification with the original moment densities must then propagate it.

This is a **reduction**, not a proof that generic Schwartz bounds imply such an estimate. Those bounds do not automatically control field insertions between evolution operators. A real-axis estimate does not automatically give positivity at imaginary angles.

A factor polynomial in \(|\beta|\), multiplying \(e^{\kappa_0N|\beta|}\) with \(\kappa_0<4\), can be absorbed into \(e^{(\kappa_0+\epsilon)N|\beta|}\) for fixed \(N\) and \(\epsilon>0\), with \(\kappa_0+\epsilon<4\). An uncontrolled increase of the exponential type cannot.

## 9. A proved free-Maxwell benchmark: planar exponent three

This section proves the required kind of tube estimate for **separated Wick-square Schwinger kernels of the four-dimensional free Maxwell field**. It does not construct an ordinary Euclidean random variable for a renormalized pointwise square.

Let \(F_I\), \(I=(\mu,\nu)\), \(\mu<\nu\), be the six components of the centered free Maxwell field strength. Its covariance is obtained by applying the exterior derivative in both variables to the massless free-photon covariance. Away from coincident points it is the usual derivative kernel of \((4\pi^2|u|^2)^{-1}\). Its full Fourier covariance is the bounded positive closed-two-form projector, up to a fixed normalization.

Split \(u=(t,x,y)\) with \(y\in\mathbb R^2\). For complex \(z=(z_t,z_x)\) in
\[
 W=\{z:q(z):=\operatorname{Re}z_t-|\operatorname{Im}z_x|>0\},
\]
let \(K_{IJ}(z;y)\) denote the covariance with the transverse displacement \(y\) left real. When \(\operatorname{Re}z_t>0\), the Fourier integral in the time momentum gives
\[
 K_{IJ}(z;y)=
 c\int_{\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2}
 e^{-z_t\omega+i z_x k_x+i k_\perp\cdot y}
 \frac{A_{IJ}(k_x,k_\perp)}{2\omega}\,dk_x\,dk_\perp,
 \quad
 \omega=\sqrt{k_x^2+|k_\perp|^2},
\]
where \(|A_{IJ}(k)|\le C\omega^2\). Possible terms supported at time zero are absent in this open tube. This representation follows by differentiating the scalar propagator before/after its time-momentum integration; two time derivatives can create a contact term, but only at time zero.

### Lemma 9.1

For all \(I,J\) and \(z\in W\),
\[
 \|K_{IJ}(z;\cdot)\|_{L^2(\mathbb R^2)}
 \le C q(z)^{-3}.
 \tag{9.1}
\]
The map \(z\mapsto K_{IJ}(z;\cdot)\) is holomorphic as an \(L^2(\mathbb R^2)\)-valued function.

**Proof.** Put \(q=q(z)>0\), \(r=|k_\perp|\). The modulus of the exponential is bounded by
\[
 e^{-\operatorname{Re}z_t\,\omega+|\operatorname{Im}z_x||k_x|}
 \le e^{-q\omega}.
\]
Therefore the transverse Fourier transform obeys
\[
 |\widehat K_{IJ}(z;k_\perp)|
 \le C\int_{\mathbb R}\sqrt{k_x^2+r^2}\,
 e^{-q\sqrt{k_x^2+r^2}}\,dk_x.
\]
Use
\[
 \sqrt{k_x^2+r^2}\le |k_x|+r,\qquad
 \sqrt{k_x^2+r^2}\ge (|k_x|+r)/\sqrt2.
\]
The integral is at most
\[
 C e^{-qr/\sqrt2}
 \int_{\mathbb R}(|k_x|+r)e^{-q|k_x|/\sqrt2}\,dk_x
 =
 C q^{-2}(4+2\sqrt2\,qr)e^{-qr/\sqrt2}.
\]
By Plancherel, the squared norm is at most a constant times
\[
 q^{-4}\int_{\mathbb R^2}
 (4+2\sqrt2\,q|k_\perp|)^2e^{-\sqrt2 q|k_\perp|}\,dk_\perp
 =C' q^{-6}.
\]
This proves (9.1). Derivatives in \(z_t,z_x\) add powers bounded by powers of \(\omega\). On compact subsets of \(W\), the same exponential majorant gives integrable \(L^2\) derivative bounds, proving Banach-valued holomorphy. No bound on imaginary time or real longitudinal displacement is needed. \(\square\)

Define the formal scalar Wick square
\[
 O(u)=\sum_I:F_I(u)^2:.
\]
For distinct Euclidean points its Schwinger kernels are defined by the Gaussian Wick rule, with pairings within a single Wick vertex omitted. After transverse smearing with scalar profiles \(f_j\in L^2(\mathbb R^2)\), denote these separated kernels by \(S_N^O(Z;f)\).

### Proposition 9.2

For \(N\ge2\), \(z_{j+1}-z_j\in W\), and
\[
 \delta=\min_{j<N}q(z_{j+1}-z_j)>0,
\]
the Wick kernels are holomorphic and satisfy
\[
 |S_N^O(Z;f)|\le C_N\delta^{-3N}
 \prod_{j=1}^N\|f_j\|_2.
 \tag{9.2}
\]
One may take \(C_N\le C^N6^N(2N-1)!!\), after enlarging \(C\).

**Proof.** Each Wick vertex has two legs and self-pairings are excluded. Every pairing graph is therefore a disjoint union of cycles of length at least two; length two means two parallel edges.

Orient an edge between its earlier and later insertion. The superadditivity
\[
 q(z+w)\ge q(z)+q(w)
\]
shows that its tube gap is at least \(\delta\). Lemma 9.1 therefore bounds the \(L^2\) norm of each transverse edge kernel by \(C\delta^{-3}\). Reversing an edge only transposes covariance indices and reverses the displacement, without changing the required estimate.

Consider a cycle of length \(n\ge2\), with transverse kernels \(K_i(y_i-y_{i+1})\), cyclic indices. Its integral is
\[
 \int\prod_{i=1}^n f_i(y_i)K_i(y_i-y_{i+1})\,dy_1\cdots dy_n.
\]
On \(L^2(\mathbb R^2)\), define the Hilbert–Schmidt integral operator
\[
 A_i(y,y')=f_i(y)K_i(y-y').
\]
Direct integration gives the **exact** identity
\[
 \|A_i\|_{\mathfrak S_2}=\|f_i\|_2\|K_i\|_2.
\]
The cycle integral is \(\operatorname{Tr}(A_1\cdots A_n)\), initially for bounded truncated kernels/profiles and then by \(\mathfrak S_2\) approximation. Schatten Hölder and \(\|A\|_{\mathfrak S_n}\le\|A\|_{\mathfrak S_2}\) for \(n\ge2\) give
\[
 |\operatorname{Tr}(A_1\cdots A_n)|
 \le\prod_i\|A_i\|_{\mathfrak S_2}
 \le (C\delta^{-3})^n\prod_i\|f_i\|_2.
\]
The same argument with absolute kernels proves absolute integrability, justifying the kernel integral and its identification with the trace. Multiply over cycles. There are at most \((2N-1)!!\) pairings and \(6^N\) component assignments. This proves (9.2).

Each edge kernel is \(L^2\)-holomorphic by Lemma 9.1; multiplication by a fixed \(f_i\) takes it continuously to \(\mathfrak S_2\). The multilinear trace of a product of at least two \(\mathfrak S_2\) operators is continuous. Hence every cycle, and then every finite Wick sum, is holomorphic. \(\square\)

### What this benchmark establishes

The bound has precisely the planar power \(\kappa=3\). For a complex rotation in the \((t,x)\) plane, \(q(R_{-i\beta}z)\ge e^{-|\beta|}q(z)\), so it gives the estimate \(C_{N,Z,f}e^{3N|\beta|}\) wherever the ordered-tube description applies. There is no tensor-index loss for \(O\): the quadratic contraction over two-form indices is preserved by the complex orthogonal action. This is an algebraic statement about the contraction, not an assumption about rotation invariance of an interacting limiting law.

The calculation uses the explicit free propagator, Wick pairings and an \(L^2\) transverse estimate. It does **not** deduce the bound from the engineering dimension alone. It therefore supplies a real benchmark for the proposed interacting estimate rather than a proof of that estimate.

Tensor reflection signs are already present in the free field-strength covariance. The proof of the absolute bound does not establish reflection positivity by replacing those signs with scalar ones. In the free theory, the corresponding composite positivity can be represented in its positive Fock space. Establishing the **two imaginary-angle Gram identities** by an interacting construction remains a distinct hypothesis of Theorem 6.1.

Finally, separated Wick-square kernels are the claim here. In four Euclidean dimensions, subtraction of the mean alone need not turn the singular pointwise square into an ordinary random tempered distribution with finite smeared variance. No such probability-space assertion is used. Extending composite correlations through coincidence must be treated separately.

## 10. A concrete Yang–Mills target, not an achieved application

For a scalar of nominal dimension \(\Delta\), transverse smearing suggests a planar cost
\[
 \kappa_{\rm target}=\Delta-\frac{d-2}{2}.
\]
For \(\operatorname{tr}F^2\), engineering dimensions \(d=4,\Delta=4\) suggest \(3<4\). Section 9 proves this planar exponent for separated free-Maxwell Wick-square kernels by an explicit calculation. Neither dimensional counting nor that free calculation proves the interacting estimate, a renormalized energy bound, cutoff-independent constants, logarithmic control, or imaginary-angle Gram positivity.

The actual target is:

> For a specified renormalized gauge-invariant scalar family and continuum limit, construct its separated planar kernels and the imaginary-angle positive kernels in both frames, prove detection across every split, and obtain angular type at most \((4-\epsilon)N\) for one \(\epsilon>0\) independent of \(N\).

Theorem 6.1 would then restore rotations on the specified domain. Nontriviality, a mass gap, extension or renormalization at coincidence, uniqueness, and the full gauge-theory interpretation remain independent tasks.
