SOFT SCALAR FACTORIZATION AND A UNIFORM COARSE MAXWELL REMAINDER
17 October 2026. Research proof, root. Pending independent audit.

Purpose. Replace the hard moving projection P Delta P by a soft block
penalty. This is an exact factorization of the ACTUAL scalar determinant
from Notebook35. The additional coarse determinant has a uniformly
bounded affine Maxwell coefficient as K grows. It does not yet compute
the scalar logarithmic coefficient, nor the full vector coefficient.

1. Data and units

Use counting inner products in dimension four, K=ell^k, mu=K^-1.
The current links are exp(i mu A(e)); A is a bounded physical potential
on fine bonds. Let D(A) be the all-site left gauge generator, and let
B(A) be exactly the moving-reference hierarchical average of ccs,
not a new nonlinear constraint. Set

  J(A)=K^2 B(A)^T,  J(A)^T J(A)=I,
  Pi(A)=J(A)J(A)^T,  P(A)=I-Pi(A),
  Delta(A)=D(A)^T D(A),
  T(A)=Delta(A)+mu^2 Pi(A),
  M(A)=mu^2 J(A)^T T(A)^-1 J(A).                       (1.1)

Transpose is the invariant real transpose and its complex-bilinear
continuation. On real backgrounds J is an isometry. Its block y reads
only the background in that same K-block. One block of J has counting
operator norm one at real backgrounds and is K^-2 times an adjoint
transport at each of its K^4 sites. At zero, J embeds normalized block
constants. All estimates below are uniform in compatible finite volume.

Uniform physical input, already available in ccs Section6 and the
ordinary-trajectory theorem: there is an r>0 independent of K on which
J is holomorphic and ||J(A)-J(0)||<=C||A||_infinity. Every block map has
the same bound and depends only on its own block. To see the scale,
primitive intermediate angles are O(ell^j ||A||/K). The logarithmic
departure of every hierarchical adjoint product is bounded by the sum
of those quantities, at most C||A||. Its normalized block column has
K^4 entries of size K^-2; its operator bound has no fiber-dimension loss.
Cauchy gives first and second block derivatives with the same uniform
constants. No smoothness of A beyond its supremum is required here.

2. Exact identity through flatness

Let V be any orthonormal frame for ker B on real backgrounds and put
G_B=V^T Delta V. In the orthogonal splitting [V,J], T has blocks

       [ G_B             V^T Delta J       ]
       [ J^T Delta V     J^T Delta J+mu^2I ].

Since the upper block is invertible near zero, Jacobi/Schur gives

  det G_B = det T det(J^T T^-1 J).

The basis-free scalar Ghat=P Delta P+mu^2Pi has determinant
(mu^2)^dim(S_c) det G_B. Hence the exact identity is

                  det Ghat = det T det M.              (2.1)

All three factors are positive at zero. They have unique normalized
holomorphic logarithms on the common germ. Thus, without separating
massless globally based determinants,

  N_0 logdet Ghat = N_0 logdet T + N_0 logdet M.          (2.2)

Covariance of D and B gives fine-site covariance of T and coarse-site
covariance of M. Their diagonal color traces are individually invariant.
This changes no ordinary constraint, contour, pivot, or Haar factor.

3. Uniform flat ellipticity

Write T0=T(0), Delta0=D0^T D0. Block Neumann Poincare, applied to phi
minus its block mean using INTERNAL block edges, gives directly

  ||P0 phi||^2 <= C_P K^2 ||D0 phi||^2.

The internal derivative of a block constant is zero; no derivative of
a sharp block indicator is taken. Thus

  c(Delta0+mu^2I) <= T0 <= Delta0+mu^2I,                (3.1)
  c=[1+max(C_P,1)]^-1.

For a uniform lower bound on M0, let E be the normalized product-tent
lift of mgs Section4, composed with J0. Then

  J0^T E=I,  ||E||<=C0,  ||D0 E||<=C1 mu.

The Schur complement
  S=J0^T Delta0 J0-J0^T Delta0 V G_B(0)^-1 V^T Delta0 J0
is the minimum scalar energy with prescribed J0^T phi=b. The feasible
lift Eb gives 0<=S<=C1^2 mu^2 I. Consequently

  M0=(I+mu^-2 S)^-1,
  (1+C1^2)^-1 I <= M0 <= I.                           (3.2)

At K=1 use E=I and the bounded unit-lattice gradient. Periodic repeated
interfaces have the multiplicities already checked in the tent proof.

4. Uniform complex domain; no hard-projection vertex bound

Let E_D=D(A)-D0. The bond-local formula gives
||E_D||<=C mu ||A||. Hence, using (3.1),

 ||T0^-1/2 (T(A)-T0) T0^-1/2|| <= C(||A||+||A||^2).   (4.1)

Indeed the two cross terms have factors D0 T0^-1/2 bounded by one
and E_D T0^-1/2 bounded by C||A||; the square term is quadratic.
The penalty difference is mu^2(Pi(A)-Pi0), whose relative norm is
at most C||A||. This proof is valid in the complex-bilinear continuation
by ordinary operator-norm estimates relative to the positive real T0.
It is not a positivity assertion for a complex transpose product.

Shrink r uniformly so the right side is below 1/2. The Neumann inverse
gives ||T(A)^-1||<=C mu^-2. Resolvent identity and ||J(A)-J0||<=C||A||
then give

  ||M(A)-M0||<=C||A||.                                (4.2)

Shrink r again using the constant in (3.2). M and T are holomorphically
invertible on a radius independent of K. The normalized physical
scalar T therefore has uniform raw relative first-two vertices for
bounded physical perturbations. This does NOT assert such a bound for
Ghat=P Delta P+mu^2Pi, which has the proved pure-gauge obstruction.

5. Weighted inverse and coarse source kernels

Here are details ensuring that operator-norm bounds do not hide a
K^4 loss in the ensuing coarse determinant. For a real 1-Lipschitz
function f of the physical position x/K, conjugate fine-site matrices
by e^(theta f). Use its value at the initial site for bond weights and
at the block center for coarse weights. Adjacent fine weights differ
by at most C theta mu; a block has bounded physical diameter.

Conjugated D0 differs from D0 by an operator of norm <=C theta mu.
The conjugated transpose uses the opposite sign of theta; expanding
both factors therefore proves the same relative estimate as (4.1),
with C(theta+||A||) on its right. Conjugated Pi differs from Pi0 by
O(theta+||A||), since J is block-local. Choose theta,r small fixed.
The weighted Neumann inverse supplies, uniformly,

  ||R||_theta <= C mu^-2,
  ||D R||_theta+||R D^T||_theta <= C mu^-1,
  ||D R D^T||_theta <= C,              R=T(A)^-1.        (5.1)

The notation means the corresponding conjugation between site/bond
spaces. These bounds follow by placing T0^-1/2 on each side of the
Neumann inverse, using ||D_theta T0^-1/2||<=C. For RD^T use the other
factor; no self-adjointness of the complex perturbed R is required.
Each may also be restricted between arbitrary final blocks. Choosing
f as physical distance from the source block gives the corresponding
operator-norm block bound with an extra exp[-theta d(x,y)].

J has block norm O(1); hence (5.1) gives
  ||M_xy||<=C exp[-theta d(x,y)],                       (5.2)
with a fixed color fiber and NO factor K^4. Weighted M(A)-M0 is O(r).
The entries of M0 imply, by coarse Schur summation, that its weighted
conjugation differs from M0 by O(theta). Thus the weighted matrices
M(A) retain a common logarithm contour separated from zero, or a
common contracting logarithm series. The same is true for their
resolvents on a fixed contour. Consequently log M has exponential
coarse matrix kernels, uniformly in K and A on a smaller ball.

For completeness localize physical potential directions a_j to fine
bonds with initial block j. Treat each such block as one supremum-norm
Banach space; do not sum over its K^4 independent scalar coordinates.
The local derivatives obey
  ||delta_j D||<=C mu ||a_j||,
  ||delta_j J||<=C||a_j||,
and corresponding second bounds C mu ||a_j||||b_j|| and C||a_j||||b_j||.
The D derivatives have one-edge support, and J derivatives have one-
block support. Mixed coefficients with disjoint block supports vanish
for these primitive maps (a harmless fixed collar can be included).

Differentiate
  M=mu^2 J^T R J,
  delta R=-R(delta T)R,
  delta T=(delta D)^T D+D^T(delta D)
                +mu^2[(delta J)J^T+J(delta J)^T].
Insert local block projections at each differentiated factor. Every
term in delta M has balanced powers of mu by (5.1): for example
mu^2 (J^T R D^T) (delta D) (R J) has norm at most
mu^2 (C mu^-1)(C mu)(C mu^-2)=C.
For two derivatives, use the same expansion once more. Intermediate
DRD^T costs O(1), RD^T or DR costs O(mu^-1), R costs O(mu^-2), while
each delta D supplies mu and each penalty insertion supplies mu^2.
All terms again have total power mu^0 or better. Their restricted
versions carry exponential factors along the chain of source blocks.
Second derivatives of one primitive factor have joint local support.
There are finitely many terms independent of K. Thus for n=1,2,

 ||delta_(j1)...delta_(jn) M_xy||
   <=C_n product ||a_j|| exp[-c tau(x,y,j1,...,jn)],      (5.3)

where tau is minimum tree length in the final-block graph. A fixed
enlargement of supports changes only C_n. The same estimate with a
smaller c holds for log M: differentiate its fixed resolvent contour;
insert (5.3) and the exponential coarse resolvents, and sum the
coarse intermediate indices. Their fiber dimension is dim(g), fixed.

In particular rho_y=N_0 tr_color(log M)_yy satisfies an absolutely
summable rooted second derivative bound with any fixed polynomial
weight in the source block distances, uniformly in K. This is a bound
on block Banach derivatives, not an unsupported raw fine-coordinate
Taylor sum. All the steps preserve compatible finite-volume periodic
distance estimates, with repeated local supports counted normally.

6. Uniform bound on the ACTUAL coarse Maxwell correction

By covariance, rho_y is individually gauge invariant. The linear color
jet vanishes for the semisimple algebra. At each fixed K its normalized
finite sum is N_0 logdet M; along the chosen structural system the
local kernels pass to the limit. The invariant-density Ward argument
of cgd identifies its actual Maxwell coefficient with twice its local
affine Taylor coefficient, after the actual stationary substitution.

Notebook33 identifies the first-two actual stationary polynomial jets
with the commuting affine representative. In the present physical
fine variables the probe is
  A_mu(x)=t (x_nu/K) H,   mu_direction != nu_direction,
so the fine link angle is t x_nu H/K^2. (Here mu_direction denotes an
axis; the scalar mu=1/K used above is unrelated.) This identification
is up to a fine gauge, harmless for each rho_y. In a source block j,
the norm of the coefficient of t is at most C(1+|j|). Applying (5.3)
for log M and summing its exponentially weighted second block kernels
therefore proves

            | beta(N_0 logdet M) | <= C,               (6.1)

uniformly in K, old depth and the chosen structural systems. The
affine substitution uses Taylor kernels only; it does not evaluate
the analytic map at a nonzero unbounded field. No uniform-in-K source
estimate for the full stationary branch is needed for this particular
coefficient, because the first-two affine jets were already identified.

Combining (2.2), the exact finite determinant identity, fixed-K local
passage and invariant-density Maxwell extraction gives

 beta(N_0 logdet Ghat)=beta(N_0 logdet T)+O(1),          (6.2)
 beta(D_actual)=beta(N_0 logdet T)
                      -(1/2)beta(N_0 logdet Q_B)+O(1).  (6.3)

This uses equality of total determinants and their local Hessians; it
does not assert equality of the different diagonal densities pointwise.

7. Remaining work
