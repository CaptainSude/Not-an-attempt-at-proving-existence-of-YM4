Combined with actual_base_ward_heat_comparison_research.txt,

uniformly in old classical depth M, under precisely that note's

 int_1^(K^2) (h_M,v(s)-2h_g(s)) ds/s

No additional charge-multiplicity factor is present.

1. Fixed operators, coordinates, and the local continuum expansion

Use Hermitian Lie algebra variables, the source's normalized invariant
trace tau, and the unit-lattice Wilson action
 S_W(U)=sum_(x,mu<nu) [1-(1/2)tau(P_(x,mu,nu)+P_(x,mu,nu)^(-1))].
There is one unoriented plaquette in this sum. On real fields it is
the usual 1-Re tau P action. The Hessian is in the left-relative chart
 U'=exp(i B)U. The vector operator is
 B_W(a)=S_W''(exp(i a))+D_a D_a^T.                        (1.1)
Here D_a phi(x,mu)=Ad_(exp(i a_mu(x))) phi(x+e_mu)-phi(x);
transpose is the fixed real/bilinear counting transpose, extended
analytically. The scalar operator is L_W(a)=D_a^T D_a.

We prove the leading vertices without guessing an off-shell action.
Introduce a physical mesh epsilon and physical link field A, so
a=epsilon A. For one plaquette use the exact physical jet
 z=(a,b,c,e)
  =(A_mu(x),A_nu(x),nabla_(epsilon,mu) A_nu(x),
                           nabla_(epsilon,nu) A_mu(x)).
The plaquette is exactly
 P_epsilon(z)
 =exp(i epsilon a)exp(i epsilon b+i epsilon^2 c)
            exp(-i epsilon a-i epsilon^2 e)exp(-i epsilon b).
Set
 F_epsilon=(P_epsilon-I)/epsilon^2,
 L_epsilon=-(1/2)tau(F_epsilon^2 P_epsilon^(-1)).          (1.2)
The exact identity P+P^-1-2I=(P-I)^2 P^-1 gives
 S_epsilon(A)=epsilon^4 sum_(x,mu<nu)
                         L_epsilon(J_epsilon A)        (1.3)
in dimension four.

P_0=I and its first epsilon derivative vanishes. Thus F_epsilon
extends jointly holomorphically across epsilon=0. Direct expansion gives
 F_0=i(c-e)-[a,b]=i{c-e+i[a,b]},
 L_0=(1/2)tau({c-e+i[a,b]}^2).                           (1.4)
On a fixed bounded complex jet polydisc, L_epsilon-L_0 and every
fixed field derivative are O(epsilon), uniformly as epsilon tends
to zero. This follows from joint holomorphy and Cauchy's formula,
not from an uncontrolled remainder of a noncommuting logarithm.

For plane waves at bounded complex physical momenta p, the difference
symbol (exp(i epsilon p_mu)-1)/epsilon is jointly analytic and
equals i p_mu+O(epsilon). Consequently the fixed field-order vertices
of (1.3), after physical normalization, converge with error O(epsilon)
to those of
 S_c(A)=(1/2)int sum_(mu<nu) tau(F_mu nu(A)^2) dx,
 F_mu nu=partial_mu A_nu-partial_nu A_mu+i[A_mu,A_nu].    (1.5)
The convergence holds jointly with any fixed number of momentum
derivatives on a bounded physical-momentum polydisc.

For clarity about all powers: the physical Hessian in the physical
pairing epsilon^4 sum is epsilon^-2 times the unit-lattice Hessian
at a=epsilon A. The nth ordinary action vertex therefore obeys
 Gamma_n^W(epsilon p_1,...,epsilon p_n)
   =epsilon^(4-n) Gamma_n^c(p_1,...,p_n)
                     +O(epsilon^(5-n)), n=2,3,4.        (1.6)
In midpoint coordinates the n=2 bare vertex has the stronger
O(epsilon^4) correction. Source-site phases can instead produce
O(epsilon^3) bare cross entries. The FULL flat Feynman base is exactly
delta(p)I in either convention and has the O(epsilon^4) correction
used in Section2.
The identity follows either by differentiating (1.3) or by comparing
coefficients in its joint convergent power series; momentum
conservation leaves n-1 independent momenta.

The physical left-relative chart is
 Xi_(epsilon,A)(B)=log(exp(i epsilon B)exp(i epsilon A))/(i epsilon)
                 =A+B+O(epsilon).
Its first B derivative is I+O(epsilon), and its second B derivative
is O(epsilon). Thus passing to the relative Hessian does not change
the limiting continuum operator. More explicitly, its exact formula
is
 S''(A)[J_A B,J_A C]+S'(A)[T_A(B,C)].                     (1.7)
The second summand is retained. In unit-lattice engineering degree,
the first relative correction to a cubic Hessian vertex contains
Gamma_2 times a local field factor and has momentum degree at least
two; corrections to the quartic Hessian vertex have momentum degree
at least one, or two for the Gamma_2/current terms. Thus they are
within the remainders asserted below.

Finally,
 epsilon^-1 D_(epsilon A)
 =epsilon^-1{Ad_(exp(i epsilon A_mu)) shift_(epsilon e_mu)-I}
 -> D_(c,A),mu=partial_mu+i ad_(A_mu)                    (1.8)
jointly on the same plane-wave jets. Its product with its transpose
has the same convergence and engineering scaling. Hence the continuum
limit of (1.1) is the ordinary continuum Yang--Mills Hessian plus
D_(c,A)D_(c,A)^T.

With the sign convention (1.8), integration by parts in (1.5) gives
 B_c(A)_mu nu=-D_(c,A)^2 delta_mu nu-2i ad_(F_mu nu).
An opposite convention for the adjoint representation reverses both
covariant signs consistently and leaves the heat traces below unchanged.
The scalar limit is L_c(A)=-D_(c,A)^2. The action normalization in
(1.5) gives coefficient one in their flat Laplacians.

2. Precise low-momentum vertex bounds

Use one fixed source-site Fourier convention; passing from midpoint
variables only inserts analytic phases equal to one at zero. Put
 rho=|k|, and take k in a fixed small punctured ball. The flat
symbols of either scalar or vector are
 B_W(0,k)=delta(k)I,  B_c(0,k)=|k|^2 I,
 delta(k)=4 sum_mu sin^2(k_mu/2).                         (2.1)
For momentum derivatives of order r<=2,
 |partial^r(delta-|k|^2)|<=C rho^(4-r).                  (2.2)

Define V_a(k,p) as the first background vertex mapping quantum
momentum k to k+p. Define W_ab(k;p,-p) as the mixed second background
vertex preserving k. Use the same convention on the continuum.
The homogeneous leading polynomial in (1.6)--(1.8) gives
 delta V=V_W-V_c=O((|k|+|p|)^2),
 delta W=W_W-W_c=O((|k|+|p|)^1),                         (2.3)
jointly as all momenta tend to zero. V_c is homogeneous of degree one,
and W_c is constant in all momenta. The scalar vertices satisfy the
same assertions, directly from (1.8). No Ward identity at a finite
momentum grid is used here.

The joint analytic Taylor expansion implies, at p=0 and r<=2,
 ||partial_p^r delta V_a(k,p)||+
 ||partial_p^r delta V_b(k+p,-p)|| <= C rho^(2-r),        (2.4)
 ||partial_p^r delta W_ab(k;p,-p)|| <= C rho^(1-r).       (2.5)
For r=2 in (2.5) this is only a loose upper bound on a bounded
analytic derivative: bounded <=C/rho for rho<1. In particular it
does not assert a genuine singularity. The shifted vertex in (2.4)
includes derivatives in both its internal and external arguments.

For either lattice or continuum baseline one may use
 ||partial_p^r V||<=C rho^(1-r),
 ||partial_p^r W||<=C rho^(-r), r<=2.                    (2.6)
The negative exponents again mean harmless overestimates. On the
lattice all required vertices and derivatives are bounded away from
the low-momentum ball. Continuum vertices are polynomials of the
explicit degrees just stated.

3. Exact comparison of the heat two-point diagrams

For either operator take the translation-invariant second kernel of
the total heat variation against compact background sources. The
exact identity is
 D^2 Tr e^-sB[a,b]
 =-s Tr(e^-sB B''[a,b])
  +s^2 int_0^1 Tr(e^-s(1-v)B B'[a] e^-svB B'[b]) dv.     (3.1)
It may be defined by these trace-class variations; an extensive
vacuum trace is not needed. In the continuum the insertions are
first-order differential operators, but for fixed s>0 the compact
source variations in (3.1) are justified by Gaussian smoothing.
Alternatively the right hand side directly defines the second
kernel. At p=0 and after at most two p derivatives its Fourier
integrals are absolutely convergent uniformly in v, as the
explicit estimates below show.

Let H_ab(s,p) denote that external Fourier kernel. Then it equals
 -s int tr[e^-sB(k) W_ab(k;p,-p)] dk/(2pi)^4
 +s^2 int_0^1 int tr[
 e^-s(1-v)B(k+p) V_a(k,p)
 e^-svB(k) V_b(k+p,-p)] dk/(2pi)^4 dv.                   (3.2)
For the lattice the domain is [-pi,pi]^4; for the continuum it
is R^4. Equation (3.2) uses cyclicity only for the total trace.
It does not move operators through a trace on a finite block.

We compare (3.2) after two external derivatives, first on a common
small momentum ball. The elementary heat bounds are
 ||partial_k^r E(t,k)||
   <=C rho^-r(1+t rho^2)^r e^-c t rho^2,
 ||partial_k^r(E_W-E_c)(t,k)||
   <=C t rho^(4-r)(1+t rho^2)^r e^-c t rho^2, r<=2.      (3.3)
They follow by direct differentiation or Duhamel and (2.2).
They hold for all t>=0, with no inverse power of t. Therefore
neither v=0 nor v=1 introduces a time-simplex singularity.

In the tadpole term a second W derivative difference is bounded
by C rho^-1, while a heat difference times a bounded second W
derivative costs C s rho^4. After the outer s factor, their
four-dimensional integrals are respectively
 C s int rho^-1 e^-csrho^2 dk =O(s^-1/2),
 C s^2 int rho^4 e^-csrho^2 dk=O(s^-2).                  (3.4)
The first bound is deliberately conservative; W_c has no momentum
derivatives and the actual lattice second derivative is bounded.

In the bubble telescope its two heat and two vertex factors.
A replaced vertex supplies degree two, the other supplies degree
one. Two external derivatives reduce the resulting degree by at
most two, whether they hit vertices or exponentials. The
differentiated product is bounded by
 C rho P(s rho^2)e^-csrho^2.                             (3.5)
Here P is a fixed polynomial and the two heat times sum to s.
After the prefactor s^2, the integral of (3.5) is O(s^-1/2).

A replaced heat factor gives its time <=s times rho^4, while
the two ordinary vertices supply rho^2. After two derivatives
the bound is
 C s rho^4 P(s rho^2)e^-csrho^2.                         (3.6)
After s^2 and integration it is O(s^-1). These bounds are
uniform on the entire time simplex.

The lattice complementary region has a fixed positive flat spectral
gap, so all its differentiated integrals are bounded by a fixed
polynomial in s times e^-cs. The continuum complementary region
has the same property, using its polynomial vertices and the
Gaussian tail on R^4. No ultraviolet continuum integral is compared
without its positive heat time.

Thus, for s>=1,
 |partial_p^2(H_W-H_c)(s,0)|<=C s^-1/2.                 (3.7)
The proof is equivalent to rescaling k=xi/sqrt(s) in the low ball,
but (3.3)--(3.6) give a common integrable majorant and control the
time-simplex endpoints explicitly. Analytic lattice phases and
left-chart corrections need not have any extra parity: the
conservative half-power rate already suffices.

4. From total kernels to rooted affine coefficients

For either LATTICE scalar or vector heat operator the diagonal
color-trace density is gauge invariant. Its fixed-s Taylor kernels
have the moments needed for the density Ward/reanchoring identity.
The linear color jet is zero for the semisimple gauge group. For
a_alpha(t,x)=t x_nu H, alpha!=nu, that identity gives
 h(s)=[t^2] tr(e^-sB(a(t)))_(0,0)
     =(1/4) partial_(p_nu)^2 H_(alpha H,alpha H)(s,0).     (4.1)
Continuum local source kernels may contain contact distributions.
We do not assert their absolute spatial-moment regularity or use a
continuum reanchoring theorem. Instead Section5 directly computes
the transverse continuum diagram derivative and verifies that its
one-quarter is exactly the explicit magnetic heat coefficient.
In particular there is no polarization factor hidden in (4.1).

Combining (3.7),(4.1) reduces the fixed lattice coefficient to the
explicit continuum coefficient, with error C s^-1/2.

5. Explicit continuum value and charge normalization

For one charge q (an eigenvalue of ad_H on the complexified adjoint
space), choose a single curvature F_12=fH. The scalar diagonal heat
density is exactly
 k_q(s;f)=(4 pi s)^-2 (qfs)/sinh(qfs).                   (5.1)
Fourier transformation in x_2 turns the magnetic plane into harmonic
oscillators of eigenvalues |qf|(2n+1). The integral over their centers
contributes |qf|/(2pi); summing n and multiplying by the two free
directions proves (5.1) pointwise. No finite-volume degeneracy is used.

For the vector the spin matrix has eigenvalues 0,0,+2qf,-2qf,
so its diagonal trace is
 k_q(s;f){2+2cosh(2qfs)}.                               (5.2)
At each fixed s>0 these formulas are twice differentiable at f=0.
We use their quadratic coefficient, not a claim of convergence of
an infinite-proper-time massless determinant at nonzero field.

Since x/sinh x=1-x^2/6+O(x^4) and
2+2cosh(2x)=4+4x^2+O(x^4), (5.1)--(5.2) give
 [f^2]k_q=-q^2/(96 pi^2),
 [f^2](vector density)=10q^2/(48 pi^2).                 (5.3)
Sum over all adjoint charges, both signs included. Complexification
does not double the trace of the original real color space. Therefore
 C_H=sum_q q^2, alpha_g=C_H/(48 pi^2),
 h_c,g=-alpha_g/2,       h_c,v=10alpha_g.                 (5.4)
This proves (0.1).

Here is the promised direct diagram verification. Take an external
a_mu with p=p_nu e_nu, mu!=nu. In one charge sector the scalar
first vertex is 2q k_mu and the second is the constant 2q^2.
Only the bubble contributes to the external second derivative.
Completing the square in its Gaussian integral gives
 H_scalar,bubble(s,p)
   =2q^2 s(4pi s)^-2 int_0^1 exp[-s v(1-v)p^2] dv.
Thus H_scalar''(s,0)=-q^2/(24pi^2), whose one-quarter is
the scalar value in (5.3). For vectors the orbital piece is four
copies. The two spin vertices have trace product 8q^2 p^2, so
their second-derivative contribution is q^2/pi^2. Mixed spin/orbital
terms have zero Lorentz trace. The one-quarter vector value is
therefore -q^2/(24pi^2)+q^2/(4pi^2)=10q^2/(48pi^2), exactly (5.3).
This directly identifies the continuum quantity in (3.7), with no
claim about absolute moments of continuum contact distributions.

For tau=Tr_fund/N on SU(N), tau(H^2)=1 gives
C_H=2N^2. No further factor two is inserted.

6. Combined consequence and the remaining existence boundary

The preceding actual-base comparison gives
 h_M,v-h_W,v=O(1/s) uniformly M. The scalar is independent of M.
Consequently (0.2) holds uniformly in M, and
 int_1^(K^2) (h_M,v-2h_g) ds/s
 =11alpha_g log(K^2)+O(int_1^infty s^-3/2 ds)
 =22alpha_g log K+O(1).                                 (6.1)

The determinant sign is fixed: for logdet G-(1/2)logdet V, the
proper-time quadratic density is (1/2)h_v-h_g; extracting
beta=2[t^2] gives exactly h_v-2h_g. Thus (6.1), and not its
negative or twice its value, is the signed Maxwell coefficient
from the middle base band.

Combining this with the separately proved actual soft-factor
determinant identities, bounded coarse corrections, short-time

is an additional assembly step. This note does not silently
identify any separately regularized singular factors. It proves
the fixed-reference ingredient and its direct uniform-M base
consequence. It does not establish a full large-field RG history,
a running trajectory outside its stated weak domain, construction
